One DSD People, Access and Culture

HCBS equity policy analysis companion

These guides support independent use by DHS staff, with optional help from the Equity and Inclusion Operations Consultant. The consultant leads One DSD People, Access and Culture. Tool drafts are saved in the current browser on this device and can be downloaded or imported; they are not sent to the program server.

Companion for policy and program staff: service lines, federal floor, lead agencies, providers

Disability Services Division · One DSD People, Access and Culture

1. What is different in this version

The HCBS version keeps the ten-step method of the DSD Equity Policy Analysis Tool and adds what DSD's home and community-based services work needs. It is written for staff who draft waiver amendments and renewals, state plan amendments, bulletins and CBSM pages, rate and DWRS changes, 245D standards, MnCHOICES changes, lead agency guidance, self-direction policy, system changes and Olmstead initiatives. Read this companion with the main facilitator guide; it does not repeat the session agendas, the capacity plan or the paper worksheet.

Four additions respond to what gets missed in DSD equity reviews today.

Blind spotWhere the tool now forces the question
Federal or CMS constraint treated as covering the whole proposalStep 1 asks for the federal floor with a citation, and separately for the state design choices above it. Step 5 asks, for each burden, whether the mechanism is the floor or a choice. Step 8 asks for alternatives above the floor.
County and Tribal lead agency variationNew Step 6 asks how the smallest county and a Tribal lead agency will implement, lists the discretion points and how they will be guided, and asks whether support and funding are equal. Tribal lead agencies have their own grid row and their own routing path.
Provider capacity in Greater Minnesota and culturally specific providersStep 6 asks for capacity by region and provider type, the risk of exit, and who loses access when a provider leaves. Small and culturally specific providers have a grid row.
Communication access for people with IDD, Deaf, DeafBlind, blind and LEP peopleStep 7 walks their paths one at a time, requires a named standard, provision, budget and owner for each, and adds the HCBS settings rule and person-centered planning. Each population has its own grid row.

The affected communities grid now opens with 24 rows: disability populations by type and program, the communities the policy names, people missing from the data (waiting lists, facilities, homelessness), family caregivers and guardians, self-directed participants and workers, DSPs, small and culturally specific providers, and county and Tribal lead agency staff. Remove rows that do not apply; add intersections. The quality check adds five items: service lines identified, federal floor cited and state choices listed, lead agency variation and provider capacity analyzed, workforce effects described.

4. Lead agency variation checklist

Complete this with Step 6. The aim is to design for the least-resourced lead agency rather than the average, and to name discretion before it produces different outcomes for similar people.

Authority: does a Tribal lead agency have the same authority as a county under this instrument? Where it differs, is that stated?

Funding: what does implementation cost a lead agency, and is it funded equally, including Tribal lead agencies?

Caseload and staffing: how many case managers and assessors does the smallest affected lead agency have, and what does this add to their work?

Systems: does the lead agency have the MMIS, MnCHOICES or portal access the change assumes?

Distance and provider supply: can the lead agency actually find a provider for the service the instrument assumes?

Discretion: list every place the instrument says "may," "as appropriate," or "lead agency determines." For each, what guidance and examples will keep readings consistent?

Timeline: do lead agencies receive the bulletin, training and templates before the effective date, with enough time to reach people?

Feedback: how will a lead agency report that the change is not working, and who answers?

5. Communication access checklist

Complete this with Step 7. For each population, name the standard, the provision, the budget and the owner.

People with intellectual and developmental disabilities: plain-language versions tested with self-advocates; pictures and examples; a person to call; time to decide.

Deaf and hard-of-hearing people: ASL interpretation for assessments, hearings and listening sessions; captioned video; video relay; text alternatives to phone-only steps.

DeafBlind people: tactile and close-vision interpreting; braille; support service providers; extended time.

Blind and low-vision people: accessible PDFs and portals (WCAG 2.1 AA, Section 508), braille and large print, screen-reader-tested forms, no image-only notices.

People with limited English proficiency: translation in the languages identified in Step 2, dialect-checked; interpreter availability in rural areas confirmed with the contract; translated versions published at the same time as English.

People without reliable broadband or devices: a non-digital route that is equally timely and staffed; mail and in-person options; phone support in languages used.

Settings and planning: the change's effect on choice of setting, community integration, privacy and autonomy, and whether the person-centered plan still drives the service.

6. Running it inside the HCBS calendar

Waiver amendments and renewals: run Steps 0 through 5 before the policy option is selected, Steps 6 through 8 before public and Tribal notice is drafted, and Steps 9 and 10 before the CMS package is assembled. Attach the record to the package narrative. Rate and DWRS changes: run the analysis before the number is final and model Steps 5 and 6 by region and provider type. Bulletins and CBSM pages: run a lighter pass but do not skip Steps 6 and 7; guidance is where variation starts. Bills: run Steps 0 through 5 as soon as referred and update each stage; use the bill-analysis equity section in Step 10.

7. Maintaining the HCBS version

The content block at the top of the HTML file holds the instrument list, service lines, default groups, coach text, routing paths and resources. Update it when a waiver is renewed, when Waiver Reimagine phases change, when CMS guidance changes, and when the DHS Equity Analysis Toolkit or Equity Outcome Map is revised. The Equity and Inclusion Operations Consultant owns both versions; the DSD policy leads for each service line review the profiles in Section 2 annually.
