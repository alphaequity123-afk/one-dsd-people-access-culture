DSD equity implementation work plan

The Equity and Inclusion Operations Consultant leads One DSD People, Access and Culture. Operationalizing equity across the Disability Services Division, aligned to the six ADSA Equity and Inclusion Implementation Plan goals

One DSD People, Access and Culture

Purpose and how to read this plan

This plan turns the six ADSA Equity and Inclusion Implementation Plan goals into dated, owned work for the Disability Services Division (DSD) over 18 months, an agreed eighteen-month period. It is built on five source documents and on the One DSD People, Access and Culture program, which already supplies the toolkit, the DSD Equity Needs Assessment, and One DSD Consult.

Two facts shape every choice here. First, DHS is in an assessment phase: the Office of Strategy and Performance, the Office of Innovation and Impact, and the Office of Employee Culture finished interviews and analysis for the 2025–2026 Equity Outcome Map and are now in joint development, so enterprise metrics and roles may still move. Second, DSD staff have already told us, through 71 interviews and the needs assessment, what they need. The plan therefore starts with what DSD controls, aligns to the Outcome Map metrics where they exist, and leaves clearly marked seams where enterprise decisions will plug in.

Read it top down. Sections 2 and 3 are the honest starting point. Section 4 is the operating model. Section 5 is the timeline. Sections 6 through 11 are the six goal workstreams, each with actions, owners, dates, and measures. Sections 12 through 14 cover measurement, risk, and the decisions leadership needs to make now.

What the source materials tell us

The five inputs agree on the destination and differ on altitude. The enterprise materials define outcomes and metrics; the ADSA materials define six goals; the DSD materials are tools waiting for a structure to run them. This plan supplies that structure.

Three themes run through all five. Equity is treated as an add-on when roles are unclear and accountability is thin; the Outcome Map names both as barriers, and the Equity Team draft anticipates the same concern as tokenism. Learning only changes practice when it is tied to a decision, which is why the Equity Analysis Toolkit appears in Goals 1, 2, and 5 and in the enterprise metrics. And nothing survives leadership change unless it is written into structures, cadences, and job expectations, which the Continuum calls the shift from identity change to structural change.

Where DSD sits today

DSD has more equity assets than most divisions and less structure to run them. Measured against the Crossroads Continuum, the division has moved past symbolic change and into identity change: it has a named equity role, a needs assessment built from 71 staff interviews, a working toolkit, a consult pathway, and leadership that commissioned all of it. What it does not yet have is the structural layer, the standing team, the decision points where analysis is required, the reporting rhythm, and the job expectations that make equity routine rather than relational.

This is a working judgment, not a score. The Continuum is a conversation tool, and the DSD Equity Team should revisit this placement with staff in its first quarter.

Decision points where an equity analysis is required. Starting in Phase 1, four decision types are proposed for review before proceeding with a completed toolkit analysis attached: new or materially revised DSD policy or procedure; new or renewed contracts and grants above the threshold the program review group sets; hiring plans for supervisor and above; and any public-facing communication or service change affecting access. This is the single most important structural change in the plan. It is what moves equity from optional to routine.

Reporting backbone. The two draft forms become one instrument with two layers. Managers submit the Equity Review Intake Form for each initiative as it starts and as it closes. The consultant compiles those submissions, the Consult log, and the toolkit analysis records into the DSD DEIA Status Report, which the program review group reviews and sends to the ADSA Equity Director quarterly. Nothing is reported twice, and every reported item traces to a record.

Time commitment. Core Equity Team members: a proposed four hours a month, subject to agreement with the responsible supervisor. Goal leads: proposed time to be agreed. program review group: one hour a month plus quarterly report review. The consultant leads this program; capacity needs should be reviewed; Section 14 asks leadership to confirm that.

Phased timeline

The plan runs in four phases over 18 months. Phase 0 secures decisions, names people, and listens to staff. Phase 1 builds the structures and trains before anything is required. Phase 2 runs the full cadence and reports real numbers. Phase 3 reassesses against the Continuum and realigns to whatever joint DHS development has settled.

implementation timeline · 4 phases, 5 milestones, proposed timing to confirm to proposed timing to confirm

The dashed line marks the one date that changes practice: from proposed timing to confirm, the four decision types in Section 4 are proposed for review before proceeding with a toolkit analysis. Everything before it exists so that date works; everything after it reports on whether it did.

Phase gates. Phase 1 does not start until the six leadership decisions in Section 14 are recorded. Phase 2 does not start until the Equity Team charter is signed and every unit has completed a practice lab. Phase 3 starts on schedule regardless, because its purpose is to look honestly at what did and did not happen.

Proposed reporting routine. Managers provide updates as initiatives begin and close. The consultant leads preparation of the quarterly report from those updates, consultation totals and completed analysis records. The relevant contributors review accuracy before an appropriate staff summary is shared. Agree the reporting dates and any required external reporting with the responsible people before implementation.

What is not measured. The plan does not score the division on the Continuum, does not report individual IDI results, and does not report stay-interview or survey content at a level that could identify a person. These limits are stated to staff when each instrument launches.

Risks and dependencies on the DHS assessment phase

The largest risk is not any single action slipping; it is building DSD structures that the enterprise later redefines. The plan manages that by keeping DSD's measures mapped to the Outcome Map, by routing data requests through the ADSA Equity Director, and by scheduling a formal realignment the quarter after DHS publishes joint development results.
