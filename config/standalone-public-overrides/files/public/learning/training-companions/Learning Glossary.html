Learning Glossary

Use these explanations to support discussion across One DHS People, Access and Culture. They are learning definitions, not substitutes for legal definitions or agency policy.

Race

A social classification shaped by history and social practices, often associated with perceived physical differences. Racial categories can affect treatment and opportunity; they do not establish a person's ability, character, or culture.

Ethnicity

A connection with shared cultural heritage, ancestry, language, traditions, or history. A person may describe several connections. Race and ethnicity are related in some settings but are not interchangeable.

Culture

Learned and changing ways people make meaning and organize life. Family, community, work, and other settings contribute. A group description does not predict every member's beliefs or behavior.

Stereotype

A generalized belief about a group that is applied to a person without sufficient attention to their circumstances. A seemingly favorable stereotype can also create unfair expectations.

Prejudice

A judgment or attitude about people based on their perceived group membership rather than a fair consideration of the person or situation.

Discrimination

Unequal treatment or exclusion related to a characteristic such as race or disability. In this learning material, the term invites examination of conduct and effects. Formal findings depend on applicable requirements and the appropriate review process.

Learning glossary continued

Racism

Ideas, conduct, and institutional arrangements that create or sustain racial disadvantage and advantage. It can involve individual behavior and patterns across policies or institutions; examining one does not make the other unimportant.

Individual and systemic racial harm

Individual harm concerns treatment in a particular interaction. Systemic analysis examines how repeated arrangements produce or sustain racial inequality. A concern about unfair treatment deserves consideration; a slogan about reverse racism should not replace examination of the facts or the appropriate complaint process.

White privilege

Advantages associated with being perceived as white in a society shaped by racial inequality. Recognizing those advantages does not mean every white person's life is easy or that effort and other barriers do not matter.

Unconscious and implicit bias

Overlapping terms for associations or judgments that may operate outside full awareness. Implicit measures study associations indirectly. Neither an informal exercise nor a course response diagnoses a person's beliefs.

Affinity bias

Favoring what or whom feels familiar or similar to oneself, without enough attention to the criteria that matter for the decision.

Confirmation bias

Giving more attention or weight to information that supports an existing belief while overlooking information that could challenge it.

Learning glossary continued

Halo effect

Allowing one favorable impression to influence a judgment about other qualities without checking the relevant evidence.

Perception bias

An interpretation influenced by prior expectations or stereotypes. Separating observation from interpretation can help a person examine that influence; it does not guarantee perfect objectivity.

Intercultural competence

The ability to understand different cultural perspectives and respond appropriately in context. Learning involves self-reflection, inquiry, practice, and feedback rather than memorizing rules about groups.

Cultural humility

An ongoing willingness to examine what one does not know, attend to power and context, and learn from people without expecting them to represent an entire group.

Equity

Examining barriers and differing circumstances so people have a fair opportunity to participate and benefit. It requires attention to how decisions work and whom they affect.

Accessibility

Designing information, environments, and participation so people with disabilities can use them. A general offer of help does not replace the relevant accessibility or accommodation arrangements.
