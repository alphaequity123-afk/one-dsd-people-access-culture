Three People, One Meeting

Kelly: Here's a common call. A navigator rings a case manager before a planning meeting and says, send me everything in the file so I can be ready. What do you do?

John: My instinct is to send it. The family asked for the navigator, so they must be fine with it.

Kelly: Asking for a navigator isn't the same as agreeing to share everything. In Minnesota, releasing someone's private data to another person takes their informed consent, which is written permission. So check the release first, and then share what the meeting needs, not the whole file.

John: What does the meeting usually need?

Kelly: What the meeting is for, what decisions might come up, any deadlines, who's interpreting and what the person wants help with. And five minutes agreeing on roles. Who explains, who interprets, who supports the person and who decides.

John: Then the meeting starts, and I ask the person about their day, and the navigator answers for them. It's helpful. It's also not the person.

Kelly: Thank the navigator and turn back to the person. Something like, that helps me understand, I'd like to hear from you too. A good navigator welcomes that. Their job is to help the person speak, not to speak for them.

John: And at the end?

Kelly: Ask the person to tell you in their own words what happens next. If it's not what you expected, the gap is in your explanation, not in them.

John: What about when the navigator pushes back? Says the provider list leaves out options that fit the family.

Kelly: That's often a sign the role is working. Brokers and navigators advocate. Ask what they're seeing, separate what the rules require from what's just habit and check whether there are options you didn't know about. Talk privately first, unless the choice is the family's. Then the family belongs in the conversation.

John: And afterward I send the family a plain-language note of what happens next.

Kelly: And with their agreement, keep the navigator informed. Families often call the navigator first. If the navigator knows the request was approved, they can share good news instead of guessing.

John: Consent first, clear roles, the person at the center, and close the loop. I can do that.
