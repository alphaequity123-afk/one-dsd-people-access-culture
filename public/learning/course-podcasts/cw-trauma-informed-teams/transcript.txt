A Lobby Like a Police Station

John: A county partner tells a program manager that families describe the lobby as like a police station. The security check is required, and several families have left before their interviews.

Kelly: So the check stays. The question is everything around it. SAMHSA's trauma-informed approach has six principles: safety, trustworthiness and transparency, peer support, collaboration, empowerment and choice, and attention to cultural, historical and gender issues.

John: Run the lobby through those, and you get practical ideas. A sign that explains why the check exists. A greeting before the screening, not after. A place to wait that doesn't feel like a holding area.

Kelly: None of that weakens security. It just stops security from being the first and only message.

Kelly: Here's the appointment letter a case manager is asked to revise. You must appear at the office on the date below. Failure to appear will result in case closure.

John: Accurate. And frightening, if you've had bad experiences with systems.

Kelly: Keep the facts and change the stance. Lead with what the appointment is for, offer a way to reschedule and say who to call. The consequence can still be there, stated plainly, near the end.

John: Now the staff side. A team handled a child fatality review this week. Several people were closely involved. The program manager wants to respond.

Kelly: Secondary traumatic stress is real. The National Child Traumatic Stress Network describes it as the emotional duress that results when a person hears about the firsthand trauma experiences of another.

John: So a cheerful team lunch isn't the answer.

Kelly: Not on its own. Acknowledge what happened, offer some flexibility with the next few days, and remind people about the Employee Assistance Program, which is confidential. Then check in again next week, not just once.

John: Last one is mine to lead. A leadership meeting will cover a staff assault from last month. Some people in the room were there when it happened.

Kelly: Tell people beforehand what will be discussed, so nobody is surprised. Say at the start that stepping out is fine. Keep the focus on what the division is changing, not on reliving the details.

John: Choice and transparency again.

Kelly: Exactly. The six principles work for staff as much as for families. That's the heart of this course.
