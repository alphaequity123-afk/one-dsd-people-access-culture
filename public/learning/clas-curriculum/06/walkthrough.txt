Host 1: Also ask whose experience is missing. People who readily respond to an online survey may not represent those who had difficulty accessing the original information. A single community member cannot be assumed to speak for every person with a disability, every language community or every family.

Host 2: Consider the format, language, communication support, timing, location or technology, and the effort required. An invitation that is inaccessible can reproduce the very problem the project hopes to address. Offer appropriate ways to participate through the approved engagement process.

Host 1: Respect a person's choice not to participate. Avoid making access to services appear dependent on providing feedback. Explain what information is requested, how it will be used and any limits on confidentiality. Follow applicable privacy and records requirements. If compensation or reimbursement is available, use the established rules; do not promise a payment you cannot authorize.

Host 2: Participation should make room for the person themselves, including self-advocates and their chosen supports. Do not automatically substitute the views of professionals or family members for the person's perspective. At the same time, different perspectives can reveal different parts of an experience when their roles are clear.

Host 1: Listen for assets as well as problems.

Host 1: Community assessment includes strengths. Ask what already works, which communication approaches are trusted, and what people would want preserved. A redesign can unintentionally remove a feature that some people depend on.

Host 2: In our example, a clear telephone option may be valuable even if a new online response form is introduced. Improving one route does not necessarily justify removing another. Look for choices that support participation rather than assuming one channel will work for everyone.

Host 1: When feedback differs, describe the difference rather than forcing agreement. One person may prefer a brief notice, while another needs more explanation. A concise main message with accessible supporting detail might address both needs, but the proposed solution still needs checking.

Host 1: The assessment in standard twelve is recurring organizational work. It revisits community strengths and needs and uses that information in service planning and implementation, rather than treating one consultation as the complete picture.
