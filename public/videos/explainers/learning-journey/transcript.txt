Your learning journey and the four levels

A place to notice growth
Equity practice grows the way most professional skill does, unevenly and through real work, so it helps to pause now and then and notice where you are. Near the top of My work you will find a panel called Your learning journey. The site describes it as a place to notice how far you have come and to choose where to go next. It is private to you, nothing in it is graded, and you remain welcome to explore anything on the site that catches your interest.

The four levels
The panel begins with one question: Where are you right now? Four levels describe common stages in equity practice. Getting oriented fits when you are new to the Disability Services Division, or new to equity work. It helps you see what the program offers and where authority for decisions sits. Foundations is about building a shared language with colleagues, so you can talk about equity, access and bias without labeling people. Applying means bringing equity into real decisions, like an analysis, a hiring process or a service change. Leading and sustaining is for shaping systems and helping others grow.

Choosing where you are
The advice is to pick the one that feels closest. You can change it whenever you like, and the site notes that experienced staff often start at the third or fourth level. The panel is plain that these levels are not a ladder you are required to climb. Once you choose, a short list called A few good next steps appears, with links suited to that level. Someone getting oriented might start with a ten-minute Equity Pause, while someone at Applying will see courses and a guided practice for equity analysis.

Your I can statements
Below the next steps, What you can do now offers a few I can statements for your level. At Foundations, for example, you might check that you can use plain language about equity, access and bias, recognize a barrier without labeling people, and ask before assuming. Each level also includes one milestone marked Try it in your work. Check these off when they feel true for you. In the panel's own words, your own judgment is the only measure.

Bring it to a conversation
Sometimes it helps to talk about your growth with a supervisor or mentor. For that, Download a summary creates a one-page Word document with your current level, the statements you have checked, and the practice plans you have worked on. It closes by saying you are sharing it by choice, as your own view of your growth, to start a conversation rather than to evaluate you. Moving to a new computer? Choose Save a copy, then use Open a saved copy on the new one to pick up where you left off.

Levels on resources
The same four level names appear across the site, so you can spot material that suits where you are. Many resources and courses show a level near the top. On this checklist for plain language and accessible documents, you will see Foundations, and selecting it takes you to the matching section of Learning paths. A level on a resource is a suggestion about where it tends to help most. It is guidance rather than a gate, and anyone can open anything.

Learning paths by level
Under Learn, the Paths page brings this together. Learning paths groups five guided pathways and nine reading paths under Foundations, Applying, and Leading and sustaining. Every path begins with a question from real work, such as whose ideas actually shape decisions in a meeting that is open to everyone. The page calls this grouping a guide rather than a gate, so follow whichever path speaks to your question today. If you are new to DSD, it suggests getting to know the program first.

Private and yours
It is worth being clear about what your learning journey is not. It is never an evaluation. It is private to you, and your choices are kept only in this browser on this computer. None of it is sent to your supervisor or to program staff. Because it stays on the computer, anyone using that same computer could see it, so keep that in mind on a shared device. As a next step, open My work, choose the level that feels closest today, and try one suggested step this week.
