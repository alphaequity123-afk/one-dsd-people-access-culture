How the program works

Start with your work
Equity work rarely begins with a framework. It begins with a notice that confuses people, a policy choice that is still open, or a team conversation you want to handle well. How the program works starts from that reality by asking what you are working on. You can choose Build your understanding to explore knowledge and examples, Work through a decision to examine who is affected and compare options, or Understand what helped to look beyond what was completed to what changed for people.

Familiar situations
If you would rather begin with an example, the next section offers situations that may feel familiar. In one, a disability service team wants to clarify a notice about a service change, knowing that a shorter letter will not help if people cannot tell what it means for their support. Each example shows what you could take back and how you would know more, by asking people to explain their next step rather than counting letters sent.

Five connected areas
At the center of the page are five connected areas, each built around a question. What informs our work brings together policy, evidence, staff knowledge and community perspectives. Put equity into practice asks what we do with what we know, and helps you choose an activity that fits a real question or decision. Tools and resources asks what we can use or produce, such as a course or a draft analysis. Explore them in any order and return as your work develops.

Progress and lasting change
The last two areas look past the activity itself. Review progress asks what changed in practice and how people experienced it, using observations and feedback rather than ratings of individual staff. Lasting change asks what should improve over time, such as access to opportunity, workplace participation, service experiences and trust. Those changes require evidence over time. The page adds a reminder: a prepared document does not establish approval or implementation, so identify the people affected and the office responsible for the decision.

How this program operates
How this program operates shows what holds the pieces together. The Program charter connects the purpose, operating principles, consultant responsibilities, authority boundaries and evidence needed to carry work forward. The requirements and review evidence workspace helps you identify applicable requirements, connect them with sources and prepare evidence for the responsible reviewer. Where a step from the Aging and Disability Services Administration plan applies, the six-goal implementation map links it to practical work. Those twenty-eight steps do not limit the program's broader equity work.

Operationalizing equity
Further down, the page explains operationalizing equity: moving it out of awareness training and slogans and into how the organization decides, spends, hires, serves and corrects itself. Equity lives inside real decisions, not beside them, which means policies, budgets, forms and services get an equity look while they can still change. It is everyone's work with named owners. Progress is measured at the program level, never by scoring or surveilling individuals, and no single tool becomes a personnel label.

Pillar one: engagement
The program rests on three pillars that work as a cycle. Engagement surfaces what staff and communities experience, analysis turns that into evidence about the system, and applied practice changes a specific decision with a specific owner. The first pillar treats staff as participants, not audiences. Engagement is voluntary, private and non-shaming, so you can learn on your own time and reflect without being recorded. Feedback loops close, which means staff hear what changed because of their input, and what could not change and why.

Seeing patterns, fixing systems
The second pillar is about seeing the pattern, not the person. You follow the data by community, language, disability and place, say plainly what the data does not show, and look at each decision point to ask who is delayed, denied or missing. Individual conduct belongs in a confidential Human Resources channel; a systemic pattern belongs in equity practice. The third pillar, applied practices, fixes the system where it lives. Every analysis ends with something usable, with a named owner and a review date, built into documents the organization already uses.

Six core commitments
The page closes with six commitments behind every decision. Equity is everyone's work. Build on existing infrastructure. Center lived experience. Follow the data. Move at the speed of trust. Work in partnership. Together, they describe a program that respects your expertise and the people you serve. When you are ready, return to the top of the page, choose the starting point that matches what you are working on today, and take one small, concrete step.
