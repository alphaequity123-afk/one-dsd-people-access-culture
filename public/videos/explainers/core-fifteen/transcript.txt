The Core 15: a first year of learning

A place to begin
When a collection holds seventy-two self-directed courses and hundreds of other resources, the hardest part can be knowing where to start. The Core 15 answers that question. It is a set of fifteen courses chosen for disability services work, gathered near the top of the Learn page so you can find them quickly. They are recommendations rather than required training, and you can take them in any order and at your own pace.

Why these fifteen
Each of these courses is built around situations people in the division actually meet, in services and in the workplace. Together they move in three steps: a shared foundation, equity applied to real decisions, and the systems view a leader needs. The groups also follow the course levels. Start here draws on Foundation courses, Apply it on Intermediate ones, and Lead it on Advanced ones, which is why the set makes a strong first year of learning.

Start here
The first six courses give you and your colleagues a common language. An Equity Lens for Ordinary Decisions offers six questions about purpose, participation, requirements, access, evidence and consequences. Other courses explore cultural humility, unconscious bias, and different ways of understanding disability and belonging. The last two look at accessibility in everyday services and work, and at language access, including the difference between translation, interpretation and checking that someone has understood. These are the habits every later course relies on.

Apply it
The next six take that language into real decisions. You choose communication for the person and the task, support informed choice in person-centered planning, and work a realistic decision through the Equity Analysis Toolkit. You also practice engagement that is honest about what people can actually shape, hiring that rests on relevant evidence rather than credentials that simply feel familiar, and responding to microaggressions with care and purpose. Each is built around real service and workplace situations, with room for reflection and practice.

Lead it
The final three widen the view to systems. From Individual Access Fixes to Accessible Systems asks you to map barriers across a whole journey, not a single document. Putting Three ADSA Equity Goals into Practice helps you explain the six goals of the Aging and Disability Services Administration and connect three of them to real conditions. Policy and Legislative Analysis through an Equity Lens works with real Minnesota policy and legislative records.

Threads that connect
Notice how the groups build on one another. Accessibility in everyday work becomes accessible communication for one person and one task, and then becomes the design of accessible systems. The equity lens for ordinary decisions grows into a full toolkit analysis, and then into policy and legislative work. Learning about unconscious bias prepares you for fairer hiring and for responding well in a hard moment. Each step asks a little more of the same skills.

Beyond the fifteen
The Core 15 are drawn from the One DSD Self-Directed Learning Series, so when a course sparks a new question, the full series of seventy-two is right below. You can choose Foundation, Intermediate or Advanced courses, and download editable slides to use with a team. Working through a course with colleagues can turn its questions into a useful conversation about your shared work. The slides you download are separate copies on your device, and taking part does not by itself establish official training credit.

Take it into your work
Learning matters most when it reaches a real decision, which is why the Learn page points you onward to the thirteen guided practices. Each practice is built around a real task, so a course on fair recruitment, for example, has a natural partner in the practice for inclusive hiring and selection. Nothing here is graded, and My work keeps your place in the courses on your own computer. For a next step, choose one Start here course that speaks to something on your desk this week, and give it an unhurried hour.
