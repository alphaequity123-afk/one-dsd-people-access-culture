Welcome to One DSD

Begin with real work
Most of us come to equity work with something real in hand: a decision to make, a draft to review, a meeting to plan, or a process that is not working for someone. One DSD People, Access and Culture is built for that moment. It is a learning and practice site for staff of the Disability Services Division at the Minnesota Department of Human Services, and it brings learning, practical tools and the right people together in one place.

Five sections, one menu
The main menu at the top of every page has five sections. Start helps you get oriented. Learn holds courses, readings and recordings. Practice is where you work through a real task with a guided practice. Support connects you with answers and with the right person or office. One DSD shows the division itself, its programs, people and plans. Beside the menu is a separate link called My work, which belongs to you alone.

Three quick questions
The Start page opens with three quick questions: the role you are bringing, the kind of work it is, and how soon you need to act. Choose Show me a place to start, and the page suggests a sensible place to begin and explains why it fits. There are no wrong answers, and your choices stay on the page. They are not saved, scored or shared with anyone.

New to DSD
Further down is a part written for people who are new to DSD, and for anyone with years of experience who wants a fresh look. It offers a few ways to get your bearings, including how to use this site and everyday examples of the program at work. Below it, several doors lead into the same program, with no required order. Taking part is voluntary, though existing agency requirements, including required equity analysis, still apply.

Learn at your own pace
Learn is where knowledge builds over time. If you are unsure where to begin, the Core 15 offers fifteen courses chosen for disability services work, to take in any order. Beyond them sits a self-directed series of seventy-two courses at Foundation, Intermediate and Advanced levels, along with a searchable collection, a library and podcasts with full transcripts. You learn at your own pace, and none of it is required.

Practice on a real task
Practice turns understanding into useful work. Thirteen guided practices are each built around a real task, such as a decision, a draft, a meeting or a hire, and each comes with a checklist you can keep. For decisions, the page helps you match the depth to the stakes, from a quick check of about ten minutes to an in-depth review over several sessions. When policy requires a full equity analysis, a quick check does not take its place.

Support and Ask
Support is there when you want another perspective. One of its doors is Ask, a place to put a question in your own words, the way you would to a colleague, and get a considered answer with its sources and its limits. You can follow up or start fresh. Please use general or made-up examples and leave out names and case details. When a decision belongs to a person, Ask points you to the person or office responsible.

Your place in One DSD
The One DSD section shows the division you belong to. You can explore DSD programs, services and priorities, see where equity shows up in everyday division work, and meet colleagues through the One DSD Team and Amplify Equity. It also keeps the setting clear: DSD sits within the Aging and Disability Services Administration, and agency policy continues to guide decisions.

My work, privately yours
Finally, the My work link opens your own workspace, where saved resources, practice plans, your reflection and the courses you left off in are kept on this computer, private to you. None of it is sent to your supervisor or to program staff. Your learning journey invites you to pick the stage that feels closest, from Getting oriented to Leading and sustaining. These stages are not a ladder, and nothing is graded. A good next step is simple: open Start, answer the three questions, and begin with the work already in front of you.
