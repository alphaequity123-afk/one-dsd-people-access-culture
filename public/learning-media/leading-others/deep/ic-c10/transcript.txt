Values in tension, and the history behind trust



Human Services Supervisor: I keep thinking about that session. The parent asked who could see their name. I said DHS is here to help. I meant it.

Program Consultant: I know you did. But that was not what they asked. They asked who could see it. We did not know the answer, and they could tell.

Human Services Supervisor: Fair. I just did not want to scare anyone off by saying I was not sure.

Program Consultant: There is research on this. Across the US and thirty-three other countries, fair process predicted trust in civil servants more than outcomes did. Sometimes several times more. Saying I do not know yet, here is when I will tell you, is fair process.

Human Services Supervisor: And people have reasons to ask. Minnesota apologized in 2010 for what happened in state institutions. Unpaid labor. Procedures without consent. Graves with only numbers.

Program Consultant: Right. And fear spreads past the people a rule covers. In 2018, even some families where everyone born abroad was already a citizen avoided benefits. They had nothing to lose under that rule.

Human Services Supervisor: So the answer matters more than the welcome. What do we do next time?

Program Consultant: Tell people signing in is not needed for information. Get the real answer on access from the area responsible for data privacy. Send it in writing by a date we name.

Human Services Supervisor: Different topic, same skill. A staff member wants to report a family because an uncle manages his niece's money. They say it is tradition.

Program Consultant: What did the staff member actually see or hear?

Human Services Supervisor: The arrangement. And the niece said she cannot buy her own medicine.

Program Consultant: Then those are two different things. The tradition alone is not a reason to report. Her statement about medicine might be reason to believe she is being harmed.

Human Services Supervisor: And if it is reason to believe, the statute says immediately. Not after a second visit. I will make sure the report goes in today, and talk with the staff member about what they noticed.
