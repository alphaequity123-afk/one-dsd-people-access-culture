Adapting without losing yourself, and leading as a bridge



Human Services Supervisor: Thanks for making time. I asked to meet because I keep sending you the hardest county calls, and I realized I never asked how that lands.

Program Consultant: Honestly, I like the counties. I know the lead agency staff, and I can usually tell what they mean when a call gets heated.

Human Services Supervisor: That shows. I also noticed you have not presented your own analysis in two months. Is that connected?

Program Consultant: Probably. After a county meeting I spend the afternoon explaining what each side meant. By the time I get to my own work, the team has moved on.

Human Services Supervisor: So the bridging is real work, and it is eating the work you were hired for. Which parts do you want to keep?

Program Consultant: The relationships. I would hate to lose those. What I do not want is being the only person who can explain anything to anyone.

Human Services Supervisor: Fair. Here is an idea. Before the next joint meeting, I will send written options ahead and build in question time, so less clarifying happens afterward.

Program Consultant: That would help. And could two other reviewers start joining my county calls? They would learn the contacts, and I would not be the single point of failure.

Human Services Supervisor: Yes. And I will deliver the hardest message at the meeting myself. That is my job, not yours. Can I ask one more thing? Do you ever soften your recommendations to keep the peace?

Program Consultant: All the time. I worry that if I am blunt, I will be seen as difficult. It is tiring to watch every word.

Human Services Supervisor: Thank you for saying that. Research on facades at work links that kind of constant self-monitoring to exhaustion. I would rather hear your real recommendation. What would make that feel safe?

Program Consultant: Knowing you will back the substance if a county pushes back. And letting me present the delay analysis myself, start to finish.

Human Services Supervisor: Done. I will check in with you after the meeting about whether your week actually got lighter. If it did not, the plan changes again.
