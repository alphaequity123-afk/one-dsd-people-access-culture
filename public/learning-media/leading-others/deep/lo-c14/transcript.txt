Opening opportunity fairly



Human Services Supervisor: Thanks for coming in. I want to ask you about the county workgroup. You mentioned last month that you had been hoping for partner work. What made you bring it up?

Human Services Specialist: Honestly, I had watched the same two people lead three groups in a row. I did not think anyone was being unfair. I just could not see how anyone else got in.

Human Services Supervisor: That is fair. I looked back at the year after you said that. You were right about the pattern. Each choice felt reasonable at the time, but together they told a different story.

Human Services Specialist: What changed this time? You did not just ask for volunteers.

Human Services Supervisor: I wrote the requirements before I thought about names. Know the waiver rules, explain them clearly to county staff, manage a timeline. Then I added one more: someone who has not led with partners yet. Research shows people bend the criteria to fit whoever they already have in mind. Writing them first is the simplest guard.

Human Services Specialist: I noticed you asked me directly. If you had sent an email, I probably would have offered to take notes.

Human Services Supervisor: That is exactly what the studies on quiet tasks found. People are asked based on who they expect will say yes. So notes now rotate for everyone, including me.

Human Services Specialist: I was nervous about the timeline. Pairing me with the consultant for the first month helped. But I am glad she advised and did not run the meetings.

Human Services Supervisor: That was deliberate. If she had run the meetings, you would have learned by watching and nobody would have seen your work. The point was for you to build a record.

Human Services Specialist: Can I ask what happens with the leadership presentation?

Human Services Supervisor: You present it. And when the division manager asks me who is ready for more, I plan to say your name and point to this work. A mentor gives advice. A sponsor speaks for you where the decision is made. I want to do both, and for more people than the two I used to think of first.

Human Services Specialist: Then I will make sure the presentation gives you something specific to point to.
