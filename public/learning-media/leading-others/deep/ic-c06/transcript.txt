Common ground, and every group's blind spots



Human Services Supervisor: I want to start with an apology. Your last three letters were missing the requirement citation and the deadline. I fixed them myself and never told you.

Licensing Reviewer: I wondered why my letters came back changed. I thought maybe it was just style. Why didn't you say something?

Human Services Supervisor: Honestly, I didn't want you to feel singled out. You were new, and you came from a different kind of work than most of the team. I worried you'd hear it as unfair.

Licensing Reviewer: I understand the intention. But it meant I was the only one not getting the real information. Everyone else learned the standard. I didn't, and then a provider complained.

Human Services Supervisor: That's exactly the pattern researchers have found. In experiments, evaluators gave people from other groups the same praise, but less criticism. Criticism just quietly disappeared.

Licensing Reviewer: And from my side, the praise starts to feel less trustworthy. If you never hear what's wrong, how do you know the good comments are real?

Human Services Supervisor: So here is the full message. Every correction letter needs the specific requirement and the deadline. Without both, a provider may not know what to fix or by when. I hold every reviewer to that standard.

Licensing Reviewer: That's clear. Thank you.

Human Services Supervisor: And I mean this part too. Providers already trust your letters because they're clear and respectful. I'm confident you can add these two pieces easily. Here's an example letter, if it helps.

Licensing Reviewer: That combination helps. The standard, plus knowing you think I can meet it. Can I ask one more thing? Some senior reviewers' letters miss the deadline too.

Human Services Supervisor: Fair question. I'll pull a sample of everyone's letters this week and give the same feedback to anyone with the gap. The standard isn't fair if it only applies to newer staff.

Licensing Reviewer: That would mean a lot. And if you ever want feedback on how you give feedback, I'm glad to tell you what I notice.

Human Services Supervisor: I'd like that. It seems only right to take the same medicine I'm handing out.
