When the same rule is not fair



Call-line supervisor: I want to talk about the morning callback window for renewals. My staff keep hearing from people who work nights. All three calls come while they're asleep, and then their case goes to review.

Policy specialist responsible for the renewal procedure: I hear you. My worry is consistency. If one group gets special timing, others will ask, and pretty soon nobody knows what the rule is.

Call-line supervisor: That's fair. Can I ask what the requirement actually is? Is the morning window in statute, or is it practice?

Policy specialist responsible for the renewal procedure: Let me be precise. The requirement is a documented good-faith attempt to reach the person, plus a written notice. The morning window started years ago because that's when the team was staffed.

Call-line supervisor: So the standard is the attempt and the notice. The timing is a habit. There's research on exactly this. In a SNAP program, families given interview dates late in the month were over twenty percent less likely to keep benefits. The requirement didn't change. Only the timing did.

Policy specialist responsible for the renewal procedure: That's striking. I'd have assumed people who really need coverage will find a way to call back.

Call-line supervisor: Most people assume that. But when Social Security field offices closed, the people who dropped out were mostly those with less education and lower earnings. The hassle didn't screen out weak claims. It screened out people with the least room to absorb it.

Policy specialist responsible for the renewal procedure: All right. Here's what I can support. Everyone gets the same standard: a documented attempt and a written notice. But at renewal, anyone can pick a contact window. That isn't special treatment. It's one standard with more than one route.

Call-line supervisor: That works. My team would also like to add a text reminder. I'd want it reviewed first, though. Some people share phones, so the message can't mention health coverage.

Policy specialist responsible for the renewal procedure: Good catch. Run it by the privacy contact, keep it generic and spread the replies across your team so it doesn't all land on one person.

Call-line supervisor: And to judge it, I'll compare procedural closures and time to first contact before and after. Those come straight from the system. A count of calls made says little.

Policy specialist responsible for the renewal procedure: Agreed. If closures drop and the standard hasn't moved, then the old window was deciding outcomes that eligibility should have decided.
