Your own culture, made visible



Policy Analyst: Can I ask you something that might sound odd? How do people here find out about project leads? I keep hearing someone got one, but I never see them posted.

Senior Policy Specialist: Oh, that's easy. You just mention it to the director after the monthly meeting. Everyone knows that.

Policy Analyst: I didn't know that. And I'm usually remote on meeting days, so I join by video and leave when it ends. Where I worked before, walking up to a director uninvited would have been seen as overstepping.

Senior Policy Specialist: Huh. I never thought of it as a rule. It just seemed like how things work. I learned it my first year from a supervisor who's long retired.

Policy Analyst: That's what makes it hard. To you it's common sense. To me it was invisible. Is there anything else like that?

Senior Policy Specialist: Let me think. When the director says, "That should work," it often means she has doubts. Most people here wait for a follow-up before acting.

Policy Analyst: I took that as a yes last month and started drafting. That explains the awkward meeting afterward.

Senior Policy Specialist: I'm sorry. Nobody meant to leave you out. Those habits started for good reasons. People wanted to be polite and avoid putting the director on the spot.

Policy Analyst: I believe that. The politeness is one of the things I like here. I just need to know what the signals mean, and I need to know when an opportunity exists.

Senior Policy Specialist: That's fair. Researchers have found that when criteria aren't written down, people tend to pick whoever they already have in mind. Writing them first takes that away.

Policy Analyst: Would it help if openings were posted with what counts and when decisions are made? Evening staff probably miss them too.

Senior Policy Specialist: I think so. I'll suggest it to the supervisor. And I'll be clearer myself. If I mean maybe, I'll say maybe.

Policy Analyst: Plain and kind. That works for me.
