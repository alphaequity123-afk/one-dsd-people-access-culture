Inclusive leadership in daily practice



Human Services Supervisor: Thanks for making time. I wanted to ask about last week's review meeting. You knew more about the caller problem than anyone there, and you didn't say anything until I asked you directly. Help me understand that.

Call-line lead: Honestly, I was doing math. Two senior specialists had already said the draft was solid. If I disagreed, would it change anything, or would I just be the person who slowed things down?

Human Services Supervisor: Those are the two questions people weigh before speaking up. Is it safe, and will it matter? I thought I'd covered both by saying the floor was open.

Call-line lead: An open floor after twenty minutes of senior people talking doesn't feel open. When you asked me by name what callers were saying about the assessment step, that was different. It told me you actually wanted my part of the picture.

Human Services Supervisor: There's research on this from intensive care units. Nurses and therapists felt less safe speaking up than physicians. In units where leaders actively invited input, that gap shrank, and the people with less status gained the most.

Call-line lead: That tracks. But what I watch most is what happens after. Last year I raised something on a different project. The memo said everyone supported the plan. That memo is why I hesitated last week.

Human Services Supervisor: That's the part leaders underestimate. A study that followed shift managers for ten months found that openness predicted who spoke up later. Openness meant listening and acting on ideas. Being inspiring didn't predict it once earlier behavior was counted.

Call-line lead: So the memo matters more than the pep talk.

Human Services Supervisor: It does. This time the memo will say what callers told you and that you raised it, if you want the credit. It will say what changed and who answers the question about the eligibility notice. I'd like you to read it before it goes up.

Call-line lead: I'd like the credit. And yes, I'll read it. One more thing. When you introduce me to the county partners, please say my surname the way I do. People usually guess.

Human Services Supervisor: Thank you for telling me. Say it for me now, and tell me how you'd like the rest of the introduction to go. I'd rather ask you here than get it wrong in front of the partners.

Call-line lead: That's the same thing, really. You asked first, and you asked where it was easy for me to answer.
