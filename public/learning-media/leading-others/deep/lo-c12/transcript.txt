Developing people on purpose



Contracts Supervisor: Thanks for making time. The senior grants specialist starts leave in three months. I want to talk about what you might take on.

Grants Specialist: Honestly, I assumed you would give it to the newer specialist. They are always the first to volunteer.

Contracts Supervisor: I will be honest too. I did assume you were not interested. You have not asked for new work in a while.

Grants Specialist: I stopped asking. I asked twice for the complex agreements a few years ago, under a different supervisor, and nothing came of it.

Contracts Supervisor: That helps me understand. Research on coaching finds supervisors tend to invest in people they already see as engaged. I may have been doing that. What would you want to learn?

Grants Specialist: The multi-year agreements with the larger providers. I know the monitoring side well. I have never built the budget pieces.

Contracts Supervisor: Then let's stage it. You build the budget for the next renewal, with the senior specialist reviewing each week. Not all of the complex work at once.

Grants Specialist: Why not all at once? I can handle it.

Contracts Supervisor: I think you can. One study of managers found that learning grows with challenge but levels off when it gets too steep, unless feedback is built in. I want you to keep learning, not just keep up.

Grants Specialist: That is fair. I would need something taken off my plate. The monitoring cycle is heavy right now.

Contracts Supervisor: I will move two monitoring visits to the newer specialist. They want to learn monitoring anyway. After each budget review, could you and I do a short debrief, fifteen minutes, on what worked and what you would change?

Grants Specialist: Yes. And I would like this in my development plan. I want to be ready for a senior role.

Contracts Supervisor: Let's write it now. One assignment, the renewal budget. One skill, multi-year budgeting. One support, weekly review with the senior specialist. Let's revisit it every month.
