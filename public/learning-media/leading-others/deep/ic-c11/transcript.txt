Power in the room



Human Services Manager: Thanks for the email about evening callers. That data is exactly what the schedule needs. I am curious, though. Why not raise it in the meeting?

Call-Line Worker: Honestly? The county directors and providers were going back and forth. I am the newest person on the team. It did not feel like my place.

Human Services Manager: That is fair, and it is common. In one study, 34 of 40 employees said they had held back on an important issue. Lack of rank was one of the top reasons.

Call-Line Worker: There was also a feeling it would not matter. The invitation said everything was open, but then the venue turned out to be fixed. People noticed.

Human Services Manager: That one is on me. I should have corrected the invitation before the meeting. Saying what is fixed and what is open is the least I owe people who show up.

Call-Line Worker: Would it have changed anything if I had spoken up?

Human Services Manager: Yes. The two extra hours are still open. Your data points to a weekday evening. That is a real choice, and the decision is not until next week.

Call-Line Worker: So what do you want me to do?

Human Services Manager: Share the weeks and times directly with the decision group. And next meeting, I will run rounds and start with people who hold the least rank. That way it is not on you to fight for a turn.

Call-Line Worker: That would help. Will people hear what happened with their input?

Human Services Manager: They will. The report-back will say what was considered, what changed, what did not and why. If your evening hour gets adopted, it will say your data is the reason.

Call-Line Worker: Then I will speak up next time. Mostly because I will know it can still change something.
