Capstone: leading intercultural change



New Human Services Supervisor: I finished the series, from the orientation through designing services. I see barriers everywhere now. Honestly, it is a little discouraging. Most of the rules are not mine to change.

Division Director: That is a common feeling. Start with one barrier. What can you actually observe, not what you think is causing it?

New Human Services Supervisor: Openings for acting lead roles in my unit get announced in the morning huddle. Evening staff rarely apply.

Division Director: Good. That is an observation. Do you know why they rarely apply?

New Human Services Supervisor: I assumed they were not interested. But the curiosity course made me doubt that.

Division Director: Right. Research on perspective taking found that imagining someone's view does not make you more accurate. Asking does. So ask them, and make it easy to answer in writing.

New Human Services Supervisor: My first instinct was to ask for a training for the day-shift leads about fairness.

Division Director: I understand. But a field experiment found a one-off training changed attitudes without changing behavior for the people it most needed to reach. Studies of employers found that naming someone responsible did more. Who owns how openings are announced?

New Human Services Supervisor: Me, actually. The selection rules belong to human resources, but how my unit announces openings is mine.

Division Director: Then you have a test. For the next two openings, announce on both shifts and in writing. Predict what will happen. Track how many people on each shift saw the notice in time.

New Human Services Supervisor: And if it is mixed? Say more evening staff apply but they still are not selected?

Division Director: Then you report exactly that. You ask why, and you bring the evidence to the person or area responsible for selection. Most published improvement projects stop after one try. Do not.

New Human Services Supervisor: So the series gave me the knowledge. The change comes from a named owner, a small test and honest follow-up.

Division Director: Exactly. And when a newer leader tells you they cannot change the rule, you will know what to ask them.
