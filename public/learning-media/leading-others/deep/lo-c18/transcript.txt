Evaluating a program while it can still change



Program Manager: The director wants one number for the evening callback pilot. Callbacks completed went from 40 to 110 in a month. That is the story, isn't it?

Data Analyst: It is part of the story. That number tells you staff made more calls. It does not tell you whether callers got what they needed.

Program Manager: If they called back, they got help. What else would they need?

Data Analyst: About 45% of them called again within three days. That suggests many evening contacts did not solve the problem. Staff say they could not mail forms after hours.

Program Manager: So the count went up partly because people had to call twice. That changes things. But I still need something simple for the director.

Data Analyst: Then give two numbers side by side. Callbacks completed, and the share of callers who could take their next step. When those two move together, you have real progress. When they split, you have a question.

Program Manager: The director also wants a target for staff reviews. Ninety percent answered within one business day.

Data Analyst: Research on English ambulance services is worth knowing here. When an eight-minute target carried public ratings, services did improve. But about a third also corrected some response times, and some showed a spike right at eight minutes.

Program Manager: The staff on this team are not going to falsify data.

Data Analyst: Most people in that study were not dishonest either. The pressure works through small choices, like when the clock starts or how a request is classed. And a different target, for emergency waits, improved care with no sign of distortion. The difference is often whether the number can move without the work changing.

Program Manager: So I keep the timeliness goal, pair it with the next-step measure, and review it as a team before anyone's individual review.

Data Analyst: And look at the edges. If requests cluster just under one day, look closer. That is where a target tells you what it is really measuring.
