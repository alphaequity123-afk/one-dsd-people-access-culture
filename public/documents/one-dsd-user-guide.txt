ONE DSD PEOPLE, ACCESS AND CULTURE

USER GUIDE

Help when you need it

Using the program

Find something useful, explore it at your own pace, and bring what you learn into your work.

You can use resources directly or work through a question with a guided activity. Choose the approach that fits your responsibility.

Find your place in One DSD

Begin at One DSD home [/] for learning and practical resources. Explore DSD programs and services [/one-dsd], the One DSD Team [/one-dsd/team] and Amplify Equity [/one-dsd/amplify] to connect the resources with your work. The division sits within the Aging and Disability Services Administration at Minnesota DHS.

Start with your current task

Understand: find a course, community perspective or reference that helps you examine the question.

Prepare: use a tool to compare options, consider who is affected and develop a next step.

Apply and learn: seek the appropriate review, use the agreed approach and notice what changed.

This is a way to connect the resources, not a required sequence.

Find a course or resource

Use Courses [/courses] to search by subject and filter by Foundation, Intermediate or Advanced. The levels describe the complexity of learning, not your position. Choose All levels to include courses whose level has not been recorded.

The resource library [/library] offers references and practical materials. Work areas [/areas] can help you find a starting point connected to your responsibilities.

A community brief provides context for thoughtful questions. People within a community have different experiences; a brief cannot tell you what an individual needs or replace direct engagement.

Study, reflect and practice

Choose a course connected to something you want to understand or do. Read its introduction, work through the examples and consider the feedback after an activity. Ask how a decision affects access, participation or opportunity in your own setting.

Where audio is provided, choose whether to play it or read the accompanying words. Listening is optional. Course photographs and charts support the subject; consider a chart’s source, labels and limitations before drawing a conclusion.

The self-directed learning collection [/learn/self-directed] offers editable PowerPoint downloads. Downloaded files are separate copies on your device. Participation does not itself establish official training credit or completion of required agency training.

Prepare an analysis or practical next step

The Equity Analysis Toolkit [/learn/equity-toolkit] and review tools [/equity-review] help you examine a policy, service or working practice. Identify the question, consider evidence and affected perspectives, compare responses and note what remains uncertain.

Use a practice plan [/my-work/plan] to record an action within your responsibility. Include the review or decision you need, who will act and how you will assess what happened. A draft or downloaded analysis does not authorize a change.

Where ADSA goals apply to your work, use the ADSA goals planner [/learn/equity-toolkit/goals] to select planning priorities and prepare a work plan for review. All six goals remain visible. These goals are not assigned to every DHS employee by this program; confirm the applicable plan and responsibilities with the authorized office.

Join a team or return to a role

The getting started guide [/orientation] helps you find resources as a new or returning colleague. Discuss your responsibilities, applicable policies and access arrangements with your supervisor through the existing onboarding process.

Choose learning connected to a real task, then use one relevant tool or example in your work. A follow-up conversation can examine what was useful, what remains unclear and what support you need. Keep private reflections your own; course participation does not establish an employee performance or equity commitment score.

Save, return to and remove your work

My work [/my-work] brings together saved resources, plans and places in the self-directed courses. Saving depends on this browser allowing local storage. Return using the same browser and device. A different device, a private browsing session or clearing browser data may not retain your work.

Check the saving message on the tool you are using. Some worksheets clear when you reload; download or print those before leaving. If a saving error appears, keep the page open and retain a copy before trying again.

Use My view [/my-view] to review and remove registered private learning information and practice drafts. Removing browser data does not remove files you downloaded or protected operational records. If removal fails, the page tells you some information may remain.

Protect information and find the right support

Use general descriptions in practice activities. Keep private client, case, health and personnel information out of learning notes and questions. Downloading or sharing a file is your choice; check its content and use an approved channel.

Common questions [/ask] provides published answers. Staff pages do not accept typed questions or consultation requests. Find the right person or office [/support/right-person] when the matter needs professional support, a formal response or an authorized decision.

These resources support your work. Applicable law, approved policies and official agency processes still apply.

Review what changed

After using an approach, return to your practice reflection [/program/reflection]. Describe what you tried, what you observed and what you would retain or revise. Completing a course or delivering an activity is different from establishing that a practice improved.

Consider who benefited, who still encounters a barrier, whether affected people helped shape the approach and what evidence is missing. Use those findings to prepare the next question or recommendation.
