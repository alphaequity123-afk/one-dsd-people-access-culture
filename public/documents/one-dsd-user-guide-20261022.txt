Help when you need it

Using the program

Find something useful, explore it at your own pace, and bring what you learn into your work.

You can use resources directly or work through a question with a guided activity. Choose the approach that fits your responsibility.

Download this guide → (https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/documents/one-dsd-user-guide-20261022.txt)

Find your place in One DSD

Begin at the One DSD home page (https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/) for learning and practical resources. Explore DSD programs and services (https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/one-dsd), the One DSD Team (https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/one-dsd/team) and Amplify Equity (https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/one-dsd/amplify) to connect the resources with your work. The division sits within the Aging and Disability Services Administration at Minnesota DHS.

Start with your current task

Understand: find a course, community perspective or reference that helps you examine the question.

Prepare: use a tool to compare options, consider who is affected and develop a next step.

Apply and learn: seek the appropriate review, use the agreed approach and notice what changed.

This is a way to connect the resources, not a required sequence.

See how the learning connects

Begin with the Equity Outcome Map and equity in practice (https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/learn/equity-in-practice) to understand the shared purpose. Then choose a course, reading or tool that helps with a question in your work.

For example, if people find a service notice confusing, explore a communication resource, consider whose perspective is missing, and use the toolkit to compare ways to make the next step clearer.

Explore books and ideas

Books and ideas for your work (https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/learn/readings) brings together 200 readings in nine collections. Search by title, author or idea, or narrow your choices by subject and reading collection. Choose Clear choices to begin again.

Open a reading for its introduction, key ideas, fuller summary and discussion questions. Follow its connections to courses and practical resources. Use a reading path (https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/learn/readings/pathways) when you would like help choosing where to begin.

Try one question in a team conversation, compare two perspectives, or work through the service-notice example (https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/learn/readings/practice/service-notice). Return to the reading after trying an idea and ask what you would change.

Reading notes introduce an author’s perspective; they do not replace the original book or establish agency policy. Check the source and consider where an idea fits your setting.

Find a course or resource

Use Courses (https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/courses) to search by subject and filter by Foundation, Intermediate or Advanced. The levels describe the complexity of learning. Choose the level that fits your question and experience. Choose All levels to include courses whose level has not been recorded.

The resource library (https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/library) offers references and practical materials. Work areas (https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/areas) can help you find a starting point connected to your responsibilities.

A community brief provides context for thoughtful questions. People within a community have different experiences; a brief cannot tell you what an individual needs or replace direct engagement.

Study, reflect and practice

Choose a course connected to something you want to understand or do. Read its introduction, work through the examples and consider the feedback after an activity. Ask how a decision affects access, participation or opportunity in your own setting.

Where a recording is available, use the player to listen, pause or return to a passage. Two-person podcasts explore a situation through conversation; short readings highlight selected passages or reflection questions. The accompanying text lets you follow along or read instead. Listening is optional. Course photographs and charts support the subject; consider a chart’s source, labels and limitations before drawing a conclusion.

The self-directed learning collection (https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/learn/self-directed) offers editable PowerPoint downloads. Downloaded files are separate copies on your device. Participation does not itself establish official training credit or completion of required agency training.

Prepare an analysis or practical next step

The Equity Analysis Toolkit (https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/learn/equity-toolkit) and review tools (https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/equity-review) help you examine a policy, service or working practice. Identify the question, consider evidence and affected perspectives, compare responses and note what remains uncertain.

Visit the toolkit worked example (https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/learn/equity-toolkit/practice#worked-example) to watch the tutorial and examine a completed example. Then try a separate practice decision before applying the questions to your own work.

Use a practice plan (https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/my-work/plan) to record an action within your responsibility. Include the review or decision you need, who will act and how you will assess what happened. A draft or downloaded analysis does not authorize a change.

Where ADSA goals apply to your work, use the ADSA goals planner (https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/learn/equity-toolkit/goals) to select planning priorities and prepare a work plan for review. All six goals remain visible. These goals are not assigned to every DHS employee by this program; confirm the applicable plan and responsibilities with the authorized office.

Join a team or return to a role

The getting started guide (https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/orientation) helps you find resources as a new or returning colleague. Discuss your responsibilities, applicable policies and access arrangements with your supervisor through the existing onboarding process.

Choose learning connected to a real task, then use one relevant tool or example in your work. A follow-up conversation can examine what was useful, what remains unclear and what support you need. Keep private reflections your own; course participation does not establish an employee performance or equity commitment score.

Connect with people and strengthen your leadership

Explore Minnesota Communities (https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/minnesota-communities) for context, employee resource groups (https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/employee-resource-groups) for connections, and cultural and language-access guidance (https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/practice/clas) when planning participation. Ask people about their own preferences and experience.

Leadership practice (https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/one-dsd/leadership) offers ways to connect equity with team decisions and staff development. Use a relevant question or example in a conversation, agree on a practical next step, and return to what you learn together.

For the wider picture, explore how the program connects learning with work (https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/program/model). The site map (https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/site-map) gives you another way to find a page.

Save, return to and remove your work

My work (https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/my-work) brings together saved resources, plans and places in the self-directed courses. Your saved learning stays in this browser on this device. Return here to find it again. A different device, a private browsing session or clearing browser data may not retain your work.

Check the saving message on the tool you are using. Some worksheets clear when you reload; download or print those before leaving. If a saving error appears, keep the page open and retain a copy before trying again.

Use My view (https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/my-view) to review and remove saved learning information and practice drafts. Removing browser data does not remove files you downloaded or protected operational records. If removal fails, the page tells you some information may remain.

Protect information and find the right support

Use general descriptions in practice activities. Keep private client, case, health and personnel information out of learning notes and questions. Downloading or sharing a file is your choice; check its content and use an approved channel.

Common questions (https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/ask) provides published answers. Staff pages do not accept typed questions or consultation requests. Find the right person or office (https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/support/right-person) when the matter needs professional support, a formal response or an authorized decision.

These resources support your work. Applicable law, approved policies and official agency processes still apply.

Review what changed

After using an approach, return to your practice reflection (https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/program/reflection). Describe what you tried, what you observed and what you would retain or revise. Completing a course or delivering an activity is different from establishing that a practice improved.

Consider who benefited, who still encounters a barrier, whether affected people helped shape the approach and what evidence is missing. Use those findings to prepare the next question or recommendation.
