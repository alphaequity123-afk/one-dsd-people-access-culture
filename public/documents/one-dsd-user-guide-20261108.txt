How to use One DSD

A short guide to finding your way around, reading pages that open in sections, and saving, sharing and coming back to your work.

Find your way around
--------------------
The menu at the top of every page takes you to Home and the four main areas: Practice, Leadership, Communities and Knowledge. Each area opens on a page that gathers what's inside it, so you're never more than a few clicks from what you need.

The home page offers three ways in: Learn, Apply a tool and Communities. A finder helps you go straight to what fits the task in front of you, and you'll see the next observances from the Culture Calendar. Ask and Search sit at the top of every page, ready when you have a question.

Not sure where to begin? The Start page asks three quick questions about your role, your work and your timing, then suggests a good place to begin and explains why. Nothing you choose there is saved or shared.

How pages are laid out
----------------------
Every page opens with what most people need first. More detail sits in panels with dark blue title bars. Select a title to open its panel, and select it again to close it.

If a link points to something inside a closed panel, the panel opens for you and scrolls to it. When you print a page, every panel opens so nothing is left out.

Long lists, like sources, readings and policies, show one page at a time. Use the search box to narrow the list and the page numbers to move through it.

Learn: courses, lessons and readings
------------------------------------
Courses are grouped by subject and level: Foundation, Intermediate and Advanced. Each lesson ends with a Finish this lesson area, where you can try a situation, think about what to carry forward and choose your next step.

The standards lessons are set out in tabs: Watch, Discuss, Practice, Use it at work, Full lesson text and Sources. Your next step follows the tabs.

The self-directed collection has 72 courses. You can filter by level, search titles, audiences and outcomes, download an editable PowerPoint or share a course with a colleague.

Readings give you a summary, key ideas and questions to talk about for each book. If you'd like help choosing where to start, try a reading path.

Minnesota communities
---------------------
Each community page opens with an overview: who the community is, the names people use, and what's on the page. From there you'll find history and culture, learning and reflection, working with the community, people and institutions, and sources.

The Native American, Tribal Nations and Alaska Native pages stay as single pages and follow their own guidance on Tribal authority and relationships.

A community page helps you ask better questions. It can't tell you what any one person needs, so ask people directly what matters to them.

Practice and tools
------------------
The Equity Analysis Toolkit helps you look closely at a policy, service or way of working. You name the question, look at the evidence and who is affected, compare options and note what's still uncertain. Start with the worked example, then try it on your own decision.

A practice plan captures something you can act on within your own role, the review it needs, who will act and how you'll know what changed. A draft plan doesn't authorize a change on its own.

Community Outreach and Engagement
---------------------------------
Community Outreach and Engagement brings together partner organizations, county agencies, designated providers, a county map, the policy catalog and how the service system fits together. Each list has a search box and page numbers.

Share and download
------------------
Most pages and resources let you share the page or send it by email. A shared link opens exactly that page and carries nothing personal.

Downloads come in several formats: Word, Excel, PowerPoint, PDF, web page and plain text. A download is your own copy, so look it over before you send it on.

Save your place and return
--------------------------
My work keeps your saved resources, plans and your place in the self-directed courses. Saved work stays in this browser on this device. Another device, a private window or clearing your browser data won't keep it.

Use My view to see or remove what this browser has saved. Some worksheets clear when you reload, so download or print them before you leave.

Ask and get support
-------------------
Ask takes a question in your own words and gives you an answer with its sources and limits. Select Ask at the top of any page and the answer opens in a panel beside the page, or use the Ask page. The Speak and Talk buttons let you say your question or have a spoken conversation, and Listen reads an answer aloud. Please keep private client, case, health and personnel details out of what you type or say.

When something needs professional support, a formal response or an official decision, About and help shows you how to find the person or area responsible.

Protect information
-------------------
Use general descriptions in practice activities. Keep private client, case, health and personnel information out of notes, plans and questions.

Your lesson and course activity is never counted, stored or shown to anyone. These resources are here to support your work; the law, approved policies and official processes still apply.
