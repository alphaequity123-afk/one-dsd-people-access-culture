One DSD People, Access and Culture

How to use One DSD

A short guide to finding what you need, reading pages that open in sections, and saving, sharing and returning to your work.

Find your way around
--------------------
The menu across the top of every page leads to the main areas: Start, Learn, Practice, Support and the other sections. Each area opens on a landing page with a short row of links to the pages inside it.

The home page offers three ways in: Learn, Apply a tool, and Communities. Near the bottom, a finder takes you to resources for your task, and a box lets you type a question for Ask. It also lists the next observances from the Culture Calendar and has a link for leaders, supervisors, managers and directors.

Not sure where to begin? The Start page asks three short questions about your role, your work and your timing, then suggests a place to begin and explains why it fits. Nothing you choose there is saved or shared.

How pages are laid out
----------------------
Each page opens with what most people need first. More detail sits in panels with dark blue title bars. Select a title to open its panel and select it again to close it.

A link that points to something inside a closed panel opens that panel for you and scrolls to it. When you print a page, every panel opens so the printout is complete.

Long lists, such as sources, readings and policies, show one page of entries at a time. Use the search box to narrow the list and the page numbers to move through it.

Learn: courses, lessons and readings
------------------------------------
Courses are grouped by subject and level: Foundation, Intermediate and Advanced. Each lesson ends with a Finish this lesson area. Use its tabs to try a situation, review what to carry forward and choose your next step.

The standards lessons are arranged in tabs: Watch, Discuss, Practice, Use it at work, Full lesson text and Sources. Your next step follows the tabs.

The self-directed collection has 72 courses. Filter by level, search titles, audiences and outcomes, download an editable PowerPoint, or share a course with a colleague.

Readings offer a summary, key ideas and questions to discuss for each book. Use a reading path when you would like help choosing where to begin.

Minnesota communities
---------------------
Each community page opens with an overview: who the community is, the names people use, and a list of the page's parts. The parts cover history and culture, learning and reflection, working with the community, people and institutions, and sources.

The Native American, Tribal Nations and Alaska Native pages stay as single pages and follow their own guidance about Tribal authority and relationships.

A community page gives context for better questions. It cannot tell you what any one person needs; ask people directly what matters to them.

Practice and tools
------------------
The Equity Analysis Toolkit helps you examine a policy, service or working practice: identify the question, look at evidence and who is affected, compare options and note what is still uncertain. Start with the worked example, then try your own decision.

Practice plans record an action within your responsibility, the review it needs, who will act and how you will know what changed. A draft does not authorize a change.

Community Outreach and Engagement
---------------------------------
Community Outreach and Engagement brings together partner organizations, county agencies, designated providers, a county map, the policy catalog and how the service system connects. Each list has a search box and page numbers.

Share and download
------------------
Most pages and resources have Share this page and Share by email. A shared link opens exactly that page and carries nothing personal.

Downloads come in several formats: Word, Excel, PowerPoint, PDF, web page and plain text. A downloaded file is your own copy; check its content before sending it on.

Save your place and return
--------------------------
My work gathers your saved resources, plans and places in the self-directed courses. Saved work stays in this browser on this device; another device, a private window or clearing browser data will not keep it.

Use My view to review or remove what this browser has saved. Some worksheets clear when you reload, so download or print them before leaving.

Ask and get support
-------------------
Ask takes a question in your own words and gives an answer with its sources and limits. Type in the box near the bottom of the home page and the answer opens in a panel beside the page, or use the Ask page. The small buttons in the box let you speak your question or hold a spoken conversation, and the one under an answer reads it aloud. Keep private client, case, health and personnel details out of what you type or say.

When a matter needs professional support, a formal response or an authorized decision, find the person or area responsible through Support.

Protect information
-------------------
Use general descriptions in practice activities. Keep private client, case, health and personnel information out of notes, plans and questions.

Lesson and course activity is never counted, stored or shown to anyone. These resources support your work; applicable law, approved policies and official processes still apply.
