One DSD People, Access and Culture

One DSD data and metrics framework

The Equity and Inclusion Operations Consultant leads One DSD People, Access and Culture. This document supports planning and review; proposed assignments, measures, resources and schedules must be confirmed with the people responsible. It does not create new DHS policy or replace required decision authority.

Data and metrics framework

Disability Services Division (DSD) · For DSD leaders, goal leads, named contacts, the One DSD Team and the Equity and Inclusion Operations Consultant

1. What this framework does

This framework recommends what DSD should measure to know whether the One DSD People, Access and Culture program, and the division's equity work as a whole, is making a difference. It covers four things:

The program itself: all thirteen functions of the application, from Ask and the Library to guided practice, help requests and the One DSD Team.

The six ADSA equity goals: Eliminate Disparities, Community Engagement, Hiring and Retention, Learning and Development, Contracts and Procurement, and Communication and Accessibility.

The two areas the goals do not fully cover: leadership and accountability, and workplace culture and belonging. Both are pillars of the program's Equity Strategic Framework.

How to collect, protect, report and act on the numbers, and who owns each one.

It lines up with the DHS Equity Policy's six objectives, the four outcomes in the DHS-wide Equity Outcome Map, the Equity Strategic Framework's measurement approach.

2. Principles

Measure decisions and outcomes, not clicks. The program never counts learning or use: no page views, site totals, completions, time spent, plays or Ask questions, not even as anonymous totals. Completion or use alone never shows skill. Every function is reviewed three ways: was it offered (delivery), did it reach real work (application), and did anything change (benefit). This follows the program's own evaluation rule of examining delivery, application and benefit separately.

Three kinds of signs over time. Signs the work is happening (early), signs how we work is changing (middle), and signs results are improving (later). Do not mistake activity for results.

Evidence people choose to record or share; break down by group where gaps live. Program evidence is decision records, plans, reviews, before-and-after work products (a revised notice, a job posting), outcome records shared without names, facilitated task sessions with volunteers and "you said, we did" records. The program never tracks, scores or profiles an individual, and it never asks users for demographic details. Gaps between groups are measured in DSD's workforce, service, contract and engagement data, where they actually occur, and are always broken down by race, ethnicity, Tribal affiliation, disability, language, age, gender, veteran status and region where the data allows.

The consultant leads program measurement. Contributors measure the work for which they are responsible. Each goal lead and named contact collects and reports their own numbers. Leaders decide what matters. The consultant leads the program and helps design measures and read results. Source owners remain responsible for the accuracy and appropriate use of their data.

Fewer, better numbers. A small set of headline numbers goes in the quarterly report. Everything else is kept for the annual review or for answering questions.

Every number has a purpose. For each measure we know who uses it, what decision it informs, and what we do if it moves the wrong way.

People's accounts count too. Numbers are paired with what staff, people who use services, families, communities and Tribal Nations tell us. A number without a story is incomplete.

Protect people. Follow the Minnesota Government Data Practices Act, hide small numbers that could identify someone, respect Tribal data sovereignty, and keep case, medical, personnel and complaint details out of the program.

8. Yearly measures

Review these at the agreed review point: workforce representation compared with the communities DSD serves; promotions and moves into leadership by group; the full climate survey by group; community members' sense of being heard; contract dollars by organization type; the full set of service gaps; group-level intercultural development where assessed; and a program evaluation that looks at delivery, application and benefit for all thirteen functions.

10. Protecting people and the data

No counts of learning or use. No page views, site totals, completions, time spent, plays or Ask totals; no user accounts tied to activity, no individual learning scores, no demographic questions to users.

Hide small numbers. Do not show any group with fewer people than the agreed minimum (often ten). Combine quarters or groups if needed.

Survey privacy. Anonymous; results shown only for groups large enough; limits explained before people answer.

Tribal data. Data about Tribal members and Tribal lead agencies is shared only with consent and under agreement, and Tribal Nations see results about them first.

Data practices. Follow the Minnesota Government Data Practices Act and HIPAA where service data is involved; keep data only as long as the records schedule allows.

No personnel records. The program and this framework do not hold, create or feed personnel records.

Who can see what. Leaders and goal leads see the full report. Staff see the Team page, "you said, we did" and the public summary. Raw data stays with its owner.

11. Data gaps to start on now

Service data broken down by race, language and lead agency, through the ADSA Equity Director (expect delays; use DSD-held data in the meantime).

Tribal affiliation in service data, which is often incomplete; work with Tribal lead agencies under agreement.

Deaf, hard-of-hearing and blind status in service data, which is usually not captured; estimate from assessment communication fields where allowed.

Applicant-flow data by group at division level, through Human Resources.

Contract applicant data by organization type, which may not be collected today; add it to solicitations.

A simple way for staff to share an outcome record or a before-and-after example without their name.
