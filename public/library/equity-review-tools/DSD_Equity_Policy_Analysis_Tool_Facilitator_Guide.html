1. What this tool is and when to use it

The DSD Equity Policy Analysis Tool is a reusable, ten-step method for analyzing a policy, procedure, legislative proposal, bill, budget, contract or service change through the DHS Equity Policy. It exists in two forms that carry the same questions: an interactive browser file (DSD_Equity_Policy_Analysis_Tool.html) that guides the analysis, checks its quality and produces the Equity Analysis Record, and this guide, which explains the method, supports team sessions and includes a paper worksheet for settings without a screen.

The tool operationalizes three things DSD staff and leaders are already accountable for. The DHS Equity Policy requires every employee to apply an equity lens to policies, proposals, practices, procedures, rules, budgets and decisions, and it requires a formal equity analysis for any action with prolonged or long-term impact that goes for approval at supervisor level or above. The State of Minnesota Equity Analysis Toolkit supplies the seven criteria the analysis must answer. The six ADSA Implementation Plan goals say what the division is trying to move. The tool is the place where those three meet a real decision.

Use it when a DSD unit is drafting or revising policy or procedure; when the division prepares an agency legislative proposal or a bill analysis; when a budget, fiscal note, RFP, grant or contract is being shaped; when a service design, eligibility rule, form or technology change will outlive the project that creates it; and when leadership asks "what does this do to the people least well served now?"

Do not use it instead of the official DHS Equity Analysis Toolkit and User Guide, which remain the authority on the required review; civil rights review by the Equal Opportunity and Access Division; Tribal consultation under Executive Order 19-24; labor relations and bargaining obligations; or legal and legislative processes. The tool prepares and records the equity analysis that accompanies those processes. It does not approve anything.

Equity scan or formal analysis

Step 0 identifies the policy criterion from the expected duration of the effects and the approval level. An action with prolonged or long-term impact scheduled for approval at supervisor level or above requires formal equity analysis. The policy allows a DHS Equity Analysis, an Equity Scan, or a customized tool developed in consultation with the equity director to accompany the action. Use the official Toolkit User Guide and consult the responsible equity office to determine the appropriate approach. A short timeline or a prior analysis alone does not settle that choice. The tool does not approve the review or replace these responsibilities.

The tool also mirrors the One DSD People, Access and Culture program's design. The coach panel's "what the policy says, questions to ask, strong versus weak, pitfalls" pattern follows the program's lesson structure (objective, explanation, practice, feedback). Resources in the tool link to the program's Equity Policy page, Toolkit Studio, Library, Minnesota Communities and "Find the right person" routes, so an analyst can move from a question in the tool to a resource in the program without leaving the work.

The affected communities grid

The grid in Step 2 is the center of the method. It lists the communities the policy names (communities of color and multiracial people, American Indian people and Tribal Nations, people with disabilities, LGBTQIA2S+ people, veterans) and adds the people DSD's decisions reach first: people with limited English proficiency, people in rural and Greater Minnesota, the direct support and provider workforce, and DSD staff. Analysts add rows for intersecting or specific populations. Each row records the effect (benefit, burden, both, unknown, not affected), severity, how the group is affected, the evidence, and whether the group has been engaged. Every "unknown" becomes a task in Step 3; every "burden" must reappear in Steps 5 and 7 with a cause and a mitigation. This is the discipline that turns a values statement into an analysis.

3. Running an analysis

Alone, in about 90 minutes

Open the browser file. Complete Step 0 before anything else; if the review track is "formal analysis," tell the decision-maker now so the decision date can hold. Work the steps in order, using the coach panel's questions. Mark "unknown" freely in Step 2 rather than guessing. Save a draft (.json) at the end of each sitting; the tool saves a draft in this browser on this device; download a copy to move or retain it separately. Use the quality check in Step 9 before producing the record.

As a team session, in about two hours

The toolkit's first best practice is to assemble a team that reflects the people affected. A facilitator (often the Equity and Inclusion Operations Consultant, a goal lead or a trained analyst) runs the session with the proposal owner, one or two staff who deliver the service or administer the procedure, a data contact, and where possible people with lived experience of the decision.

Facilitation notes. Keep personal and case details out of the record; describe roles and communities, not individuals. Read the "weak" example aloud when a field starts to sound like it. When the team cannot agree on the verdict, record the disagreement in the summary and send both views to the decision-maker; the decision is theirs, the analysis is the team's.

4. Bill analysis and legislative proposals

For bills and agency legislative proposals, Step 0 captures the file number, author and stage, and Step 9 adds a button that copies a formatted equity section for the standard bill analysis. The section carries the equity position (support as drafted, support with amendments, significant concerns, oppose), the summary, communities benefited and burdened, the net effect on disparities, evidence, engagement and Tribal consultation status, access and CLAS provisions, recommended amendments, fiscal notes for equity provisions, formal reviews triggered, and the policy objectives and ADSA goals engaged.

Timing matters more in session than anywhere else. Run Steps 0 through 5 as soon as a bill is referred, even with unknowns; a partial record filed early is worth more than a complete one after the committee deadline. Update the record at each stage and keep the draft files named by bill number and date. Route the analysis through the division's legislative or government relations contact; the tool supports the bill analysis process and does not change who owns it.

5. Building capacity in staff and leadership

The tool teaches while it is used, but the division should also use it deliberately to build skill. The sequence below follows the program's Learn, Use, See pattern and can be run inside the 18-month Equity Implementation Work Plan.

Learn (first 60 days). Every policy, program and supervisory staff member completes the program's toolkit companion and the DHS Equity Analysis Toolkit training. Leaders read Section 1 of this guide and complete Step 0 for one live decision in their area. Outcome: everyone can say what the policy requires and tell a scan from an analysis.

Use (days 60 to 180). Practice labs. Once a month, a facilitator runs the two-hour session on a real DSD decision with a mixed group. Participants rotate roles (owner, data contact, facilitator). Each lab produces a real record and a short debrief using the strong-versus-weak examples. Outcome: a pool of staff who can lead an analysis, and a set of worked DSD examples for the program library.

See (from day 180). Records are shared with the DSD Equity Team and the ADSA Equity Director; the quarterly status report counts analyses completed, verdict distribution, unknowns resolved, and recommendations closed. Leaders attend one lab a quarter and review one record each month. Outcome: the analysis is visible in how decisions move, and leaders can read a record and ask the right next question.

Coaching rubric

Use the strong-versus-weak pairs in the coach panel to give feedback on a draft record. A record is ready for a decision-maker when all items in the Step 9 quality check are met, when every burden has a cause and a mitigation, when at least two alternatives were compared, and when the summary paragraph could be read by someone outside DSD and understood.

What leaders should be able to do after six months

Determine the review track for any decision in their area. Read a record and locate the verdict, the burdened communities and the owned recommendations in under two minutes. Ask, in any meeting, "who did we hear from and what changed?" and "what happens to the gap?" Refuse to approve a lasting action that arrives without its analysis.

7. Quality check

A record is ready for the decision-maker when each statement is true.

Decision-maker and decision date are named, and review questions are answered. Confirm the appropriate review with the official User Guide and responsible equity office.

Outcomes are stated for the people affected, with a measure.

Every affected group has a description of how it is affected; unknowns were carried into data follow-up.

Data sources and findings are recorded.

Engagement is described and its influence on the proposal is stated; Tribal consultation status is determined.

Root causes are named and the net effect on disparities is judged.

Disability access and language provisions are specified.

At least two alternatives were compared and formal routing paths are identified.

Disaggregated measures, a review date and resources for equitable implementation are set.

A verdict and summary are written, at least one recommendation has an owner and a date, and communication of results is planned.

8. Maintaining the tool

The Equity and Inclusion Operations Consultant leads the program and maintains the tool and keeps it aligned with the official DHS Equity Analysis Toolkit and User Guide. Review it twice a year and whenever DHS publishes a change to the policy, the toolkit or the Equity Outcome Map. Changes to the default community list, the routing list, the coach text and the rubric are made in the browser file's content section; this guide is updated with it. Completed records are division records; store them where the division's records schedule directs and send a copy to the ADSA Equity Director and the DSD Equity Team.

The browser file saves drafts locally on this device. Analysts can also download a draft and reopen it with Open draft. Use an approved work device and storage location. Clear local draft data when working on a shared device. Keep personal, case, medical, personnel and complaint details out of every field.
