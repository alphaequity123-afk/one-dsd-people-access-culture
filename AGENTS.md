## Program writing

Write program-authored pages, navigation, learning materials, explanations, and audio/video scripts in plain, professional language for adults working in human and social services. Aim roughly at grades 10–12 while respecting the reader's experience and judgment. This is guidance for clarity, not a minimum complexity score: keep simple instructions simple.

Use familiar words, direct sentences, short connected paragraphs, and concrete examples. Retain necessary professional terms, explain them when first needed, and preserve evidence, nuance, and qualifications. Keep the tone calm, warm, supportive, direct and professional. The owner-approved One DHS Learn introduction, “Learning for the work you do,” is the reference. Explain what a page offers and the next useful action. Avoid robotic system phrasing, promotional enthusiasm, overfamiliarity and condescension. Warmth comes from useful guidance and respect for the reader’s judgment.

Keep original source documents, policy wording, citations, and quotations accurate. Apply the writing standard to the application's own explanations around those sources; do not silently rewrite the original as though it were the publisher's wording. Preserve the approved GEFen read-aloud voice and approved female/male Qwen podcast voices. User-facing playback labels stay simple: "Listen" and "Watch."

## Ask

Provide guidance and support: help the reader understand the issue, consider relevant options and identify a practical next step. Use the same calm, warm, supportive and direct voice as the approved Learn page. Ask a focused follow-up when it materially affects the guidance; preserve privacy, source attribution and the distinction between general guidance and official decisions.

Ask's primary function is evidence-based inference and synthesis across relevant application resources, agencies, partners, and the broader public web. It supports any subject, original work products, and optional contextual follow-up questions. Do not confine questions to diversity, equity, human services, or a fixed website list. Distinguish evidence, interpretation, uncertainty, and official authority. Preserve explicit source-only requests, citation integrity, privacy protections, owner stop controls, and existing spending limits. Keep the interface calm and preserve approved voice services.

## Source republication

The program owner chose full in-app copies only for verified public-domain or openly licensed content. Keep other working original links. An accessible website or government domain alone is not an approval for its images, guest material, or third-party publications. Record the specific source, license evidence, attribution, and reviewed content identity in `config/source-republication.json`. Preserve substantive text and source credits in converted readings. If a page cannot be reproduced completely, retain its original destination; do not replace it with an empty or partial reading. Confirmed missing pages are excluded from featured resources.

## Application accessibility

The owner requires application-wide accessibility for this internal staff tool. Follow `docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md` for shared interfaces, Ask, learning, practice, media and document exports. Preserve approved branding and voice. Test meaningful keyboard and assistive-technology behavior; never infer full conformance from automated checks alone.


## One DHS program purpose

Use `docs/ONE-DHS-APPLIED-LEARNING-STANDARD.md` to design context-sensitive learning, practice, feedback and application throughout the staff experience.

The owner-provided `docs/ONE-DHS-PROGRAM-REQUIREMENTS.txt` records the program's governing purpose and requirements. Design and review across One DHS around practical cultural development: help staff consider different perspectives, examine assumptions, adapt their work and learn from effects in services, programs, decisions, communication and workplace relationships. Connect learning to appropriate practice, guidance and follow-through. Preserve the owner's words as source material.

Treat the document's descriptions, counts, privacy promises and statements of official requirements as claims to verify against the application and authoritative sources, not as evidence that a capability, policy or outcome has already been established. Do not infer or label a person's or the agency's intercultural orientation, score employees, equate course completion with cultural change, or claim an assessment result without authorized evidence. Protect privacy, accessibility, source attribution, Tribal sovereignty and existing stop and spending safeguards. Keep a person's question useful on its own terms; do not force a cultural lesson into unrelated work.
