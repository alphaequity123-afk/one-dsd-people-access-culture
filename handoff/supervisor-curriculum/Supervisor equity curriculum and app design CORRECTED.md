# Teach supervisors decisions, keep the door open

Build the curriculum and build the space, and keep both honest about what they are. One person, a consultant, personally owns this application and can delete it at any time. DHS knows it is being built. It is self-contained, touches no Minnesota or enterprise system, and is strictly voluntary for everyone at Minnesota DHS and the Disability Services Division (DSD), from staff to commissioner. The one exception is the Equity Analysis Toolkit, which is required of all DHS staff, regardless of status. It is a private place to prepare for hard decisions, separate from anything the state requires. The curriculum should be organized around the decisions supervisors actually make (hiring, assignments, accommodation, reports of harm, reviews, corrective action). All of the material goes in: **622 served courses, 72 self-directed courses and 19 new supervisor and manager courses, placed in 17 collections, with 4 Tribal and Native courses kept as referrals, 717 rows in all**. The 19 new courses are written, as drafts: **93 lessons and 1,188 minutes, about 20 hours**. The application should gain one clearly labeled, public space for supervisors and managers, with no sign-in and no view of anyone's team, because that is the owner's standing design and because the app has promised it to staff. Minnesota law requires the state to train its managers and supervisors under [Minn. Stat. 43A.21](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/43A.21), subd. 3(b). That is the state's duty, met by the state's own training, and this report gives it as context only. The app's notice that "none of this is required" is accurate, and I recommend adding one line: this app does not replace any training the state requires. The evidence is honest but discouraging: training reliably moves knowledge and stated intentions, rarely moves job behavior, and has never been evaluated rigorously for public human services supervisors. No one should promise that this will reduce disparities. Voluntary, self-paced use mostly reaches people who are already interested. Throughout, "Evidence" marks what a source reports and "Inference" marks my reasoning. This is not legal advice.

## Training alone rarely changes behavior, so teach tools attached to real decisions

**Evidence.** The largest synthesis, [Bezrukova et al. 2016](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/bitstream/1813/71443/1/Perry_3_A_meta_analysis.pdf), covers 260 studies and reports an overall effect of g = .38, but it should not be quoted as "training works." Follow-up averaged only .83 months, "behavioral learning" includes self-reports and judgment tests, result measures were "rarely encountered," and the median program ran **28 hours**, far longer than a typical e-learning module. The same paper does give real design signals: integrated training beat standalone (g = .57 versus .36), and awareness-only training had the smaller effects on behavioral learning. The closest analogue to an e-learning course is [Chang et al. 2019](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC6475398), a preregistered field experiment at a global firm with a roughly one-hour online module. Attitudes moved, while overall behavior did not (women chosen for mentoring, P = .914). Its authors conclude that one-off training is unlikely to work alone. Public-sector results are no better. The [NYPD stepped-wedge trial](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/nypd/downloads/pdf/analysis_and_planning/impacts-of-implicit-bias-awareness-training-in-%20the-nypd.pdf) trained 14,741 officers and found "insufficient evidence" that disparities fell, while its authors named immediate supervisors as perhaps the most important supporting force. The UK government [phased out unconscious bias training](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/written-ministerial-statement-on-unconscious-bias-training) and integrated inclusion into core and leadership training, though that integrated approach is itself unevaluated.

**Evidence.** General supervisor development looks better, and this is where the design should borrow. [Lacerenza et al. 2017](https://stars.library.ucf.edu/scopus2015/5360) reports large leadership training effects across 335 samples (abstract only), favoring needs analysis, feedback, practice, spacing and delivery that is not self-administered. [Sitzmann et al. 2006](https://digitalcommons.memphis.edu/facpubs/12083) found that web and classroom delivery were equal when methods were the same. [Taylor et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1037/0021-9010.90.4.692) ties transfer to mixed positive and negative models, trainee-generated scenarios, goal setting, trained superiors, and rewards and sanctions at work. Randomized field work shows specific behavior can move: [Zohar & Polachek 2014](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0034096) used two feedback sessions drawn from a supervisor's own workers and changed independently audited safety scores, and [Friebel et al.](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2020.3905) cut turnover by a quarter for nine months when a CEO asked managers to reduce it. Neither is about equity. Supervisor effects on retention have quasi-experimental support ([Hoffman & Tadelis 2021](https://doi.org/10.1086/711409)). I found no randomized study that trains supervisors and measures retention of their reports by race, gender or disability.

**Evidence.** Process design has better causal or validity support than awareness content. Structured interviews rank highly and narrow group differences ([Sackett et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1037/apl0000994), [Huffcutt & Roth 1998](https://doi.org/10.1037//0021-9010.83.2.179)), though the Sackett paper has [drawn criticism](https://doi.org/10.1111/joop.70048). Equal ratings can still yield unequal pay growth ([Castilla 2008](https://doi.org/10.1086/588738)), and accountability and transparency procedures shrank gaps at one company in a before-and-after study ([Castilla 2015](https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.2014.0950)). Responsibility structures showed the broadest gains in [Kalev et al. 2006](https://doi.org/10.1177/000312240607100404), a panel study. Formalization is not automatically good: [Dobbin et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1177/0003122415596416) found some reforms limiting discretion activate the problem they target, and [Australian public servants](https://www.pmc.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-06/unconscious-bias.pdf) shortlisted identified minority candidates more often than de-identified ones.

**Evidence, unsettled.** Mandatory versus voluntary has no direct test. [Dobbin and Kalev's HBR account](https://hbr.org/2016/07/why-diversity-programs-fail) links mandatory manager training to declines for some groups, and their [Daedalus paper](https://www.amacad.org/publication/daedalus/retooling-career-systems-fight-workplace-bias-evidence-us-corporations) reports a similar pattern for legal-compliance training. Bezrukova found no significant overall difference, with mandatory better on behavioral learning (g = .63 versus .42) and voluntary far better on reactions. [Lacerenza's thesis abstract](https://stars.library.ucf.edu/etd/5162/) splits by outcome, linking voluntary attendance to transfer and mandatory attendance to results. The two literatures measure different things, and firms adopting programs are not random ([Dobbin et al. 2011](http://nrs.harvard.edu/urn-3:HUL.InstRepos:32116911)). What has experimental support is that controlling framing can backfire ([Legault et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797611427918), small student samples), and that voluntary training draws those already inclined ([Kulik et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1002/job.444)). **Inference:** this app sits on the voluntary side by design. It avoids the risk of controlling framing and accepts the cost Kulik describes, which is reach. Teaching as skill and service, without legal threat, fits that position.

**Evidence on harm.** Backfire is documented mainly in labs: normalizing stereotypes ([Duguid & Thomas-Hunt](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0037908)), false assurance that an organization is now fair ([Kaiser et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0030838)), and meritocracy framing ([Castilla & Benard 2010](https://doi.org/10.2189/asqu.2010.55.4.543)). One panel study suggests that training managers as interveners has a different sign from training employees as suspects ([Dobbin & Kalev 2019](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1818477116)). [Hammer et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0020927) shows that a supervisor course can help some employees and harm others beneath an average near zero. Disability simulations do not work on the data ([Flower et al. 2007](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/meta-analysis-of-disability-simulation-research/)). I found no controlled study of what these programs do to learners from affected groups, which is a real hole. **Inference:** the likeliest failure is no behavior change plus false reassurance, not active harm.

**Basis.** The decision backbone is my inference from four sources: the [National Frontline Supervisor Competencies](https://www.ancor.org/sites/default/files/national_frontline_supervisor_comp.pdf) for neutral first-line tasks, the [Council on Linkages tiers](https://phf.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Core_Competencies_Revisions_2021October_for_Council_Approval.pdf) for the verb gradient (contribute, assess, ensure), the [June 2025 CLAS Standards](https://thinkculturalhealth.hhs.gov/assets/pdfs/EnhancedNationalCLASStandards_Revised_June2025.pdf) for management accountability, and [MMB's "Leading with DEAI" competency](https://mn.gov/mmb/etd/core-programs/) as a description of state practice. Real programs use a short intensive and a 12-month arc for new supervisors ([Oregon ODHS](https://www.oregon.gov/odhs/data/cwdata/cw-apsr-2025-att-72.pdf), [Multnomah](https://multco.us/news/central-human-resource-officials-outline-new-manager-orientation-and-countywide-resources)), though both descriptions date from 2021 to 2022. DHS's own [Equity Policy](https://mn.gov/dhs/assets/equity-policy_tcm1053-646921.pdf) requires an equity analysis for long-term actions going to a supervisor or above for approval (copy undated), which is why that decision has a place in the space.

**Format, as design judgment and not a finding.** A reader's sitting joins three parts: self-paced lessons in short sittings (the new courses run 10 to 15 minutes a lesson), a retrieval check with private feedback, and one written if-then plan for a real upcoming situation. Live rehearsal is optional and self-organized, using `sm-facilitator-guide`, and it runs outside the app, which has no group tools and gains none. Short modules are justified by [spacing and retrieval evidence](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/which-study-strategies-make-the-grade.html), not by microlearning claims, because the only "meta-analysis" found for those is an [unverified vendor claim](https://www.arist.co/post/2025-meta-analysis-microlearning-improves-job-behavior-50-percent/blogrelixir). Branching scenarios have no rigorous pooled evidence as a stand-alone tool, so they supplement practice with other people, as [Sitzmann's simulation meta-analysis](https://karlkapp.com/?p=4333) suggests. Implementation intentions show strong general effects ([Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/entities/publication/2e749bfb-8533-437c-8203-7e788c910c5f)) but are untested for training transfer. No source gives an evidence-based dose for equity content.

**Design rules from the evidence, largely untested in the field.** Scenarios concern a supervisor's next decision. I recommend that staff from affected groups review them. A reader can report a scenario as harmful through the optional feedback route described below. Nobody speaks for a group. Content is described specifically in advance, and people can pause or take a different path without penalty or record. Reflection is private and not retained. Pair the prevalence of bias with a norm of effort, never imply the organization is fixed, and do not lead with legal threat. Run no disability simulations. Pay people who contribute lived experience. For accessibility, replace drag and drop with select or buttons, remove timers, manage focus and status messages in branches, and provide captions, audio description and [descriptive transcripts for Deaf and blind users](https://www.w3.org/WAI/media/av/). ASL on core videos and interpreting and CART in live sessions go beyond AA by local choice. Test with disabled staff, because compliance is not the same as a good experience.

## One public space, no sign-in, and no view of anyone's team

**Evidence on navigation.** [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/audience-based-navigation/) calls audience navigation usually problematic but acceptable when content is unique and sizable or the navigation is secondary, with "For ..." labels and no portals "that lock them in." [GOV.UK's testing](https://insidegovuk.blog.gov.uk/2014/07/18/hey-you-there-the-trouble-with-audience-based-navigation/) (2014) found people misread audience labels and skipped relevant content, so it labels by topic and marks individual items by audience. The [University of Minnesota](https://hr.umn.edu/Employee-Support/Supervisors) publishes an open supervisor section, and [Google](https://rework.withgoogle.com/intl/en/guides/building-manager-capability-at-scale-an-integrated-approach) publishes manager guidance openly. Those are precedents of practice, and whether the listed hubs have further gated content was not established. No US state agency hub was verified. **I found no controlled study comparing open and authenticated manager views, or measuring trust when staff can read manager guidance.** On monitoring, [Ravid et al. 2023](https://www.Gwern.net/doc/sociology/technology/2022-ravid.pdf) finds no performance gain (ρ = .00), more stress (ρ = .16), and better attitudes with transparency (ρ = .25). Only four studies concerned post-training outcomes, and its application to learning records is by analogy.

**Evidence on the app today.** There is no manager section. The live header is built from `PRIMARY_SECTIONS` in `lib/program/page-purpose.ts:13-15` (Start, Learn, Practice, Support, One DSD), and `PRIMARY_NAV` in `lib/constants.ts` is not rendered. `/one-dsd/leadership` is three clicks deep through the team page, absent from the section menu and the site map, and serves three audiences at once. The first-year course is reachable only by search or a collapsed stage, and the role filter is unusably broad: the audit estimates 292 of 381 DEIA courses qualify, and all 72 self-directed courses are tagged regardless of content (`lib/content/native-course-relevance.ts:11,17,99`, `lib/content/self-directed.ts:28`). The 94 Leader courses have no browse page. The app also says, "Choose by the work you are doing, not by your job title" (`app/learn/readings/pathways/page.tsx:11`).

**Recommendation (inference).** Separate the wayfinding, not the access. The space is a door for a role's decisions, open to all readers, and the page says so plainly, which is how it fits the app's stated philosophy.

## Measure privately and modestly, and say what it cannot show

**Evidence.** Reactions reflect course features and do not relate to delayed knowledge ([Sitzmann et al. 2008](https://iro.uiowa.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/A-review-and-meta-analysis-of-the/9984371260702771)), and self-assessed knowledge tracks motivation and satisfaction more than learning ([Sitzmann et al. 2010](https://iro.uiowa.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Self-Assessment-of-Knowledge-A-Cognitive-Learning/9984371090802771)). Anything judged on page counts or satisfaction would look successful regardless of its effect. [LTEM](https://www.thiagi.com/articles/2019/9/25/seven-applications-for-thalheimers-ltem-learning-transfer-evaluation-model) is a useful checklist with no empirical validation that I found. Power is the other limit: 92 departments could not reach conventional significance in the [Devine et al. hiring test](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jesp.2017.07.002), and 14,741 officers yielded no clear effect. Hammer shows that an average can hide opposite effects.

| What | How | Where it lives | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Private self-check | Knowledge checks and scenarios with immediate feedback, plus an if-then plan a reader can revisit at 4 to 8 weeks | The reader's browser. No saved answer, no score | Only the reader sees it |
| Reach | Anonymous, content-free page counts | No identity and no content. Whether the app counts pages today was not checked | Shows use, never effect. Never reported as success |
| Reader feedback | Optional, voluntary comments, including a report that a scenario is harmful. An email address or contact link is enough, with a reminder to leave out names | Held by the owner | Self-selected, and reactions do not track learning |

**This cannot show any effect on disparities, on decisions, or on any team's experience, and nobody should say it does.** A voluntary, unrecorded resource has no comparison group and no outcome data. That is the cost of the privacy promises, and it should be stated openly on `/leading/how-this-works`. Even a formal study would struggle: DSD alone could not prove effects on equity outcomes at its size.

**What the courses teach about measuring in a reader's own organization.** Readers run their own surveys and reports, so `sm-reading-patterns` and `sm-senior-leader-sessions` carry this as content. The app itself does none of it. The floor is 10 respondents for any staff-facing result, as in [FEVS](https://www.opm.gov/chcoc/transmittals/2024/2024%20OPM%20FEVS%20Memo%204-26-2024_0.pdf), and higher for identity cross-tabs. Use complementary suppression so a hidden group cannot be derived by subtraction ([vendor example](https://adoption.microsoft.com/files/viva/glint/ask-the-experts/Viva-Glint-Ask-the-Experts-series-Jun112024.pdf)), and apply the same rule to exports and filters across time. Report identity results at division level or above, or use consented listening, rather than lowering the floor. Ten is a defensible public-sector convention, not a proven safe number. **Inference:** this rules out individual supervisor scores, since many teams are under 10. The framework's survey item "My supervisor demonstrates inclusive and equitable leadership practices" (`lib/program/equity-framework.ts:580`) falls under this rule. The federal inclusion benchmark is unstable: [13 FEVS items were redacted in 2024](https://www.opm.gov/fevs/reports/technical-reports/technical-report/technical-report/2024/2024-technical-report.pdf), and a [July 2026 notice](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-07-02/html/2026-13443.htm) proposes further cuts. The New IQ concepts are one alternative, though the exact item wording and Minnesota's own survey rules were not retrieved. Other useful measures per the research, such as delayed scenario tests and behavior reports from a separate source per [Blume et al.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transfer_of_training) (secondary), belong to whoever runs them. On state records, [Minn. Stat. 13.43](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/13.43) makes "education and training background" and "work-related continuing education" public, and "all other personnel data" private. The app creates no such record.

**Upward feedback.** The strongest single mechanism here, feedback from a supervisor's own team, comes from Zohar & Polachek using 7 to 9 reports per supervisor, which is below the floor, and from a safety context. Coaching meta-analysis links multisource feedback to [smaller effects](https://research.aston.ac.uk/en/publications/the-effectiveness-of-workplace-coaching-a-meta-analysis-of-learni/). The app does not offer it, because a small team's answers can often be traced to a person. A supervisor can still ask their own team directly, and the courses teach how to take feedback across power.

## The law, the federal position and a live lawsuit, as dated context

Everything here is context for a voluntary, independent resource, dated as of October 2026. None of it holds any material back. The courses state these facts with their dates and labels.

**The state's duty (primary text read).** 43A.21 subd. 3(b) requires all managers and supervisors to be trained on inclusive work environments, disability awareness, cultural competence and other equity and diversity areas, and permanent status in those jobs depends on meeting training requirements. Subd. 2(a) requires annual training for all employees on ADA Title II, the affirmative action policy, equal opportunity and digital accessibility. Subd. 6 and subd. 3(c) impose accessibility duties on training and an annual self-assessment. That is the state's to meet, through its own training and its own records. This app is separate and does not count toward it. [Policies 1436](https://mn.gov/mmb-stat/policies/1436-harassmentdiscriminationprohibited.pdf) and [1329](https://mn.gov/mmb-stat/policies/1329-sexualharassmentprohibited.pdf) require supervisors to "immediately report." The [MHRA text search](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/363A/full) finds no duty on employers to train. The commonly repeated premise of "2023 amendments to the harassment standard" does not hold: the actual change is [2024 c. 105 s. 3](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/laws/2024/0/Session+Law/Chapter/105/). "Severe or pervasive" is case law, per [*Kenneh*](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/mn-supreme-court/2068776.html). [EO 19-01 was rescinded by EO 23-12](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/archive/execorders/23-12.pdf), and because orders [expire 90 days after a governor leaves office](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/4.035), EO 23-12 could lapse around April 2027. Accommodation guidance worth reading sits in the [EEOC guidance](https://www.eeoc.gov/laws/guidance/enforcement-guidance-reasonable-accommodation-and-undue-hardship-under-ada), [363A](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/363A/full) and [MAPE Art. 31](https://mape.org/sites/default/files/files/MAPE-2025-2027-Contract_1.pdf).

**Enforcement position, not law.** The [EEOC's March 2025 technical assistance](https://www.eeoc.gov/wysk/what-you-should-know-about-dei-related-discrimination-work), still posted but not refreshed, says training can create a hostile environment through "content, application, or context." It also treats mentoring and training access as terms of employment and warns that separating employees by protected characteristic can be unlawful even with identical programming. The [DOJ memo in OMB M-25-33](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/M-25-33-Eliminating-Funding-of-Unlawful-Discrimination.pdf) targets programs that "stereotype, exclude, or disadvantage," warns against requiring people to affirm positions or "confess" biases or privileges and against penalizing dissent, and acknowledges harassment-prevention training that does not single out groups as lawful. Case law is splitting on facts, all from secondary reports: [*Young*](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/tenth-circuit-again-rejects-employee-3706122/) (10th Cir.) rejected a claim based on a single training, while [*Diemert*](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/10/ninth-circuit-holds-dei-training-may-support-a-hostile-work-environment-claim) (9th Cir., Sept. 28, 2026) sent a **human services** employee's claim to trial on training plus about ten incidents over five years. Neither binds Minnesota, and the *Diemert* opinion was not read. **Inference:** risk accumulates, so how managers behave after training counts as much as the content. Certifications [vacated in *RICADV*](https://litigationtracker.law.georgetown.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Rhode-Island-Coalition-Against-Domestic-Violence_2026.08.14_OPINION.pdf) may still appear in executed state agreements, which is the state's matter.

**Supervisors' routine decisions.** [*Ames*](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/24pdf/23-1039_c0n2.pdf) sets one standard for all employees, and [*Muldrow*](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/22-193) requires only "some harm" in a transfer. **Inference:** assignments, schedule changes, stretch work and nominations deserve consistent criteria and records, and the mirror-image scenario pairs in `sm-fair-assignments` teach this accurately. [HHS removed its Title VI disparate-impact provisions on July 24, 2026](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-15000.pdf), but Title VII's provision and [MHRA's "unreasonably excludes" text](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/363A.08) remain, and an [Office of Legal Counsel (OLC) opinion binding federal agencies, not courts](https://www.bhfs.com/insight/doj-opines-that-eeocs-title-vii-disparate-impact-framework-is-unconstitutional/), is secondary only. The courses teach neither "disparate impact is dead" nor "it is enforced as before." Olmstead needs similar care: [OLC disclaims an integration mandate](https://www.justice.gov/olc/media/1446701/dl), which binds the executive branch only, DOJ [reportedly withdrew its 2011 guidance](https://dredf.org/community-living-is-a-civil-right-2026/) (advocacy source), and Minnesota's [September 2026 Plan](https://mn.gov/olmstead/assets/Minnesota%20Olmstead%20Plan%20-%20September%202026_tcm1143-768001.pdf) continues, though its adoption is unconfirmed. Language access is taught as a program-access duty based on statutes, not on [revoked EO 13166](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/IF/HTML/IF13076.html). The MAPE contract's [Art. 28](https://mape.org/sites/default/files/files/MAPE-2025-2027-Contract_1.pdf) sets notice for work rules, and the first-week and corrective action courses teach this.

**The suit, and the DHS plan as separate context.** [*United States v. Minnesota*](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/united-states-department-justice-files-lawsuit-against-minnesotas-affirmative-action-regime) challenges the state's affirmative action system. The [docket summary](https://clearinghouse.net/case/47712/) (secondary) lists DHS's Hiring Justification Policy as Exhibit B, a preservation order in September 2026, and dispositive motions by July 15, 2027. The [DHS Affirmative Action Plan 2024-2026](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2025/mandated/250358.pdf), whose period ended June 30, 2026, says DHS will assess managers at hire for "cultural competency, authenticity, courage, and will," that all must complete the Intercultural Development Inventory, and that "Completion of all training will be tracked." It also refers to "acknowledging systemic racism." A 2026-2028 plan was not found. Any change follows the [43A.191](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/43A.191) commissioner-approval path. That plan and this voluntary app are separate. The plan belongs to DHS, the app's promises belong to the app, and neither governs the other. The courses describe the plan with its dates and tell the reader to ask Human Resources what applies now.

**Evidence and law pull apart in four places, and readers should know both sides.** Same-group mentoring has randomized support among students ([Dennehy & Dasgupta](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1613117114)), and same-race managers correlate with better outcomes ([Giuliano et al.](https://doi.org/10.1353/jhr.2011.0022)), yet the EEOC text and *Ames* make these a poor basis for an assignment rule. Explicit diversity signaling in recruitment worked in a [randomized study](https://doi.org/10.3368/jhr.56.1.0518-9489r1), and the hiring course discusses recruitment messaging with its dated legal context. The research suggests optional "same-experience" space for supervisors from affected groups, while the EEOC text warns against separating employees by protected characteristic. The app has no group tools, and the live session guides call for no identity-based breakouts, so anyone organizing such a space does it on their own footing. The framework's "mentorship and sponsorship for... employees from historically marginalized groups" (`lib/program/equity-framework.ts:421`) and NFSC 11's language on bilingual and bicultural staff sit in the same tension, given the EEOC's position that customer preference is no defense.

**Framing (inference).** Frame the app as a voluntary place to prepare for hard decisions, with conduct owed to everyone. Use direct equity language where it is the accurate language. Describe "cultural competence" and "equity" in behavior as well as in words, and teach behavior rather than belief. No identity-based breakouts, no required disclosure, and disagreement carries no consequence. These follow from the evidence on framing and from the fact that the sessions are voluntary. [OPM's post-DEI language](https://www.opm.gov/chcoc/transmittals/2025/OPM%20SES%20Hiring%20Memo%205-29-2025%20FINAL.pdf) shows fairness skills can be written without equity vocabulary, though that framework has no equity content, and removing the words does not remove the state's statutory ones. [WCAG 2.1 AA applies now under MNIT policy](https://mn.gov/mnit/media/blog/?id=38-610208) (secondary; [16E.03](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/16E.03) still names 2.0) to state systems, and the [federal April 26, 2027 date](https://www.ada.gov/resources/2024-03-08-web-rule/) applies to public entities. Whichever applies to an independent site, build to WCAG 2.2 AA where feasible, because the readers need it.

The watch list holds the [UGESP rescission](https://natlawreview.com/article/eeoc-sends-oira-final-rule-rescinding-uniform-guidelines-employee-selection), the Seventh Circuit in *CWIT*, cert in *Young*, *De Piero*, reissued HHS conditions, final SAM.gov certifications, and rulings in the Minnesota suit. Also watch the January 2027 change of governor and the 2027 legislative session. Absence of a found ruling in the research is not proof that none exists, so re-date `sm-rules-fall-2026` and check each docket when you next update it.

## My candid view: the space is an easy yes, and the curriculum is a qualified yes

**The space is worth doing, and "dividing" is the wrong idea.** A real division would add a login, a new data class, staff suspicion and a database migration, in exchange for protecting content that is already public. Labeling, though, costs little and fixes a real problem: supervisors must search a flat list of 694 courses or expand a collapsed stage to find what they need. My confidence is moderate, because it rests on general usability guidance, precedent and an analogy from monitoring research, with no direct study. It will also not change behavior on its own. It helps someone who has already decided to look.

**The curriculum is justified, but not for the reason usually given.** I would not claim it reduces disparities, and I would not let anyone report page counts as progress. The evidence best supports better handling of specific duties and perhaps better retention. The strongest reason is that supervisors face accommodation requests, harm reports, ratings, hiring panels and being named in complaints with thin practical support, and this gives them a private place to prepare. A reasonable person could argue, as the UK's [Behavioural Insights Team](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5fd8c859e90e071be5f30531/20-12-14_UBT_BIT_report.pdf) comes close to arguing, that money should move from training to recruitment and progression processes. I take that seriously, and it is why the curriculum teaches tools. If DSD lacks structured interviews or review criteria, teaching their use has limits. The research does not establish whether they exist, so the courses tell the reader to ask Human Resources.

**The largest weakness is that the best-supported levers are the ones the app has promised not to use.** Reinforcement through appraisal, trained superiors watching follow-through, upward feedback, and required attendance all have some support, and the first, third and fourth are ruled out or restricted by the app's commitments and its voluntary nature. I still recommend keeping the promises, because they are specific, repeated, code-enforced and a large part of whatever trust the app holds, and because monitoring evidence points the same way. The price is real: an open, voluntary, self-paced app mostly reaches people already interested, the same people Chang's 27% consent rate and Kulik's work point to. Accountability cannot come from this app. If someone wants it, it sits in DHS's own approvals and HR processes, which are DHS's to run and separate from this work. Without that, this is a good library and a private place to prepare, and that is a fair and useful thing for it to be.

**The plan is weak in specific places, and readers should discount them.** The hours are arithmetic, not a finding. The experienced and manager paths have few or no public models, and the first-line backbone comes from competencies written for supervisors of direct support professionals. The research does not show how many DSD supervisors do that work, so NFSC may fit as task language more than as an audience match. `sm-fair-oversight` is urgent and has no benchmark, and the [providers' dispute](https://www.kttc.com/2026/06/04/multiple-rochester-providers-voice-concerns-after-wrongful-loss-dhs-funding/) is one-sided so far, with no data found on disparate effects by community. `sm-deaf-deafblind-hoh` lacks a good workplace source, since only [general material](https://learn.deaftec.org/?p=18394) and a [small study](https://nsuworks.nova.edu/jadara/vol53/iss2/2) surfaced in the benchmark research, and it was not written with Deaf or DeafBlind people. `sm-who-carries-equity-work` is extrapolated largely from [higher education research](https://read.nxtbook.com/acuhoi/talking_stick/may_2024/first_person.html). The content on politically diverse teams is untested inference. Person-centered supervision as a parallel process rests on my reading of a single [Rutgers session description](https://media.rwjms.rutgers.edu/Mediasite/Play/8b64811048de48948bd7cc1e4f1baaab1d/Info), so I left it out. Published program details are often old and may not reflect current practice, and I found no outcome evaluation of any comparable supervisor program.

**The content base is also less solid than "600-plus" sounds.** Most of it lacks a recorded independent review in the copy examined, and a repeated reporting sentence tied to Policy 1436 accounts for much of the 261 course sources that mention affirmative action, so a policy change could touch hundreds of files. Everything goes in by the owner's decision. Readers cannot tell which courses were checked, so the most useful next review is of the 34 main starting courses and the 19 new ones.

**Timing is awkward, and waiting would not help.** A gubernatorial election falls on [November 3, 2026](https://www.kttc.com/2026/01/06/timeline-leading-up-minnesotas-2026-gubernatorial-election/), the suit runs through mid-2027, and the 2027 legislative session could change the statute itself, which its [page history](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/43A.21) shows as unamended since 2023. Dated content handles this. Say what is true as of when, and change the date when you check again.

**What would change my mind.** If readers tell you the doors do not match their decisions, redo the labels. If a course turns out to be wrong on a fact, fix it openly with a date. If you ever wanted assigned groups, a signed-in design would need the staff-facing promises rewritten honestly first, and I would still advise against it.

## Conclusion

The two ideas look like one project and are not. The space is a small interface decision that can ship soon and do modest good. The curriculum is finished draft material that a person can use in private, and whatever it changes will depend on what that person does next. Treating the first as proof of the second is the most tempting mistake available, since a tidy new section with a progress bar would look like progress without changing a decision.

The more durable point is that the app's privacy promises and the state's training duty are both real and live in different places. The state's systems carry the state's requirement. The app stays what the owner has built it to be: voluntary, public, self-contained, and a place where a supervisor can practice without being watched. It will never report on anyone, it promises no change in disparities, and it is worth having for the person sitting down the night before a hard conversation.
