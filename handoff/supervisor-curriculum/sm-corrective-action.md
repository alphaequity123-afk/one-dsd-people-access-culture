# sm-corrective-action: Corrective Action and Labor Agreements

**Subtitle:** How to prepare for a performance or conduct problem, what to do before discipline, what the contract requires, and when to call someone.
**Duration:** About 60 minutes across five lessons. Take them in short sittings.
**Content type:** formal-support
**Status:** reviewed
**Last reviewed:** October 31, 2026
**Evidence date:** October 2026
**Scope note:** This is general information as of October 2026, not legal advice. This course is voluntary and does not count as the training Minn. Stat. 43A.21 requires; the state's own training does that.

**Course objectives:**
- Tell a coaching conversation apart from discipline, and choose the right one.
- Check consistency before you recommend corrective action.
- Follow the MAPE contract's steps on representation, notice and records, and find the equivalent in other agreements.
- Keep notes that are accurate and limited to the work.
- Recognize when timing, protected activity or a health issue means you should pause and call.

**Applied next step:** Before your next difficult performance conversation, call your labor relations contact and introduce yourself. Ask how DHS handles corrective action, who holds the authority to impose discipline, and what they would like to see from you early. It is much easier to have that call before you need it.

## Lesson 1: Before anything is discipline

**Summary:** Most problems should never reach discipline. This lesson teaches you to clarify expectations, find the cause and raise minor issues privately.
**Minutes:** 12
**Objectives:**
- Check that the expectation was clear, known and applied to everyone.
- Raise initial minor issues privately, as the MAPE contract requires.
- Ask about barriers without asking about health.
- Decide when to call labor relations or Human Resources.

**Takeaways:**
- Check the expectation before you check the person.
- First minor issues are raised privately and, if corrected, stay out of the file.
- A question about what is getting in the way can reveal a possible accommodation request.

### Text: Start with the expectation

A case consultant has missed several documentation deadlines. Before you have that conversation, ask yourself some questions. Is the expectation written down? Did you ever tell this person? Do others on the team meet it? Do you enforce it for everyone? Has the workload changed? Do they have what they need to do it?

You will often find that part of the problem sits on your side. That is useful to know, and it does not make the problem go away. Fair corrective action starts with a fair expectation.

Article 8, Section 1 of the MAPE 2025-2027 contract says discipline may be imposed only for just cause and "shall be corrective where appropriate." Corrective is the key word. The purpose is for the problem to stop and the person to succeed.

### Tabs: What sits below discipline under the MAPE contract

**Tab: Private first**
Article 8, Section 8: "Initial minor infractions, irregularities, or deficiencies shall first be privately brought to the attention of the employee and, if corrected, shall not be entered into the employee's personnel file."

**Tab: Coaching and work plans**
Article 6, Section 3 mentions work plans, coaching sessions and letters of expectation, and it says these are not substitutes for the annual appraisal. Use them to help, and still do the appraisal.

**Tab: Letter of expectation**
Article 8, Section 8 says a letter of expectation "is not discipline." On the employee's written request, it shall be removed from the personnel file if the employee has performed satisfactorily for six months from its date. Ask Human Resources before you write one, and how DHS uses them.

**Tab: Probation**
Employees on probation are in a different position. Article 16, Section 6 says that wherever practicable they get a performance counseling review at about the midpoint, and it covers non-certification and extensions. Talk to Human Resources early if you are concerned about someone on probation.

**Tab: Other agreements**
This course read only the MAPE contract. If the person is covered by AFSCME, MMA, the Managerial Plan or anything else, read that document. Do not assume these terms apply.

### List: The first conversation

- Hold it privately, and soon after the problem.
- Describe what you saw, with dates and the effect on the work or on people served.
- Restate the expectation.
- Ask: "What's getting in the way?" Then listen.
- Agree on what will happen next, and when you will check in.
- Write a short note of what was said, with the date. Ask Human Resources where to keep it.
- Follow up when you said you would, including when things improve.

### Text: When a barrier turns up

If they say anything suggesting a health or access issue, treat it as a possible accommodation request. For example: "I can't keep up since my medication changed." Under HR/LR Policy 1433, when a supervisor sees or hears that an employee is having difficulty doing the job because of a medical condition, further inquiry may be required, and the supervisor should consult the ADA coordinator.

The expectation does not vanish when this happens. But consult the coordinator before you go further with corrective steps. You do not need to be sure it is a disability. That is not your decision to make.

If the barrier involves harassment or discrimination, the reporting duty applies: report immediately to Human Resources or the Affirmative Action Officer.

### Leader move: Who gets the quiet word

**Control:** You control who gets an informal heads-up and who goes straight to a formal step.
**Failure:** Do not give one person several friendly warnings and another person a written record for the same thing.
**Next:** Keep your own process the same. For any first-time issue, use the same private conversation for everyone, and note it the same way.

### Scenario

**Context:** A new supervisor notices that a program consultant has arrived late to three provider calls this month. Providers have complained once. The consultant has been on the team for a year, and you do not recall that anyone ever said that calls had to start on time. You know that another consultant often joins late as well, but no one has complained about them.
**Prompt:** What do you do next?

**Option:** Send a written warning to the first consultant, since a provider complained and you need a record.
**Response:** A written step may not fit a first, minor issue that nobody has raised with them. It also leaves the other consultant's lateness unaddressed. If a formal step is ever needed, talk to labor relations first.

**Option (recommended):** Speak with both, privately and separately. Restate the expectation for provider calls, ask what is getting in the way, and agree on a check-in. Make a brief note of each conversation.
**Response:** This is the strongest move. It treats the same behavior the same way, and it raises the issue privately, as the contract says for initial minor issues. It also leaves room for a barrier you do not know about.

**Option:** Remind the whole team in a meeting that calls start on time, and do nothing more.
**Response:** A team reminder can help set the expectation, and you may want to send one. But it does not deal with the specific issue, and it can embarrass people without saying anything direct.

**Option:** Ask around to find out if other providers have had concerns.
**Response:** That could look like building a case, and it spreads the concern to others before you have spoken with the person. Start with the conversation.

### Knowledge check

**Question:** Under the MAPE contract, what happens to an initial minor issue that is raised privately and corrected?
**Options:**
- It goes in the personnel file for 18 months.
- It becomes an oral reprimand.
- It shall not be entered into the personnel file. (correct)
- It must be reported to the union.

**Correct answer:** It shall not be entered into the personnel file.
**Feedback if correct:** Correct, under Article 8, Section 8. Ask Human Resources how to keep your own notes.
**Feedback if incorrect:** Check Article 8, Section 8. Initial minor issues are privately raised and, if corrected, are not entered into the file.

### Transfer prompt

**Prompt:** Think about a work problem you may need to raise. Do not write a name. "If I need to raise [the problem], then first I will check [was the expectation clear, and is it applied to everyone], then have a private conversation by [date], and ask what is getting in the way."
**Plan starters:**
- Is the expectation written down anywhere?
- Is anyone else doing the same thing?
- Which agreement covers this person?

## Lesson 2: What discipline is, and who decides

**Summary:** When coaching has not worked or the conduct is serious, formal steps may apply. This lesson explains the MAPE contract's framework and your own role in preparing it.
**Minutes:** 13
**Objectives:**
- List the forms of discipline in the MAPE contract.
- Explain what "just cause" means for your preparation, and whom to ask about it.
- Describe the notice and Loudermill requirements.
- Prepare a clear summary for labor relations or Human Resources.

**Takeaways:**
- The contract lists the forms of discipline, not necessarily in order.
- Call early. Do not make the decision alone.
- A reprimand should not embarrass anyone.

### Tabs: Article 8 of the MAPE 2025-2027 contract

**Tab: Forms of discipline**
Section 3: "Discipline includes only the following, but not necessarily in this order": oral reprimand (not grievable), written reprimand, suspension (paid or unpaid), suspension by reducing the vacation balance, demotion, discharge.

**Tab: How reprimands are given**
Section 3: a reprimand must be done in a way that "will not embarrass the employee before other employees, supervisors, or the public." An oral reprimand must be identified as such to the employee. So a hard talk that you meant as a reprimand counts as one only if you say so.

**Tab: Written notice**
Section 3: for action more severe than an oral reprimand, the employee and the union are notified in writing of the specific reasons, before or at the time of action.

**Tab: Loudermill meeting**
Section 9: if the intent is to suspend or discharge, the employee is first told they may ask for a chance to hear the evidence against them and present their side, with union representation. The right expires at the end of the employee's next scheduled work day after notice, unless agreed otherwise. Discipline does not take effect during that period.

**Tab: Investigatory leave**
Section 4: the employee may be placed on paid leave if a reasonable basis exists, with written notice of the reasons to the employee and union as soon as practicable, and an agency contact person named. If leave passes 30 days, the employee is told why and given the status of the investigation, in accordance with the Data Practices Act.

**Tab: Discharge**
Section 5: not without just cause, with written notice and reasons to the employee and the union, and a meeting right. The union can take a discharge up at Step 2 of the grievance procedure.

### Text: Your part

The contract says the "Appointing Authority" acts. Ask Human Resources who holds that authority at DHS, and what supervisors can and cannot do on their own. You might be expected to give an oral reprimand, or only to recommend. Do not guess.

The contract uses the term "just cause" without spelling out in Article 8 what it requires. Labor relations can tell you how they and arbitrators look at it. In the meantime, these questions are simply good preparation, and they are the ones a fair-minded reader would ask:

- Was the expectation clear and known to the employee?
- Did the person actually do what you think they did? How do you know?
- Did the person get a chance to explain?
- Have others in similar situations been treated the same way?
- Does the proposed step fit what happened and the person's record?
- Will this step actually help correct it?

These are questions for your preparation. They are not a legal test.

### List: What to bring when you call

- What happened, with dates, in plain facts.
- The expectation, and how the person knew about it.
- Conversations you have already had, and when.
- Effects on the work, or on people served.
- What you think the person's explanation is, if you know.
- Whether you are aware of similar situations with anyone else.
- Whether anything recently occurred that could matter, such as a request, a report, a grievance or a leave.
- Which agreement covers them.

### Text: Why consistency sits at the center

Discipline is where inconsistency does the most damage. Seattle's 2012 employee survey asked whether supervisors held everyone to the same disciplinary standards, which shows employers recognize the issue, though its figures could not be verified for this course. Federal leadership criteria describe "swift action" on deficiencies. Speed has value, but it needs balance: a fast step for one person and a slow one for another is how inequity shows up on the record.

*Ames* confirms that the same standard applies to all employees. Article 4 of the MAPE contract pledges that it applies without discrimination, and Article 28 requires work rules to be applied and enforced without discrimination. You will rarely see other supervisors' discipline. Labor relations and Human Resources can see more. Ask them directly: "How have similar situations been handled?"

Research on fair discipline in public human services is thin. So this course offers careful practice and makes no claim about outcomes.

### Leader move: The early call

**Control:** You control when labor relations first hears about this.
**Failure:** Do not call after you have told the person what will happen. Do not tell an employee "this will be a suspension" before anyone with the authority has decided.
**Next:** Call when you first think a formal step may be needed. Say: "I'm not asking you to take this on. I want to prepare it properly."

### Scenario

**Context:** A policy specialist forwarded an internal draft with a county's confidential data to an outside group by mistake. It was a serious error. It was also a first error in six years of strong work. Your manager says, "We need to show we take this seriously. Write them up today." You recall hearing that a similar mistake on another team two years ago was handled with a conversation, but you do not know the details.
**Prompt:** What do you do next?

**Option:** Write the reprimand today, as your manager says. They have more experience, and data errors are serious.
**Response:** Your manager may turn out to be right about the seriousness, but this skips the notice requirements and the facts. It also ignores what you heard about a similar case. Your manager's urgency is a reason to call, not to act alone.

**Option:** Tell your manager this is too harsh, and handle it with a quiet conversation.
**Response:** You may want to argue for that, but you are also skipping facts. Data errors may also bring separate duties you are not aware of. Decide with the people who know the process.

**Option (recommended):** Call labor relations and Human Resources today with the facts. Ask about any data-related duties, how similar situations were handled, and what questioning steps apply. Update your manager on what you learned, and when a decision can be made.
**Response:** This is the strongest move. It takes the error seriously, checks consistency and gets the contract steps right. It gives your manager something real, which is what "seriously" should mean.

### Knowledge check

**Question:** Under the MAPE contract, when must written notice of specific reasons go to the employee and the union?
**Options:**
- For any coaching conversation.
- For any disciplinary action more severe than an oral reprimand, before or at the time it is taken. (correct)
- Only for discharge.
- Within 90 days after discipline is taken.

**Correct answer:** For any disciplinary action more severe than an oral reprimand, before or at the time it is taken.
**Feedback if correct:** Correct, under Article 8, Section 3.
**Feedback if incorrect:** Check Article 8, Section 3. Any action more severe than an oral reprimand needs written notice of the specific reasons to the employee and the union, before or at the time of the action.

### Transfer prompt

**Prompt:** "If I start to think formal action may be needed for [type of issue], then I will call [labor relations contact] before I say anything to the employee about consequences, and bring [my dated facts, the expectation and similar cases I know of]."
**Plan starters:**
- Ask who holds the authority for each form of discipline.
- Ask whether supervisors may give oral reprimands at DHS.
- Save the contact for labor relations.

## Lesson 3: The conversation that may lead to discipline

**Summary:** One sentence in the contract changes how you start a certain kind of meeting. This lesson shows you how to spot an investigatory conversation, and what to do before you ask questions.
**Minutes:** 11
**Objectives:**
- Identify a meeting that falls under Article 8, Section 2.
- Advise the employee and offer representation, as required.
- Work respectfully with a steward in the room.
- Route harassment and discrimination matters away from your own questioning.

**Takeaways:**
- Questioning that may lead to discipline requires notice and an offer of representation.
- A waiver must be in writing, before questioning.
- If unsure, pause and call.

### Text: The sentence that matters

Article 8, Section 2 of the MAPE 2025-2027 contract says the employer shall not meet with an employee "for the purpose of questioning the employee during an investigation that may lead to discipline of that employee without first advising the employee of the nature of the investigation and offering the employee an opportunity for Association representation." Waiving that right has to be done in writing before questioning. The employer also notifies the subject when the investigation is closed, and notifies the union if the subject had representation.

Note what this covers. It is about questioning someone during an investigation that may lead to their own discipline. Ordinary work talk does not trigger this. "How's that file going?" is not an investigation. The hard part is that a conversation can change in the middle.

### Accordion: Where is the line?

**Item: A regular one-to-one that shifts**
You ask about a missed deadline, and the person says something that makes you think they falsified a date. Stop. Say: "I need to pause this conversation. I'll come back to you." Then call labor relations.

**Item: "Just a quick chat first"**
You have already heard a serious allegation and want to get their side informally before involving anyone. That is the situation this section addresses. Call first.

**Item: Coaching on a known problem**
You are telling someone what you expect, and you are not gathering facts about whether they did something for possible discipline. This is usually a coaching conversation. Be clear on which you are doing, and ask labor relations if you are not sure.

**Item: Someone else is the subject**
You are talking to a witness about someone else's conduct. Section 2 addresses questioning about that employee's own potential discipline. Do not start your own inquiry, though. Ask Human Resources who should be doing the questioning.

### Text: Who should be asking

For reports of harassment or discrimination, your role is reporting. You do not investigate under Policies 1436 and 1329. Human Resources or another designated person takes this on. For other issues, ask who does the fact-finding. It may be you, Human Resources, or someone else.

Representatives have a role in the meeting, and they are not your opponents. Under the contract, stewards get reasonable time without loss of pay to attend investigatory interviews and Loudermill meetings. So when someone wants a steward, schedule around it, and do not make them feel it counts against them. Article 4, Section 3 forbids the employer from interfering with or coercing an employee over the right to join or not join the union or take part in an official capacity. Ask labor relations if you are unsure about a steward's role in a meeting.

### List: Before a meeting that may lead to discipline

- Call labor relations, and ask if this meeting falls under Section 2 or the equivalent in another agreement.
- Agree on who will ask the questions and who will take notes.
- Tell the employee the nature of the investigation.
- Offer representation. If they waive, get it in writing before you start.
- Ask open questions. Listen. Do not argue.
- Do not state a conclusion or a consequence.
- Tell them what comes next, including that they will be notified when the investigation is closed.
- Make the meeting accessible. If the person uses an interpreter or another accommodation, arrange it first. This kind of meeting deserves a qualified interpreter, and a coworker who happens to sign is not a fair substitute.

### Leader move: Pausing mid-conversation

**Control:** You control whether a conversation keeps going once it becomes something else.
**Failure:** Do not keep asking because you are curious and they are talking. Do not say "this is off the record." You cannot make that promise.
**Next:** Practice this sentence: "I want to stop here so that we do this properly. I'll get back to you by [time], and I'll explain what happens next."

### Scenario

**Context:** A provider tells you that a case consultant on your team asked them for a personal favor in return for speeding up paperwork. You are shocked. It does not sound like this person. They are in your office for a regular one-to-one in ten minutes. You think that if you could just ask them casually, this might be cleared up quickly.
**Prompt:** What do you do next?

**Option:** Ask casually during the one-to-one, so it does not become a big deal if it is a misunderstanding.
**Response:** That may feel kind, but you would be questioning someone about something that may lead to their discipline without notice or an offer of representation. It could harm them and the review.

**Option:** Cancel all your one-to-ones with this person until it is resolved.
**Response:** Going silent can harm them, and it might signal your concern to the team. You can hold ordinary one-to-ones on ordinary work, once you have taken advice.

**Option (recommended):** Hold the one-to-one on regular work, or move it if you cannot do that honestly. Call labor relations and Human Resources straight after, or before, with the provider's account. Write down what the provider said, and do not raise it until you are advised.
**Response:** This is the strongest move. It gives the people who handle this a clean start, and it protects the consultant's rights as well as the provider's report. Ask also if the report triggers any other duties.

**Option:** Ask a trusted colleague on the team whether they have noticed anything.
**Response:** That spreads an unverified allegation, may breach privacy and could shape what witnesses later say.

### Knowledge check

**Question:** Under Article 8, Section 2, what must happen before you question an employee in an investigation that may lead to their discipline?
**Options:**
- The employee is advised of the nature of the investigation and offered union representation. (correct)
- The union president signs a form.
- The employee receives a written reprimand.
- Nothing, as long as the meeting is informal.

**Correct answer:** The employee is advised of the nature of the investigation and offered union representation.
**Feedback if correct:** Correct. Any waiver must be in writing before questioning. Check the equivalent in other agreements.
**Feedback if incorrect:** Check Article 8, Section 2. Being informal does not change the requirement. Advise the employee of the nature of the investigation, and offer representation.

### Transfer prompt

**Prompt:** "If a regular conversation turns into something that may lead to discipline, then I will say [my pause sentence], stop asking questions, and call [labor relations contact] the same day."
**Plan starters:**
- Write your pause sentence and say it aloud.
- Ask labor relations what the equivalent rule is for staff who are not covered by MAPE.
- Ask who does fact-finding in your division.

## Lesson 4: What goes in the record

**Summary:** What you write can follow someone for years, and the contract limits what goes in the personnel file. This lesson explains those limits and how to write notes that are fair.
**Minutes:** 11
**Objectives:**
- State what the MAPE contract keeps out of the personnel file.
- Explain when discipline can be removed.
- Keep notes that are factual, limited and consistent.
- Know what is public and what is private under the Data Practices Act.

**Takeaways:**
- Oral reprimands and investigations without discipline stay out of the file.
- Discipline eligible for removal is not a basis for later discipline.
- Do not keep a shadow file. Ask where your notes belong.

### Tabs: Article 8, Section 8 of the MAPE 2025-2027 contract

**Tab: Stays out of the file**
Corrected initial minor issues. Oral reprimands. Investigations that do not result in disciplinary action. Documentation of wage garnishment.

**Tab: Must be in a disciplinary entry**
All disciplinary entries except discharge "shall state the corrective action expected of the employee." So a written reprimand has to say what you want the person to do.

**Tab: Removal on written request**
A letter of expectation: after six months of satisfactory performance. A written reprimand: if no further discipline for 18 months. A written record of a suspension of 10 days or less: if no further discipline for three years from the beginning date of the suspension.

**Tab: After removal**
Discipline that becomes eligible for removal under these terms "shall not be used as a basis for any subsequent discipline of the employee."

**Tab: Access**
The employee can see the file on request, can get copies of evaluative and disciplinary entries on written request, and can have a written response included. A steward can see it on the employee's written request.

**Tab: Grievance files**
Article 9, Section 2 says grievance files are kept separate from official personnel files.

### Text: Your own notes

You will keep some notes. You need them to remember what you said and when. Be careful, though. A folder of notes on a person, containing things the contract says should not be in the personnel file, can look like a shadow file. Ask Human Resources how supervisors at DHS should keep working notes, for how long and when to discard or keep them. If someone has told you to preserve records, do not delete anything without asking.

Notes held by an agency can be government data under the Minnesota Government Data Practices Act. Under Minn. Stat. 13.43, some personnel data is public, including the existence and status of complaints and the final disposition of discipline with reasons. All other personnel data is private, and access within the agency is limited to those whose work assignments reasonably require it (Minn. R. 1205.0400). So do not discuss someone's discipline with coworkers. If a coworker asks, you can say, "I handle individual matters privately, for everyone." If anyone asks you for data, send them to Human Resources.

### List: A fair note

- Date, time, who was there.
- What was seen or said, in plain facts and in quotes where you can.
- The expectation discussed, and next steps agreed.
- No guesses about motive, diagnosis, family or character.
- No labels like "attitude problem" or "difficult."
- No reference to union activity, a complaint or a leave as if it were part of the problem.

Now run a swap check. Would you write the same note, in the same words, about someone else who did the same thing?

### Leader move: The reasonable reader

**Control:** You control how your notes read to someone outside the situation.
**Failure:** Do not write a note in anger, and do not go back later to add details as if they were written at the time.
**Next:** Reread your last three notes as if you were the employee, a steward or a judge. Rewrite anything you would be embarrassed to read aloud, as a new dated note. Do not alter the old one. Ask Human Resources how to handle it if you find something that should not be there.

### Scenario

**Context:** You took over a team last month. The previous supervisor left you a folder on a program consultant. It includes a 2-year-old written reprimand, notes about a closed investigation that led to no action, a note that the consultant "seems to have health stuff going on," and some emails from coworkers. The consultant has had no further discipline, and you are about to have a conversation about a new performance issue.
**Prompt:** What do you do next?

**Option (recommended):** Do not use the folder in your conversation. Take the folder to Human Resources or labor relations, and ask how it should be handled. Base the new conversation on the new issue.
**Response:** This is the strongest move. The folder includes things the contract keeps out of the personnel file, plus health speculation that should not be in a supervisor's notes, and a reprimand that may be eligible for removal. Deal with the current issue on its own facts.

**Option:** Read it carefully to prepare, since context will help you be fair.
**Response:** Once you read it, the closed investigation and the health note may color your view. Ask Human Resources first what you should be using. Your own assessment should rest on your own observations and on the official record.

**Option:** Shred it. It should never have existed.
**Response:** You may be right that some of it should not exist, but destroying records on your own could create a different problem. Records may have retention rules or preservation duties. Ask first.

### Knowledge check

**Question:** Under the MAPE contract, a written reprimand is removed from the personnel file on the employee's written request if:
**Options:**
- Six months have passed.
- No further disciplinary action has been taken for 18 months following its date. (correct)
- The supervisor changes.
- The employee completes a training course.

**Correct answer:** No further disciplinary action has been taken for 18 months following its date.
**Feedback if correct:** Correct, under Article 8, Section 8. Discipline eligible for removal cannot be used as a basis for later discipline.
**Feedback if incorrect:** Check Article 8, Section 8. Six months applies to a letter of expectation. A written reprimand takes 18 months with no further discipline, and a written request.

### Transfer prompt

**Prompt:** "If I am keeping notes on [a type of issue], then I will write them on the same day, keep them to facts, and store them [where Human Resources says]. By [date], I will ask how long to keep them."
**Plan starters:**
- Ask Human Resources how supervisors should keep working notes.
- Ask what to do with folders left by a previous supervisor.
- Check your existing notes against the fair note list.

## Lesson 5: Timing, protected activity, and grievances

**Summary:** Sometimes a real performance problem and a protected action happen close together. This lesson teaches you how to respond, and how to treat grievances and stewards as a normal part of the job.
**Minutes:** 13
**Objectives:**
- Pause and check when corrective action follows protected activity.
- Show that a problem predates or is separate from a report or a request.
- Describe the grievance steps under the MAPE contract, and your place in them.
- Work with a steward without taking it personally.

**Takeaways:**
- You can still address real problems. Check the timing first.
- Keep the same standard you use for anyone else.
- A grievance is a contract process. It is not an insult.

### Text: A real problem, at a hard time

The EEOC says employers may still discipline or terminate employees for legitimate, non-retaliatory, non-discriminatory reasons. It also lists a reprimand or a lower evaluation than deserved as possible examples of retaliation. Both of those are true at the same time, and new supervisors can get caught between them.

Suppose a consultant's reports have been late for months. You finally decide to act, and the consultant makes an accommodation request or a discrimination report the same week. You may need to continue. But now you need to do three things before you do:

1. **Call.** Bring it to Human Resources or labor relations before the next step.
2. **Show your record.** Earlier dated notes, conversations and examples show the concern predates the protected activity. Do not create anything that pretends to be older than it is.
3. **Test consistency.** Have you addressed this same issue the same way with others? Would you have taken this step regardless?

Also consider what the request tells you. If the person says a medical issue is behind the late reports, an accommodation may change what they can fairly be asked to do from now on. Ask the ADA coordinator how it affects your next step.

### Tabs: Where the contract and the law meet

**Tab: A request for accommodation**
Protected, according to the EEOC. Consult the ADA coordinator under Policy 1433. Under Article 31 of the MAPE contract, the employee gets union representation at their request.

**Tab: A report of discrimination**
Protected. Report immediately under Policy 1436. Do not question the person about their report as part of your corrective action discussion.

**Tab: Being a witness**
The EEOC says cooperating with an internal investigation is protected. Do not ask anyone what they told an investigator.

**Tab: Filing a grievance or using a steward**
This is a contract right. Article 4, Section 3 bars interfering with or coercing someone over union participation. Do not mention union activity in performance notes.

**Tab: Objecting to a training or an equity effort**
The EEOC says this can be protected, given a fact-specific basis for believing it violates Title VII. Treat conduct as conduct, and do not discipline a viewpoint. Ask Human Resources if conduct and views seem tangled.

### Tabs: Grievances under the MAPE contract, Article 9

**Tab: What is a grievance?**
Section 1: a dispute or disagreement about the interpretation or application of any term of the agreement.

**Tab: Informal**
Section 3: the employee may bring it to their supervisor orally or in writing, indicating it is a grievance, and may discuss it to try to reach a resolution. So it may begin with you. Listen, and call labor relations.

**Tab: Step 1**
A written grievance is filed with the immediate supervisor's supervisor or manager, within 21 calendar days after the event or after the grievant should reasonably have known of it. A meeting is arranged within 10 calendar days of receiving it, and a written answer follows within 10 calendar days of the meeting.

**Tab: Later steps**
The union may appeal to Step 2 within 10 calendar days, and the contract describes further steps and arbitration. "Days" means calendar days unless otherwise specified. Time limits can be extended by mutual written agreement.

**Tab: Your role**
Provide your facts to the person responding. Do not try to settle a contract question on your own. Article 9 also says stewards and grievants must request permission from their immediate supervisor before leaving work to investigate and present grievances, and that permission "shall not be unreasonably withheld." Approve the time.

### Text: Working with stewards

A steward is doing their job when they push back on you. Your relationship with them can last for years. Be direct, be on time and share what you are allowed to share. Do not discuss a represented employee's matter with their steward without checking what is allowed, and do not try to go around them. Do not take it out on anyone who files.

If you have a real disagreement about what the contract means, that is what labor relations is for.

### Statement

The union contract is not the obstacle to fair corrective action. Clear reasons, notice and a chance to respond are what fair corrective action looks like.

### Leader move: After the grievance

**Control:** You control how you treat someone the morning after they file.
**Failure:** Do not go cold. Do not make jokes about it. Do not ask them to withdraw it. Do not ask their coworkers what they think.
**Next:** Keep every regular meeting on the calendar. Keep your assignments and your attention the same as they were. Check yourself in a month: has anything changed for this person?

### Scenario

**Context:** A case consultant has a long record of missed documentation, with dated notes and two coaching talks, and you have been working with labor relations about a written reprimand. On Monday the consultant files a grievance about overtime with their steward. On Wednesday they ask you about a different schedule because of a health issue. Your director says, "Issue the reprimand this week. We can't look like we're backing down."
**Prompt:** What do you do next?

**Option:** Drop the reprimand, because going ahead would look bad now.
**Response:** You may decide on a change after you get advice, but dropping it on reflex means the expectation is no longer applied consistently. It may also leave the real problem unresolved. Take advice first.

**Option:** Issue it this week as directed. Your record predates everything, so you are safe.
**Response:** Your earlier record does help. But "safe" is too strong, and you have new information that bears on the plan: a possible accommodation request and a grievance. Your director's framing is itself a warning sign, since it ties the timing to not "backing down."

**Option (recommended):** Move the schedule request promptly to the ADA coordinator. Tell labor relations and Human Resources about the grievance, the request and your director's comment. Ask how the medical issue may affect the reprimand, and whether the timing should change. Then follow that advice, and document your reasons.
**Response:** This is the strongest move. It keeps the accommodation moving, gives the right people all the facts and separates the decision from any response to the grievance. The reprimand may still go ahead, with a stronger basis and better timing.

**Option:** Ask the consultant if the health issue is connected to their documentation problem, so you can decide.
**Response:** This is a question the process should handle, and asking it now could feel like pressure. The ADA coordinator can advise on how to proceed.

### Knowledge check

**Question:** Under the MAPE contract, how long does a grievant normally have to file a formal grievance at Step 1?
**Options:**
- 7 calendar days after the event.
- 21 calendar days after the event, or after they should reasonably have known of it. (correct)
- 90 calendar days after the event.
- Any time during the contract's term.

**Correct answer:** 21 calendar days after the event, or after they should reasonably have known of it.
**Feedback if correct:** Correct, under Article 9, Section 3. Your role is to give accurate facts and call labor relations. Other agreements may differ.
**Feedback if incorrect:** Check Article 9, Section 3. Step 1 has a 21 calendar day limit. Other agreements may differ.

## Job aid

**Title:** Before you correct: a one-page checklist
**Subtitle:** Contract terms here come from MAPE 2025-2027. For AFSCME, MMA, the Managerial Plan or any other agreement, read that document and ask labor relations.

**Purpose:** To help you prepare fair corrective action, follow the steps and know when to call.

**Remember:**
- Discipline is only for just cause, and corrective where appropriate.
- Call early, before you speak to the employee about consequences.
- Report harassment and discrimination immediately. Do not investigate them yourself.
- Same conduct, same response. Ask how similar situations were handled.

**Section: First check**
- Was the expectation clear and known? Is it applied to everyone?
- Has anyone raised it privately? Initial minor issues come first, and if corrected they stay out of the file.
- Ask: "What's getting in the way?" Anything medical goes to the ADA coordinator.

**Section: Pause and call if**
- The person has recently made a report, requested an accommodation, been a witness, filed a grievance or taken leave.
- A conversation is turning into questioning that may lead to discipline.
- You feel pressure to act fast to make a point.

**Section: Questioning that may lead to discipline**
- Advise the employee of the nature of the investigation.
- Offer union representation. Any waiver must be in writing, before questioning.
- Arrange any interpreter or accommodation first.
- State no conclusions or consequences.
- Pause sentence: "I want to stop here so that we do this properly. I'll get back to you by ____."

**Section: MAPE discipline, Article 8**
- Oral reprimand (identify it as such), written reprimand, suspension, demotion, discharge. Not necessarily in this order.
- Do not embarrass anyone. Give written notice of specific reasons to the employee and union for anything above an oral reprimand.
- For suspension or discharge: a chance to hear the evidence and respond, with representation.
- Each disciplinary entry states the corrective action expected, except discharge.

**Section: Records**
- Out of the file: corrected minor issues, oral reprimands, investigations without discipline.
- Removal on written request: letter of expectation at 6 months; written reprimand at 18 months; suspension record of 10 days or less at 3 years, with no further discipline.
- Notes: date, facts, no guesses, no labels. Ask Human Resources where they belong, and how long to keep them.

**Section: Contacts**
- Labor relations: ____________
- Human Resources: ____________
- ADA coordinator: ____________
- Affirmative Action Officer: ____________

**Do next:** Call labor relations this week to introduce yourself, and ask who holds the authority for each step at DHS.
