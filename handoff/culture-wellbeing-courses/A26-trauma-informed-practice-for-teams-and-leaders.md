## A26. Trauma-Informed Practice for Teams and Leaders

- **Level:** Advanced, self-directed
- **Audience:** managers, directors and senior leaders
- **Domain:** Well-Being (links to Access and Disability Justice)
- **Summary:** Many of the people we serve, and many of our own staff, carry the effects of trauma. This course helps leaders apply the six principles of a trauma-informed approach to how their division runs, and to support staff who are exposed to others' trauma every day.
- **Outcomes:** By the end, learners can (1) explain the six principles of a trauma-informed approach in plain terms, (2) review one process or space through those principles, and (3) put in place one practice that supports staff exposed to secondary trauma.

### Slide outline

1. **Title:** One DSD People, Access and Culture / Trauma-Informed Practice for Teams and Leaders / Leading with safety, trust and choice / Advanced self-directed learning
2. **Opening case:** A family arrives for an eligibility interview. The waiting room is loud, the security check is abrupt and the first question is about past evictions. The parent leaves before the interview starts. Down the hall, a case manager who handled a child protection call that morning is back at a desk with no break.
3. **What trauma-informed means:** A trauma-informed organization understands how common trauma is, recognizes its signs, builds that knowledge into how it works and tries not to cause harm again. It doesn't require knowing anyone's history.
4. **Why it matters for leaders:** Policies, spaces and schedules can calm people or put them on alert. Leaders decide most of those things.
5. **Principle 1, safety:** People need to feel physically and emotionally safe, in the lobby, on the phone and in team meetings.
6. **Principle 2, trustworthiness and transparency:** Say what will happen, why and what comes next. Keep the promises you make.
7. **Principle 3, peer support:** People with lived experience, and colleagues who have been through the same work, help others feel understood.
8. **Principles 4 and 5, collaboration and choice:** Share power where you can. Offer real choices, even small ones, and ask people what they need.
9. **Principle 6, culture, history and gender:** Trauma is shaped by racism, ableism, colonization and other harms that systems, ours included, have caused. Name that history and design with it in mind.
10. **Practice 1:** see below.
11. **Reviewing a process through the principles:** Walk through a client's first visit step by step. At each step, ask: Is it safe? Is it clear? Is there a choice?
12. **Secondary traumatic stress:** Staff who hear about trauma every day can be affected by it. Signs include intrusive thoughts, avoidance and feeling on edge. It is a known result of the work, not a weakness.
13. **Supporting exposed staff:** Debrief after hard calls. Give people time between intense cases. Make sure supervision includes how people are doing, not only what they did.
14. **Practice 2:** see below.
15. **Trauma-informed leadership in meetings:** Share agendas ahead of time. Warn people before discussing hard material. Let people step out without explaining.
16. **Practice 3:** see below.
17. **What this is not:** This isn't therapy, and leaders aren't asked to diagnose anyone. It is a way to set up work so it does less harm.
18. **Rewrite activity:** Rewrite this appointment letter so it is clear, calm and offers a choice: "You must appear at the office on the date below. Failure to appear will result in case closure."
19. **Your division's next step:** Pick one client process or staff routine and review it through the six principles with the people who use it.
20. **Shared culture, real responsibilities:** Everyone can treat people with care. Leaders control spaces, policies and schedules, so a trauma-informed division depends most on their choices.
21. **Reflection:** Where in your division might a person feel least safe, and what would change that?
22. **Closing:** People can't take in help while they feel threatened. Safety comes first, for the people we serve and for the people who serve them.

### Practice slides

Each practice slide ends with: "In Slide Show view, select a response to explore its effects."

**Practice 1.** The county partner tells the program manager that families describe the lobby as "like a police station." What does the program manager do?

- **A.** Explains that the security setup is required. *Feedback: It may be required, and the explanation leaves families' experience unchanged.*
- **B.** Walks through the lobby with the county partner and two parents, and asks what would help them feel safer. *Feedback: This uses collaboration and choice, and finds changes that can work within security rules.*
- **C.** Orders new lobby furniture. *Feedback: It may help, and it skips asking the people affected what matters most.*

**Practice 2.** The supervisor's team handled a child fatality review this week. What does the program manager do?

- **A.** Sends a thank-you email. *Feedback: Thanks matter, and an email alone may not reach what people are carrying.*
- **B.** Offers a facilitated debrief, lighter assignments for a few days and a reminder about the Employee Assistance Program. *Feedback: This treats the effect of the work as real and gives people choices about support.*
- **C.** Asks the supervisor to check that everyone is okay. *Feedback: Delegating care is reasonable, and the supervisor was exposed too.*

**Practice 3.** A leadership meeting will cover a staff assault that happened last month. How does the program manager open?

- **A.** Goes straight to the details. *Feedback: Efficient, and it can catch people off guard.*
- **B.** Says what will be discussed, why, and that anyone can step out without explaining. *Feedback: Transparency and choice let people take part without being overwhelmed.*
- **C.** Skips the topic to avoid upsetting anyone. *Feedback: Avoidance leaves people without answers and the safety issue unaddressed.*

### Narration

- **Opening (after slide 1):** "Many people who come to us have lived through trauma. So have many of the people who work here. A trauma-informed approach doesn't ask anyone to share their story. It asks leaders to set up the work in ways that build safety, trust and choice. This course may touch on difficult experiences, so pause whenever you need to."
- **Explainer (after slide 9):** The six principles work together. Safety without transparency can feel like control. Choice without safety can feel like being left alone.
- **Check-in (after slide 13):** How does your division support staff after a hard call or case today?
- **Explainer (after slide 17):** If this material brings up something for you, support is available, including your Employee Assistance Program and, in the U.S., the 988 line.

### Video script: "The First Visit" (about 3 minutes)

**Cast:**

- **Narrator:** C3 (Host voice)
- **Supervisor:** C6 (Co-host voice)
- **Case manager:** C5 (Host voice)
- **Program manager:** C2 (Host voice)
- **New colleague:** C7 (Co-host voice)
- **County partner:** C1 (Co-host voice)
- **Parent:** C4 (on screen, no lines)
- **Security officer:** voice only (Co-host voice)
- **Staff member:** voice only (Host voice)

**Scene 1. Lobby.**

NARRATOR: A parent arrives for an eligibility interview. Watch the first ten minutes through their eyes.

SECURITY OFFICER (voice only): Bag on the table. Next.

(The parent waits. A TV is loud. A number is called twice, sharply.)

STAFF MEMBER (voice only): Have you ever been evicted?

(The parent stands up and leaves.)

**Scene 2. The program manager and the county partner, walking through the lobby.**

COUNTY PARTNER: Families tell us it feels like a police station.

PROGRAM MANAGER: Security has to stay. What else could change?

COUNTY PARTNER: Ask them. I can bring two parents next week.

**Scene 3. Same lobby, after changes.**

NARRATOR: Some changes cost almost nothing.

SECURITY OFFICER (voice only): Morning. I just need a quick look in your bag, then you're all set.

CASE MANAGER: Hi, I'm your case manager. Here's what we'll do today, and it takes about thirty minutes. You can stop any time. Would you like water first?

**Scene 4. Team room, after a hard call.**

SUPERVISOR: (to the new colleague) That was a tough one. Take fifteen minutes before your next case. Want to talk it through after?

NEW COLLEAGUE: Yeah. Thanks.

NARRATOR: Safety, trust and choice, for the people we serve and the people who serve them.

### Put this course to work

Pick one client process, such as a first visit or an appointment letter, and walk through it with the people who use it, asking at each step: Is it safe? Is it clear? Is there a choice?

- **Framework link:** Well-Being domain, with links to Access and Disability Justice; Apply, Connect and Lead modes.
- **Tension it addresses:** security and compliance versus safety and dignity.
- **Care points:** content warning before the course; no disclosure asked; the opening case shows no graphic detail; EAP and 988 named, with 988 to be confirmed for your audience.
