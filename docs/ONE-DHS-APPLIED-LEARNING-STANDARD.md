# Applied learning for One DHS

This design standard supports the owner-provided One DHS Program Requirements Document. Its purpose is to help staff apply cultural understanding to decisions, services, communication, workplace relationships and organizational practices. It is a design direction, not proof of an employee's or the agency's Intercultural Development Inventory orientation or a claim that the application has achieved culture change.

## A useful learning cycle

When a task benefits from learning support, make it possible to:

1. Start with the staff member's work question, decision or goal.
2. Offer a concise explanation and a realistic, clearly identified example that distinguishes evidence from assumptions.
3. Let the person try a choice or draft, with access to guidance when useful.
4. Provide specific feedback that explains reasoning, tradeoffs, uncertainties and what could be improved. Do not score or label the person.
5. Help the person adapt the learning to their own work, identify an appropriate next step and reflect on what happened.

People may use one step without completing the others. A directory lookup, simple factual answer or other short task should remain direct. Suggest, rather than require, deeper learning when it is useful. Ask questions that acknowledge differences without assuming an individual shares a group's characteristics. Preserve the person's choice, applicable policy and responsible decision authority.

## How the application supports it

- **Home and Your work:** offer recognizable work and role starting points, with access to learning, guidance and practical tools.
- **Ask:** give a substantive, source-grounded response; explain uncertainty; help consider perspectives, options and a next step when relevant; and connect to an appropriate practice or resource. Let the user review suggested questions before submitting them.
- **Learn and Library:** state a useful objective, connect concepts to real work, offer an example or brief practice and make the next relevant action easy to find. Retain the full source and its context.
- **Communities:** prepare staff to ask and listen rather than profile individuals. Keep Tribal sovereignty and the existing Tribal pages intact.
- **Practice and Apply:** use realistic situations, meaningful choices and feedback that explains why. Connect drafts to the responsible person, relevant sources and an optional return to learning or Ask.
- **Videos and audio:** align narration with what is shown, present short sequential steps, use visual variety only when it supports comprehension, and provide captions and transcripts.
- **Leaders and teams:** support voluntary discussion and practical changes in meetings, decisions and services. Distinguish learning activity from evidence that a change helped.

## Privacy, access and review

Use optional reflection and repeated practice to support learning, not employee surveillance, ranking or performance assessment. Do not infer orientation from job title, course choice or a response. Do not request confidential client, case, health or personnel information. Be accurate about where work is stored or processed and document unresolved differences between the owner's privacy commitments and the implementation.

Design for keyboard and assistive technology, accessible formats and small screens. Preserve official sources, user choice, existing safety and spending controls, and relevant accessibility protections. A completed activity, positive response or generated answer is not proof of individual development or agency-wide culture change. Evaluate service and workplace outcomes using appropriate evidence and voluntary, privacy-respecting participation.

The U.S. Institute of Education Sciences recommends combining worked examples with problem-solving, explanatory questions and spaced review: https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/1. Its guide was developed for educational settings; applying those principles to this staff tool is a design adaptation, not evidence that One DHS has achieved a particular outcome.

Each resource should offer a relevant opportunity to reflect, practice, or apply its ideas in work, with a route to Ask for feedback. Preserve original sources and distinguish their guidance from the application’s learning support.
