# Program evaluation: a guide for the owner and goal leads

One DHS People, Access and Culture. Model version 2026-11-16.1.

This guide explains how to tell whether the program is making a difference: in how people work, in the decisions DHS
makes, and in what staff and the people DHS serves experience. It covers the logic model, the seven outcomes, the six
ADSA goals with what to look for each year, the Plan-Do-Study-Act cycle, who reviews what, and which evidence counts.

The definitions behind this guide are in `lib/program/evaluation.ts`. A check (`node scripts/evals/run.ts`, eval B)
confirms that every goal action and outcome has something to look for, and that nothing relies on counting learning.

---

## 1. The ground rules

**Evidence comes only from records people choose to keep or share.** Nothing anyone does while learning is ever counted.

### Evidence you can use

| Source | What it holds | Who keeps it |
|---|---|---|
| Shared decision record | A decision, who it affects, the evidence, the alternatives, the decision owner and a review date | The person who shares it to the team space |
| Voluntary outcome record ("Share a result") | What someone tried, what changed, what they noticed, and keep, adjust or stop. No name, month only | The person who shares it; only the program owner reads it |
| Before-and-after pair | A notice, form, meeting plan or job posting before and after a change, with a short note | The goal lead or decision owner |
| Equity review record | The review tool used, what it found and the decision owner's disposition | The decision owner or review lead |
| Program work record (a Plan-Do-Study-Act cycle) | Question, prediction, measure, what was tried, what was found, the disposition | The program owner or a goal lead |
| Representative task session | Themes from facilitated, consented sessions where five to eight volunteers try real tasks. No names in notes | The program owner |
| DHS aggregate data | Service access, hiring and retention, accommodation and interpreter timeliness, vendor participation, by group, small cells suppressed | The person or area responsible for the data |
| "You said, we did" account | Input received, what changed, what did not and why | The goal lead, with the people who gave the input |
| Plan, charter or guide | A charter, plan, guide or crosswalk with its responsible role | The goal lead or the program owner |
| Meeting decisions | Decisions and follow-up from a leaders' review or team meeting | Whoever keeps the meeting's decisions |
| Content and source record | Source dates, corrections, and which guidance each correction reached | The program owner |
| Application check results | Accessibility, sources, links and sharing checks | The program owner. Delivery evidence only, always reported apart from outcomes |

### Never evidence

- Page views, visits or traffic.
- Completions of courses, lessons, podcasts or any other learning.
- Time spent.
- Answers to checks, quizzes or practice.
- Counts of Ask or Guidance use.
- Attendance tied to learning.
- Any other count of learning or use, including anonymous totals.
- An on-page feedback widget or rating (ruled out by the owner, 2026-11-16).
- Scores, rankings or records about individual staff. Responses are evaluated; people never are.

Training that DHS requires, such as Tribal Consultation training or the IDI, keeps its records in the official DHS
system with the person or area responsible. The program holds none of them.

### Label every finding

- **Its level.** *Delivery*: the output exists (it does not show use). *Application*: it was used in real work.
  *Benefit*: something changed for people, with its limits; contribution, not causation.
- **Its source.** A *participant report* (someone's own account) or an *independent observation* (a record or an
  observer). Keep the two apart.
- **Its limits.** What it cannot show.
- **Empty evidence means "not yet evidenced"**, never "no change".

Every evaluation also states its question, sample, period, findings, limits and the role responsible for it.

## 2. The logic model

The program's logic model is shown at `/program/model`. Each level has something to look for.

| Level | What to look for | Evidence | Level of evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inputs | Resources carry a dated source checked in the last 12 months; corrections are handled within 30 days. Each ADSA goal and area of work has a named goal lead or responsible role. | Content records, application checks, charters | Delivery |
| Activities | Leaders' reviews are held with their decisions written down; equity reviews are offered before key decisions. Each improvement cycle names its question, prediction, measure and decision owner before the change is tried. | Meeting decisions, program work records | Delivery |
| Outputs | Published resources are accessible, sourced and easy to share. A tool or guide exists for each of the 28 ADSA actions. | Application checks, content records | Delivery |
| Short-term outcomes | In task sessions, volunteers find a source, prepare an accessible meeting or notice, name who to involve and explain the next step. Staff share a first application. Barriers are documented. | Task sessions, shared results, review records | Application |
| Intermediate outcomes | Key decisions carry an equity review and the decision owner's disposition. A meeting, supervisory or hiring practice changes after a review. Follow-up accounts show comparable work done with less help. | Shared decision records, before-and-after pairs, shared results | Application |
| Long-term outcomes | Priority service gaps narrow by race, disability, language and place. The two-year retention gap narrows. Community input is acted on. Later decisions reuse earlier work. Other divisions and administrations adapt a DSD practice. | DHS aggregate data, "you said, we did" accounts, decision records | Benefit |

## 3. The seven outcomes

| Outcome | What to look for | Source | Reviewed by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Staff capability | In quarterly task sessions, participants find a source, prepare an accessible meeting or notice, name who to involve and explain the next step. Note where they hesitate. | Task sessions | The program owner with one goal lead |
| Capability transfer | Follow-up accounts of comparable work done alone, with what the person could and could not do alone. | Shared results | The program owner |
| Access | Barriers documented and corrected, each checked so people can now complete the task. Accommodation and interpreter requests met on time. | Before-and-after pairs, review records, DHS data | The accessibility contact; the data partners |
| Workplace culture | Changed practices with a review: meeting formats, onboarding, interview panels. Climate results by group from HR's own survey. | Decision records, HR aggregate data | The Goal 3 lead with an HR partner |
| Institutional application | Policy, budget, technology and procurement decisions with a review and the decision owner's disposition. | Shared decision records, review records | Decision owners |
| Organizational memory | Corrections traced to every piece of affected guidance; decisions that cite earlier work. | Content records, decision records | The program owner |
| Agency coherence | Another division or administration adapts a DSD practice, with what changed for its setting; pilot findings lead to a decision to expand or revise. | Program work records, decision records | The program owner with contacts in each administration |

## 4. The six ADSA goals, year by year

Year 1 looks for signs the work is happening (delivery). Year 2 looks for signs that how work is done is changing
(application). Year 3 looks for signs that results are improving (benefit). Years move when the work is ready, not on a
calendar. For each action, its two evidence questions (at `/program/implementation`) are the questions to ask when you
review it, and its work product is the record to look for.

### Goal 1: Eliminate Disparities (actions 1.1 to 1.5)

| Year | What to look for | Evidence | Reviewed by |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A division equity team is chartered, with a chair connected to the administration's Equity Committee (1.1). Disparity goals have baselines (1.2). Each goal has an action plan with a responsible role (1.3). | Charter, goal proposals, action plans | Leaders' review |
| 2 | Key decisions carry an Equity Analysis Toolkit review done while the decision could still change, with the decision owner's disposition (1.4). Each disparity goal has an approved measure definition (1.5, 1.2). | Decision records, review records, measure definitions | Decision owners; data partners |
| 3 | Priority service gaps narrow by race, disability, language and place (1.2, 1.5). | DHS aggregate data | ADSA Equity Director and HR partners |

### Goal 2: Community Engagement (actions 2.1 to 2.5)

| Year | What to look for | Evidence | Reviewed by |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Engagement plans offer accessible ways to take part and payment for people's time where policy allows, prepared with the Toolkit and the Community Relations Guide (2.3, 2.5). The route to Tribal Consultation training in the official DHS system, and to the responsible Tribal liaison, is named and current (2.2). | Engagement plans | Goal 2 lead with community partners |
| 2 | Decisions take in community input before they are final; "you said, we did" items are closed; the relevant advisory council is consulted through its relationship owner (2.4, 2.1). | "You said, we did" accounts, decision records | Goal 2 lead with community partners |
| 3 | Communities report being heard, in their own words, gathered by the goal lead (2.3, 2.4). | "You said, we did" accounts | Goal 2 lead with community partners |

### Goal 3: Hiring and Retention (actions 3.1 to 3.8)

| Year | What to look for | Evidence | Reviewed by |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Job descriptions and hiring steps reviewed; interview panel guidance and job-related equity questions adopted with HR; an onboarding plan in place (3.3, 3.5, 3.4). The yearly employee-experience survey has an owner in HR, a baseline and protections for small groups (3.1). | Review records, plans | Goal 3 lead with an HR partner |
| 2 | Panels formed to the guidance (3.3). A workplace practice addressing bias or microaggressions changes after a review (3.2). Employee resource group and Employee Engagement Committee support shows in scheduling and workload decisions (3.7, 3.8). | Decision records, review records | Goal 3 lead |
| 3 | The two-year retention gap by group narrows (3.6); climate results by group improve in HR's own survey (3.1). | HR aggregate data | ADSA Equity Director and HR partners |

Taking part in an employee resource group is never required, recorded or asked about.

### Goal 4: Learning and Development (actions 4.1 to 4.3)

| Year | What to look for | Evidence | Reviewed by |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Learning-and-application plans for staff and hiring managers; skills-building and leadership opportunities listed with how people can reach them (4.1, 4.3). The official ADSA IDI Implementation Plan and its authorized administrator are named, and assessment results stay with that administrator (4.2). | Plans | Goal 4 lead |
| 2 | Staff choose to share accounts of learning applied in their work; task sessions show people doing representative tasks with their reasoning (4.1). | Shared results, task sessions | The program owner with the Goal 4 lead |
| 3 | Follow-up accounts show comparable work done with more independence; leaders describe changed practice in their teams (4.1, 4.3). | Shared results, meeting decisions | Leaders' review |

Never completions from this application.

### Goal 5: Contracts and Procurement (actions 5.1 to 5.3)

| Year | What to look for | Evidence | Reviewed by |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Current contracting and grant practices reviewed with the Toolkit (5.1); a solicitation outreach plan compares venues (5.3); the verified current benchmark and baseline for vendor participation identified (5.2). | Review records, plans | Goal 5 lead |
| 2 | Solicitations change after a review, and venues are added so more eligible vendors hear of opportunities (5.1, 5.3). | Before-and-after pairs, review records | Decision owners |
| 3 | Vendor participation moves toward verified benchmarks (5.2). | Procurement aggregate data | ADSA Equity Director and the person or area responsible for procurement data |

### Goal 6: Communication and Accessibility (actions 6.1 to 6.4)

| Year | What to look for | Evidence | Reviewed by |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | An inclusive-language guide (6.1), a crosswalk of communication practice to the National CLAS Standards (6.4) and a notable-dates recognition plan (6.2). | Guides, crosswalk, plan | Goal 6 lead |
| 2 | Communications revised after a review, with a check that people can complete the task; accessibility remediation closed and rechecked (6.1, 6.3, 6.4). | Before-and-after pairs, accessibility records | Accessibility contact, with people who use assistive technology |
| 3 | Access complaints fall, and accommodation and interpreter requests are met on time (6.3, 6.4). Read fewer complaints alongside people's accounts: it can also mean people gave up. | DHS aggregate data | ADSA Equity Director and HR partners |

Accessibility training records stay in the official DHS system.

## 5. The Plan-Do-Study-Act cycle

Plan-Do-Study-Act is the program's six-stage improvement cycle (at `/equity-framework#cycle`) in four steps. Each cycle
is a program work record in the Consultant Workspace (`/consultant/program`). Run a cycle when the people involved
agree, not on a calendar.

| Step | Improvement-cycle stages | What you record |
|---|---|---|
| Plan | Assess, Plan | The question, the change, who is affected, a prediction, the measure, the decision owner, and when to look again |
| Do | Implement | What actually happened when you tried it small |
| Study | Measure, Learn | The result against the prediction, the evidence, its level and source, the limits and your confidence |
| Act | Improve | The decision owner's disposition: retain, revise, stop or not yet known; the next action and who carries it |

### Step by step, with the Goal 6 example

**The situation.** People who get one unit's appointment notice often call to ask what to do next. Goal 6, actions 6.1
(inclusive language) and 6.3 (accessibility). Outcome: access.

1. **Plan.**
   - *Question:* Is the notice hard to act on?
   - *Change:* Rewrite it in plain language with the next step first, and check it with a screen reader.
   - *Who is affected:* People receiving the notice, their families, and the unit's phone staff.
   - *Prediction:* Fewer callers will ask what the next step is, and people using the notice will say it is clear.
   - *Measure* (the ten measure fields): calls tagged "what do I do next" about this notice, divided by notices sent,
     for one month before and one month after. Source: the unit's call-reason tallies, an operational record of the
     service kept by the unit lead, with no caller details. Comparison: same unit, same month length; seasonal changes
     could explain some of the difference. No target is approved; this is a small test.
   - *Decision owner:* the unit lead. *Look again:* March 2027.
   - The measure never counts learning or use. A measure such as "staff who completed the plain-language course" is
     refused when you save it.
2. **Do.** One unit, one notice, one month. Keep the before and after versions together. Write down what actually
   happened.
3. **Study.**
   - Compare with the prediction: next-step questions fell; two callers still asked where to bring documents.
   - Evidence: the call-reason tallies for the two months, comments from people using the notice, and phone staff
     observations.
   - Level: application. Source: independent observation (the tallies and staff observations), kept apart from
     people's comments, which are participant reports.
   - Limits: one month each side; call volume varies by season; the tallies depend on how staff tag calls.
   - Confidence: early observation.
4. **Act.**
   - The unit lead records *revise*: add where to bring documents, then use the template for the unit's other notices.
     Carried by the unit lead with the Goal 6 lead.
   - Tell the phone staff and the people who commented what changed and why ("you said, we did").
   - Carry what was learned into the next comparable notice. That is organizational memory at work.

The quarterly program review reads every open and closed cycle and writes the evidence summary by level.

## 6. Who reviews, and when

| When | Who | What |
|---|---|---|
| At the end of each cycle | The decision owner, with the goal lead for that ADSA goal | The prediction against the result, and the disposition |
| Monthly | One DSD Team (the standing volunteer committee) | Open cycles, "you said, we did" accounts, themes from shared results |
| Quarterly program review | The program owner, the goal leads and leaders' review | The evidence summary by level, application check results reported apart, and decisions to retain, revise, connect, support or stop |
| When aggregate data is refreshed | The ADSA Equity Director and HR partners who hold the data | Aggregate DHS data, small-cell suppression and the comparison period |
| After each engagement | The Goal 2 lead with community partners; the responsible Tribal liaison for Tribal matters | Whether community input changed the decision, and the account given back |
| After each accessibility review | The accessibility contact, with people who use assistive technology | Whether the correction works for people |
| Once a year | A reviewer outside the program, for example a DHS research and evaluation colleague | Method, limits, whether claims match their evidence level, and the privacy of the evaluation itself |

## 7. Shared results (the voluntary outcome record)

Colleagues can describe a change they tried at `/support/share-result`. The form asks what they were trying to improve,
what they tried, what changed, what they noticed, what should happen next and their recommendation (keep, adjust, stop,
or more time needed). They can name the outcome and the ADSA action it connects to, a public page that shows the change,
and a month to look at it again. Sharing needs their consent, and the text is checked for names and client, case, health
or personnel details before anything is kept.

What is kept: the account itself, the scope (One DHS or One DSD), the month it arrived, and the outcome and action named.
What is never kept: a name, contact, role, unit, network address, browser details, the page they came from, or the day
and time.

The program owner reads shared results at `/consultant/outcomes`, grouped under each ADSA goal and each outcome. Read
them for themes: what helped, what got in the way, what to try next. They are participant reports at the application
level. There are no totals: a pile of accounts is not a measure of reach. When a theme points to something worth
testing, open a Plan-Do-Study-Act cycle for it.
