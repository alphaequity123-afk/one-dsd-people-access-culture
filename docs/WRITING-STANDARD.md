# Program writing standard

Accepted November 13, 2026. The rollout applies to program-authored explanations, lessons, activities, summaries, and page guidance. Official policy wording, law, source titles, attributed quotations, and transcripts tied to published recordings retain their original wording.

- Write colleague to colleague for Minnesota DHS and DSD staff and leaders. Do not assume that a person's role determines their ability.
- Lead with a short plain-language summary and retain a substantive professional explanation below it.
- Aim for body text around grades 11–13, with advanced material reaching grade 14 where warranted. Use grades 8–10 as a guide for clear instructions. A score does not determine quality or justify adding difficult words.
- Use connected paragraphs and varied sentence lengths. Aim for an average of 17–22 words in running prose, with no more than 15 percent of sentences at eight words or fewer. Labels, lists, and instructions need separate judgment.
- Explain the reason for an instruction at least once. State a course-wide limitation once rather than repeating it in every lesson.
- Ground examples in the named DHS or DSD work supported by the source. Preserve factual limits; do not invent a policy, legal obligation, agency procedure, or community preference to make a lesson more specific.
- Increase the depth of advanced learning through evidence, alternatives, consequences, and judgment rather than complex vocabulary.
- Review readability with two measures, sentence-length distribution, repeated wording, and source-grounded specificity. These measurements support an editor's review and do not replace it.
- Keep each recorded transcript synchronized with its audio. A change to the lesson explanation does not authorize a silent change to the transcript.

Implementation records distinguish inventoried, drafted, reviewed, published, and protected material. A shared prompt change is not a completed rewrite of existing pages.
