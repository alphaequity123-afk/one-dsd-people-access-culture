## Scene 1: What this course is for (0:00–0:32)
**Visual:** A weekly planner on a desk with four items written on it: "a plan," "a hire," "a contract," "a memo." The four items are named in the narration.
**Narrator (C3):** "Leading equity at DSD: what the DHS Equity Policy asks of leaders" is a Foundations course for division and section leaders, managers and supervisors who sign off on plans, people decisions, contracts and communications. It connects the policy to work you already manage, giving you a clear picture of leaders' duties under the policy and how those duties show up in a plan, a hire, a contract or a memo in an ordinary week.

## Scene 2: A moment at work (0:32–1:12)
**Visual:** The program manager (C2) and a newer colleague (C7) sit at a small table. Three folders are labeled "Training records," "Section charter" and "Policy changes this quarter." A printed outline reads "Two-page summary, due Friday." The labels and the outline title are spoken in the dialogue.
**Program manager (C2):** Our deputy wants a two-page summary of how the section is living the Equity Policy. It's due Friday.
**Newer colleague (C7):** That seems manageable. We have the training records and the statement in the section charter, and we could mention last year's listening session. The team thought we'd lead with those.
**Program manager (C2):** Those are all real. We also sent out three policy changes this quarter, and two of them went out without anyone checking who they would burden.
**Newer colleague (C7):** So what goes in the middle of the two pages?
**Program manager (C2):** That's the call I need to make before Friday.

## Scene 3: What the course gives you (1:12–2:33)
**Visual:** Four cards appear one at a time, each read aloud: "Decide with people," "Check help and burden," "Make access the default," "Fair people decisions and honest data."
**Narrator (C3):** The course turns the policy into responsibilities you can point to: decide with people; check help and burden, meaning who a change helps and who it costs; make access the default; and fair people decisions and honest data. For an action with prolonged or long-term impact scheduled for leadership approval, the policy requires the DHS Equity Analysis or Equity Scan, or a customized tool developed in consultation with an equity director.
**Visual:** A draft document with six short lines down the margin, several marked "not yet." The words "not yet" are spoken in the narration.
**Narrator (C3):** A middle lesson offers the six-line check, six preparation questions you run on a draft rather than the final. An honest "not yet" on a line tells you what to do before the decision goes out. The check helps you prepare; it does not replace the required analysis. The course also separates a leader's own decisions from concerns that need a formal process, such as a discrimination report or an accommodation request. Those decisions stay with Human Resources, the Equal Opportunity and Access Division and the other responsible offices.
**Visual:** The job aid's opening line on screen: "Identify the issue. Hear affected perspectives. Check requirements. Use the responsible process. Review evidence. Plan follow-through." The line is read in the narration.
**Narrator (C3):** The course ends with a job aid that follows the work in order: identify the issue, hear affected perspectives, check requirements, use the responsible process, review evidence and plan follow-through.

## Closing (2:33–2:58)
**Visual:** The course page, with the first lesson card, "What the policy asks of leaders," highlighted. The card title is spoken.
**Narrator (C3):** Here is a question to consider privately. In the last decision you signed off on, what in the record would show who was affected and how their perspectives shaped the choice? When you're ready, open the first lesson, "What the policy asks of leaders." The course is voluntary; the required equity analysis and assigned training remain separate responsibilities.
