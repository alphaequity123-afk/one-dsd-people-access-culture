## Scene 1: What this course is for (0:00–0:30)
**Visual:** A short printed document on a desk, its cover reading "Executive Order 23-12." The title is spoken in the narration.
**Narrator (C3):** "Executive Order 23-12 in plain language" is a short Foundations course for DSD leaders, supervisors and staff who want to understand the signed order and how it connects with agency work. It explains what the order requires of agencies and how it shapes DSD work. It helps you avoid two easy mistakes: treating the order as only a statement of values, or treating it as a detailed checklist for every task.

## Scene 2: A moment at work (0:30–1:06)
**Visual:** A case manager (C5) stops at the supervisor's (C6) desk before a staff meeting. A meeting agenda lies on the desk, with no readable text.
**Case manager (C5):** Quick question before the staff meeting. I heard that Executive Order 23-12 sets a hiring target for our unit, and a rule for how we export PDFs. Is that right?
**Supervisor (C6):** You're the second person to ask me that this week.
**Case manager (C5):** If it's in the order, we should be following it. And if it isn't, does the order have anything to do with us?
**Supervisor (C6):** Both fair questions. Give me a minute, because I want to answer them carefully in front of the whole team.

## Scene 3: What the course gives you (1:06–2:33)
**Visual:** A simple diagram with two boxes labeled "One Minnesota Council" and "Community Council," each connected to a smaller box labeled with its support office. The labels are read in the narration.
**Narrator (C3):** The signed order continues two statewide councils. The Governor's One Minnesota Council on Diversity, Inclusion, and Equity brings agency leaders and equity practitioners together to collaborate and to monitor and advise on relevant policies and programs. The Governor's Community Council on Inclusion and Equity brings community advice into that collaboration and reviews the annual report. The Office of Inclusion supports the One Minnesota Council and agency-specific equity plans, and the Governor's Office of Equity, Opportunity, and Accessibility supports the Community Council. Preparing the annual report is the One Minnesota Council's responsibility, not a deadline for each unit.
**Visual:** Two cards, read aloud: "Activity" and "Evidence."
**Narrator (C3):** Later lessons separate activity from evidence: a meeting can help a plan action, but attendance alone does not show the intended improvement happened. Four optional preparation questions ask who is affected, what is unclear or missing, which requirement and decision role apply, and what next step fits your authority. They do not replace the analysis the DHS Equity Policy requires. Reporting and accommodation concerns follow their own policies, and those decisions stay with HR and the other responsible offices.
**Visual:** The job aid page, with four headings: "Keep the order's structure clear," "Use optional preparation questions," "Preserve actual requirements and evidence" and "Recognize the formal response." The headings are read in the narration.
**Narrator (C3):** The closing job aid has four parts: keep the order's structure clear, use optional preparation questions, preserve actual requirements and evidence, and recognize the formal response.

## Closing (2:33–2:58)
**Visual:** The course page, with the first lesson card, "What the order actually says," highlighted. The card title is spoken.
**Narrator (C3):** Here is a question to consider privately. When a colleague says a statewide order requires something, how do you usually check that before passing it on to your team? When you're ready, open the first lesson, "What the order actually says." The course is voluntary, and practice examples or private reflection are enough; no live work product is required.
