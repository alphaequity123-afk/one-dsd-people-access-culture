## Scene 1: What this video covers (0:00–0:30)
**Visual:** Title card: "Transformational leadership: shared purpose and change." Plain background, no logos or real people.
**{{NARRATOR}}:** This short video looks at transformational leadership, which this pathway sums up as shared purpose and change. The leader motivates people around a vision, challenges existing practice and supports people's development. That can make major redesign possible, but transformation is not equitable by itself, because the direction of the change and who carries its costs both matter. We will look at one fictional scenario and two ways a leader might respond.

## Scene 2: The common scenario (0:30–1:10)
**Visual:** A simple illustrated team meeting. On-screen title: "Who gets the visible work?" {{CASE_MANAGER}} appears as the employee who raised the concern.
**{{NARRATOR}}:** Every lesson in this pathway uses the same fictional scenario, "Who gets the visible work?" A supervisor keeps giving high-visibility assignments to employees described as polished, confident and ready to represent the team. A Black employee points out that they get a lot of behind-the-scenes technical work but fewer chances to present to leadership, and a colleague interrupts: "Everything doesn't have to be about race." Anything that may involve discrimination or retaliation goes through the agency's process, with the area responsible. Later, the employee goes to the supervisor.

## Scene 3: A stronger version (1:10–1:45)
**Visual:** {{SUPERVISOR}} and {{CASE_MANAGER}} at a small table in an office.
**{{CASE_MANAGER}}:** I want the assignments reviewed, and I'm concerned that raising this will hurt my opportunities.
**{{SUPERVISOR}}:** This concern exposes a gap between our stated values and how we distribute opportunities. We need to change the process, not simply reassure people.
**{{NEW_COLLEAGUE}}:** (a week later, to {{CASE_MANAGER}}) There's an owner for the review now, and a date on the calendar to check how it's going.
**{{NARRATOR}}:** The supervisor then sets up a change effort with an owner, resources and follow-up, so the vision is tied to a concrete change in how opportunity is distributed.

## Scene 4: A harmful version (1:45–2:05)
**Visual:** The team meeting room.
**{{SUPERVISOR}}:** We all need to unite behind our vision and stop focusing on differences.
**{{CASE_MANAGER}}:** (quietly, to {{NEW_COLLEAGUE}}) So raising a problem makes me the one working against the team.
**{{NARRATOR}}:** Unity becomes a demand to suppress disagreement, and the employee's concern is recast as a threat to the team's purpose.

## Scene 5: What it is not, and what it means for equity (2:05–2:50)
**Visual:** On-screen list, read aloud: "Not transformative leadership. Not a demand for unity. Not a promise without a plan."
**{{NARRATOR}}:** Transformational leadership is not the same as transformative leadership, which centers power and structural inequity, and it is not a demand for unity that silences disagreement. It is also not a promise without a plan. A shared purpose can carry a team through hard change, and investment in development can widen who is ready to lead. The risks are overpromising with too little attention to who carries the work, vision-driven pressure that suppresses inconvenient evidence, and change that improves performance for most people while leaving some further behind. The lesson sees moderate to high potential when the vision includes explicit equity outcomes, and says inspiration alone is not enough.

## Closing (2:50–3:10)
**Visual:** The reflection question on screen, read aloud.
**{{NARRATOR}}:** A question to consider on your own: How can a leader tell the difference between resistance and a real problem? The rest of this lesson, on this page, covers another conflict, strengths and risks, when to use this approach, safeguards, and a practice exercise.
