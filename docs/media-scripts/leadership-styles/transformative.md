# Video script: Transformative or equity-centered leadership

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learn/leadership-styles/transformative
- **Length:** about 3 minutes (about 450 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Supervisor (C6, Co-host voice), Case manager (C5, Host voice), Newer colleague (C7, Co-host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: What this video covers (0:00–0:30)
**Visual:** Title card: "Transformative or equity-centered leadership: changing structures." Plain background, no logos or real people.
**Narrator (C3):** This short video looks at transformative or equity-centered leadership, which this pathway sums up as changing structures. It explicitly examines power, inequitable assumptions, institutional arrangements and who gets the benefits and burdens. It does not presume the answer. It investigates criteria, history, authority and effects, and then changes the rules and resources that create barriers. We will look at one fictional scenario and two ways a leader might respond.

## Scene 2: The common scenario (0:30–1:05)
**Visual:** A simple illustrated team meeting. On-screen title: "Who gets the visible work?" The case manager (C5) appears as the employee who raised the concern.
**Narrator (C3):** Every lesson in this pathway uses the same fictional scenario, "Who gets the visible work?" A supervisor keeps giving high-visibility assignments to employees described as polished, confident and ready to represent the team. A Black employee points out that they get a lot of behind-the-scenes technical work but fewer chances to present to leadership, and a colleague interrupts: "Everything doesn't have to be about race." Anything that may involve discrimination or retaliation goes through the agency's process, with the area responsible. Later, the employee goes to the supervisor.

## Scene 3: A stronger version (1:05–1:45)
**Visual:** The supervisor (C6) and the case manager (C5) at a small table in an office.
**Case manager (C5):** I want the assignments reviewed, and I'm concerned that raising this will hurt my opportunities.
**Supervisor (C6):** We need to examine how "polished" became a selection criterion, who has benefited from that definition, and whether access to preparation and sponsorship has been equitable.
**Case manager (C5):** I'd like to know where that word came from, and what the records show.
**Narrator (C3):** The supervisor turns the conflict into an inquiry into structure. Possible actions include replacing vague judgments with job-related criteria, making opportunities visible, offering equitable preparation, clarifying who decides, and handling the reported conduct through the appropriate channels.

## Scene 4: A harmful version (1:45–2:10)
**Visual:** The team meeting room.
**Supervisor (C6):** This is obviously systemic racism, and anyone who questions that is part of the problem.
**Newer colleague (C7):** (afterward, to the case manager (C5)) I wanted to help look into it, but now I'm not sure I can ask anything.
**Narrator (C3):** It may name a real concern, but it treats a finding as proven before any inquiry, shuts out people who could help, and offers no workable change.

## Scene 5: What it is not, and what it means for equity (2:10–2:55)
**Visual:** On-screen list, read aloud: "Not transformational leadership. Not a structural explanation already proven. Not strong language without workable change."
**Narrator (C3):** Transformative leadership is not the same as transformational leadership. It is not treating a structural explanation as already proven, and it is not strong language without workable change. It promotes equity by making racism and institutional power legitimate subjects of analysis, by targeting durable rules and resource decisions, and by connecting individual concerns to patterns across the organization. It can undermine its own aims if it moves faster than the evidence, stalls without authority or budget, or becomes rhetoric without follow-through. The lesson rates its alignment with equity goals as high, and adds that alignment is not evidence of effectiveness.

## Closing (2:55–3:15)
**Visual:** The reflection question on screen, read aloud.
**Narrator (C3):** A question to consider on your own: What does it mean to investigate a structure without presuming the answer? The rest of this lesson, on this page, covers another conflict, strengths and risks, when to use this approach, safeguards, and a practice exercise.

## Transcript notes
Media manifest summary: A fictional team scenario contrasts a careful inquiry into how "polished" became a selection criterion with a response that declares the answer before any inquiry.
