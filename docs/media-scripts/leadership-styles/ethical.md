## Scene 1: Principled conduct and accountability (0:00–0:23)
**Visual:** Title card on a plain background: "Ethical leadership: principled conduct and accountability." It fades to an illustrated office.
**{{NARRATOR}}:** This lesson is about ethical leadership, which the pathway sums up as principled conduct and accountability. It emphasizes fair treatment and consistent standards for everyone. In the next few minutes, we'll see how this approach might respond to the common scenario, what it is not, and how it can help or harm equity and inclusion.

## Scene 2: The common scenario (0:23–1:00)
**Visual:** On-screen title: "Who gets the visible work?" The supervisor hands a presentation folder to one team member while {{CASE_MANAGER}} works through spreadsheets at a nearby desk. A short cut to a team meeting where another colleague, shown from behind, speaks over {{CASE_MANAGER}}.
**{{NARRATOR}}:** The pathway's fictional common scenario is called “Who gets the visible work?” A supervisor keeps giving high-visibility assignments to people they describe as polished, confident and ready to represent the team. {{CASE_MANAGER}}, a Black employee, points out that they get a lot of technical, behind-the-scenes work but fewer chances to present to leadership. In a team discussion, a colleague interrupts: “Everything doesn't have to be about race.” Later, {{CASE_MANAGER}} asks for the assignments to be reviewed and says they're worried that raising it will hurt their opportunities.

## Scene 3: A stronger version (1:00–1:32)
**Visual:** {{SUPERVISOR}} and {{CASE_MANAGER}} talk privately in a small meeting room. On-screen label: "A stronger version."
**{{SUPERVISOR}}:** The concern deserves a fair response. We need to address the dismissive interaction, protect appropriate reporting, and review how opportunity is distributed.
**{{CASE_MANAGER}}:** That covers what happened in the meeting and the assignments. Both of those matter to me.
**{{NARRATOR}}:** The leader names all three matters and commits to each. Protecting reporting means the formal part goes to the person or area responsible. Fairness here is something the leader does, not something the leader claims.

## Scene 4: A harmful version (1:32–2:01)
**Visual:** {{SUPERVISOR}} talks with {{PROGRAM_MANAGER}} at a desk with a closed laptop. On-screen label: "A harmful version."
**{{SUPERVISOR}}:** I'm a fair person, and I've never treated anyone differently. I don't see what there is to review.
**{{PROGRAM_MANAGER}}:** Nobody is questioning your intentions. The question is what the assignment record shows.
**{{NARRATOR}}:** The leader offers personal integrity as evidence about the process, and it is not. A fair person can run an unfair process without knowing it, and the assignment history may show a pattern the leader never intended.

## Scene 5: What it is not, and equity (2:01–2:41)
**Visual:** The laptop opens to a simple illustrated chart of who received visible assignments, drawn as unlabeled figures and bars. No text on screen.
**{{NARRATOR}}:** Ethical leadership is not “I'm a good person, so this must be fair,” and it is not applying standards only to people the leader is not close to. It brings clear attention to fairness, voice and accountability, with consistent standards that apply to powerful people too. Its weakness is that it can stay interpersonal and aspirational. Ethical leadership must reach beyond intentions to outcomes. A leader can sincerely believe they treat everyone the same while assignment, promotion or discipline patterns differ by race. Accountability means looking at those patterns and acting on them.

## Closing (2:41–3:01)
**Visual:** The reflection question on screen: "What does accountability look like after a fair-minded leader finds an unfair pattern?" Then the lesson page's section list.
**{{NARRATOR}}:** A question to consider on your own: what does accountability look like after a fair-minded leader finds an unfair pattern? The rest of this lesson, on this page, covers another conflict, strengths and risks, when to use this approach, complementary approaches and safeguards, and a practice exercise.
