# Video script: Neuroinclusive Work and Individualized Support

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=I12
- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 195 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Supervisor (C6, Co-host voice), Program manager (C2, Host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: Opening (0:00–0:22)
**Visual:** A title card reads "Neuroinclusive Work and Individualized Support." The narrator (C3) stands beside it in an open office area.
**Narrator (C3):** Knowing your work well does not help much when you cannot tell which request matters most. Neuroinclusive Work and Individualized Support prepares you for moments when instructions, work methods, or support arrangements become the obstacle, and the essential result needs to be told apart from the familiar way of reaching it.

## Scene 2: Priorities that did not carry over (0:22–0:56)
**Visual:** The program manager (C2), recently moved to a development assignment, stands at the desk of the supervisor (C6) on the new team. Several sticky notes with unreadable writing cover the edge of the program manager's notebook.
**Program manager (C2):** Can I check something with you? In my last role, I had written priorities every week. Here, requests come in all at once, and I'm finding it hard to tell what comes first.
**Supervisor (C6):** Things move fast on this team. Most people find their rhythm after a while.
**Program manager (C2):** I want to get this right. The work is getting harder to organize.
**Visual:** The supervisor (C6) leans back and considers.
**Narrator (C3):** The course pauses here and asks what this supervisor should examine first.

## Closing (0:56–1:27)
**Visual:** The narrator (C3) on camera in the open office area.
**Narrator (C3):** The course offers practice in making priorities, instructions, changes, and feedback understandable, and in respecting privacy while using the established accommodation process when it applies. It also asks whether support continues when a role, a supervisor, or a work setting changes. A question to take with you: in a task you assign or receive, which part is the essential result, and which part is simply the way it has usually been done?

## Transcript notes
A program manager on a new development assignment loses the written priorities that helped in an earlier role. An introduction to separating essential results from familiar methods and keeping support continuous.
