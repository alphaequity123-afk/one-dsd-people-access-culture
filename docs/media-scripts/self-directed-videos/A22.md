## Scene 1: Two matching totals (0:00–0:20)
**Visual:** Two practice budget proposals side by side, with identical totals at the bottom of each, as the narrator describes. The course title appears and is spoken. No real logos or real people.
**Narrator (C3):** Two proposals can carry the same total and still ask very different things of the people involved. Equity in Budgets, Rates, Contracts, and Procurement is an advanced self-directed course for the moment you examine a resource or purchasing decision and need to trace where opportunity goes and where unpaid work falls.

## Scene 2: A requirement and a smaller organization (0:20–0:55)
**Visual:** The community partner (C1) and the program manager (C2) at a small conference table with a practice contract proposal; its text is not readable.
**Community partner (C1):** We'd like to compete for this work. Our organization is small, though, and the proposal requires a large administrative office.
**Program manager (C2):** That requirement is there for a reason. The work needs reliable reporting.
**Community partner (C1):** We understand that, and we take reporting seriously. In our response, we described a reporting arrangement we think would work without that office.
**Program manager (C2):** I've read it. I can't set the reporting safeguard aside, and I also have to decide what to do with what you've proposed.

## Closing (0:55–1:30)
**Visual:** The course title card, then the first slide of the course.
**Narrator (C3):** The course helps you trace where resources and opportunity go, and where uncompensated burden falls, across budgets, rates, contracts, and procurement. You'll identify a participation barrier inside a proposed requirement, compare allocation or selection options using clear criteria and fitting evidence, and prepare an equity explanation that keeps actual budget and procurement responsibilities in place. Take this question with you: in a requirement you know, what is it meant to protect, and how would you know whether it does?
