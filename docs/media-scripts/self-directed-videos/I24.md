# Video script: Comparing Policy Options and Possible Unintended Effects

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=I24
- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 215 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Supervisor (C6, Co-host voice), Case manager (C5, Host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning. The only on-screen text is the course title, which the narrator speaks; documents and screens shown in the video carry no legible text.

## Scene 1: A good purpose and what it does (0:00–0:20)
**Visual:** The narrator (C3) beside a planning board with a calendar grid, one block shaded and no legible text. A title card shows the course title.
**Narrator (C3):** A good purpose can make a proposal feel finished before anyone has traced what it actually does. Comparing Policy Options and Possible Unintended Effects prepares you to weigh alternatives with the same questions, so effects nobody intended have a chance to come into view.

## Scene 2: One learning period for everyone (0:20–1:00)
**Visual:** The supervisor (C6) and the case manager (C5) stand at the planning board; the supervisor points to the shaded block.
**Supervisor (C6):** I'd like the whole team in one common learning period. Shared learning works better when we're all in the room together.
**Case manager (C5):** I agree with the purpose. The trouble is that I cover urgent public contacts during that time, and so do others on the team. Someone has to answer.
**Supervisor (C6):** One period keeps it simple, and most people can make it.
**Case manager (C5):** Then the people on urgent contacts miss the session, and the discussion that comes after it, each time.
**Supervisor (C6):** Before I send the plan around, I need to work out how to weigh this.
**Visual:** The supervisor (C6) looks at the shaded block. No plan is shown.

## Closing (1:00–1:30)
**Visual:** The narrator (C3) at the same planning board.
**Narrator (C3):** The course shows how to keep a proposal's intended purpose separate from its possible effects. It helps you compare two or three bounded alternatives with the same evidence and assumptions, consider burdens, resources, access and affected perspectives alongside costs, and recommend an option with safeguards and honest limits while formal legislative and fiscal roles stay distinct. As you start, consider who, in a choice your team is weighing, might carry a cost that the proposal does not mention.
