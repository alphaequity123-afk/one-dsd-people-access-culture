# Video script: Applying the Equity Analysis Toolkit to a Realistic Decision

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.

- **Length:** about 1.6 minutes (about 221 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Program manager (C2, Host voice), Community partner (C1, Co-host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning. The only on-screen text is the course title, which the narrator speaks; documents and screens shown in the video carry no legible text.

## Scene 1: Tidier for some, harder for others (0:00–0:25)
**Visual:** The narrator (C3) beside a laptop showing a simple web form mock-up with no agency logo and no legible text. A title card shows the course title.
**Narrator (C3):** A change can make work tidier for staff and harder for someone else at the same time. Applying the Equity Analysis Toolkit to a Realistic Decision walks through one invented practice proposal, a single online page for information requests, and prepares you to examine a change like that before anyone calls it finished.

## Scene 2: A line that says help is available (0:25–1:00)
**Visual:** The program manager (C2) and the community partner (C1) look at the practice request page mock-up together.
**Community partner (C1):** I tried the page with a few people I work with. They could read it, but there's no place on it to ask a question.
**Program manager (C2):** We heard that concern earlier, so we added a line near the bottom. It says help is available.
**Community partner (C1):** When someone sees that line, who do they reach, and how?
**Program manager (C2):** We haven't worked that part out yet. The line is there, though, and the concern has been acknowledged. I'd like to list it as resolved at Friday's review.
**Community partner (C1):** Is that where we are?
**Visual:** The program manager (C2) looks back at the screen. No decision is shown.

## Closing (1:00–1:35)
**Visual:** The narrator (C3) in the same office setting.
**Narrator (C3):** The course starts by defining what is being proposed, what it is meant to achieve and what is actually required. It then examines who may benefit, face burdens or be missing, compares alternatives using evidence, community perspectives and explicit uncertainty, and prepares a supported recommendation within the relevant review process. The toolkit supports your reasoning; it does not replace a required analysis or approval. As you begin, consider which promised result in a proposal you know has not yet been shown.
