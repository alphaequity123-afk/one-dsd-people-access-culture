# Video script: Person-Centered Thinking in Public Decisions

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=F11
- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 205 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Supervisor (C6, Co-host voice), Program manager (C2, Host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: Decisions made far from the person (0:00–0:22)
**Visual:** A plain title card reading “Person-Centered Thinking in Public Decisions,” which fades to an illustrated office where a form is being drafted.
**Narrator (C3):** Many public decisions are shaped far from the person who will live with them. Person-Centered Thinking in Public Decisions prepares you for the moments when a form, an instruction, or an internal choice is being drafted, and a person’s goals, preferences, and chosen support can either stay visible or drop out.

## Scene 2: Which question comes first (0:22–0:51)
**Visual:** The supervisor (C6) and the program manager (C2) look over a draft form on a tablet. The fields are outlined, with no readable text.
**Supervisor (C6):** In this draft, people pick a service from the list first. What they want their week to include comes on the next page.
**Program manager (C2):** The service list is the information the process needs, so it made sense to put it up front.
**Supervisor (C6):** All right. We need to finalize it this week.
**Narrator (C3):** The order of the questions is about to be set. Which revision would best support a person-centered conversation?

## Closing (0:51–1:28)
**Visual:** The closing question appears on screen as the narrator reads it: “What does the first question on a form you use invite people to talk about?”
**Narrator (C3):** The course explains how person-centered thinking starts with a person’s goals, preferences, and chosen support, and how that can guide public decisions. It helps you trace how a form, instruction, or internal decision can affect daily life, distinguish a choice meaningful to the person from one defined solely by available services, and ask how the person’s voice can inform a decision within your role. A question to carry into the course: what does the first question on a form you use invite people to talk about?

## Transcript notes
A short introduction to Person-Centered Thinking in Public Decisions: a draft form asks people to choose a service before describing their week, and it is about to be finalized. The video stops at the decision.
