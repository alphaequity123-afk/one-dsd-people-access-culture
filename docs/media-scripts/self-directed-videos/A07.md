# Video script: Whose Knowledge Shapes the Decision?

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=A07
- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 218 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Program manager (C2, Host voice), Newer colleague (C7, Co-host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: Heard differently (0:00–0:20)
**Visual:** A team meeting room after a meeting, with a whiteboard listing several proposals. The course title appears on screen as the narrator (C3) speaks it.
**Narrator (C3):** Every decision depends on accounts that someone receives, writes down, and then either weighs or sets aside. Whose Knowledge Shapes the Decision? prepares you for the moment when the same idea or concern is heard differently depending on who says it, and the difference carries into what happens next.

## Scene 2: Where the idea started (0:20–0:55)
**Visual:** The newer colleague (C7) and the program manager (C2) stay behind as others leave. The meeting notes are on a wall screen.
**Newer colleague (C7):** Early in the meeting, I proposed changing how we hand off requests between teams, and the discussion moved on.
**Program manager (C2):** Then a senior colleague raised the same change near the end, and the group agreed to try it. The notes credit the senior colleague.
**Newer colleague (C7):** I’m glad it’s going forward. I just wonder whether anyone will remember where it started when development assignments come around.
**Program manager (C2):** That’s a fair thing to wonder about. I’m not sure yet what the right response would be.
**Visual:** The program manager (C2) looks at the meeting notes on the screen. The scene ends there.

## Closing (0:55–1:35)
**Visual:** The narrator (C3) in the meeting room. The closing question appears on screen as the narrator speaks it.
**Narrator (C3):** The course helps you recognize a credibility discount, when someone’s account is given less weight in how it is received or recorded, and explain how the available categories can leave an experience undescribed. You will design a route through which an affected person’s contribution can influence a decision, and recommend a repair that preserves their meaning and ownership of the account. A question to carry in: when has a title, confident delivery, or familiar language made an account easier for you to accept, and what evidence did you examine?

## Transcript notes
A short introduction: a newer colleague’s proposal gains attention only after a senior colleague repeats it and receives the credit, and a program manager stops at what the right response would be.
