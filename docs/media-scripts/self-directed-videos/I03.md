# Video script: Cultural Humility in Professional Judgment

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=I03
- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 216 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Program manager (C2, Host voice), Community partner (C1, Co-host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: Opening (0:00–0:21)
**Visual:** A title card reads "Cultural Humility in Professional Judgment." The narrator (C3) stands beside it in a plain meeting room.
**Narrator (C3):** Knowing a process well is valuable, and it can also make a barrier harder to see. Cultural Humility in Professional Judgment prepares you for moments when your expertise and another person's experience point in different directions, and you need to use what you know while staying open to correction.

## Scene 2: A proposal that seems efficient (0:21–1:03)
**Visual:** The program manager (C2) and the community partner (C1) sit at a meeting table. A laptop shows a plain mock-up of a web page, with no real site or logo.
**Program manager (C2):** Our plan is to move the service explanation online. It's faster to update, and people can read it whenever they need it.
**Community partner (C1):** The people I hear from have concerns about that. It assumes everyone has a way to get online and already knows where to look.
**Program manager (C2):** We looked at this carefully, though. It's the most efficient option we have.
**Community partner (C1):** I understand that. I'm telling you who it may leave out.
**Narrator (C3):** Both points are now on the table. The course pauses here and asks what response would best improve the judgment.

## Closing (1:03–1:36)
**Visual:** The narrator (C3) on camera. The mock-up page is visible, out of focus, behind them.
**Narrator (C3):** In the course, you look at how professional authority changes the choices available in an interaction, and you compare a familiar interpretation with the person's own explanation and the relevant evidence. You also practice choosing a response that keeps participation meaningful while leaving decisions with the people actually responsible for them. One question to carry in: in your own work, whose account would you need to hear before you could say a process is easy to use?

## Transcript notes
A community partner raises an access concern about a program manager's preferred online service explanation. An introduction to using professional knowledge while staying open to correction.
