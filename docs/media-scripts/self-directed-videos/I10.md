# Video script: Supporting Informed Choice and Person-Centered Planning

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=I10
- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 195 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Supervisor (C6, Co-host voice), Case manager (C5, Host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: Opening (0:00–0:21)
**Visual:** A title card reads "Supporting Informed Choice and Person-Centered Planning." The narrator (C3) stands beside it in a quiet office.
**Narrator (C3):** Planning goes better when a person's own direction shapes the options, rather than the options deciding the direction. Supporting Informed Choice and Person-Centered Planning prepares you for moments in services, provider support, and supervision when the choice needs to be clear, the realistic options explained, and the uncertainty named.

## Scene 2: A development path chosen in advance (0:21–0:55)
**Visual:** The supervisor (C6) and the case manager (C5) meet at a small table. The supervisor holds a printed list of development projects with one item circled.
**Supervisor (C6):** I've set aside the lighter development project for you. I thought it would be the most comfortable fit.
**Case manager (C5):** Oh. We haven't talked about development yet.
**Supervisor (C6):** I know. I didn't want to put too much on your plate.
**Visual:** The case manager (C5) looks at the circled item.
**Narrator (C3):** The supervisor has assumed that an employee with a disability wants a less demanding path, and the employee's own goals have not been discussed. The course stops here and asks what the best response would be.

## Closing (0:55–1:27)
**Visual:** The narrator (C3) on camera in the quiet office.
**Narrator (C3):** The course gives you practice keeping a person's direction distinct from family, staff, or organizational preferences, and recognizing how cultural meaning, communication, and available supports affect participation. It also helps you spot planning routines that narrow the options before a person has a chance to consider them. A question to take with you: when someone says "that sounds fine," how would you know whether they are acknowledging an offer or making a choice?

## Transcript notes
A supervisor sets aside a lighter development project before hearing an employee's goals. An introduction to clarifying choices and keeping a person's own direction at the center of planning.
