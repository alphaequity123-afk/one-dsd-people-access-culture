# Video script: How Routine Rules Can Create Systemic Inequities

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=F22
- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 226 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Community partner (C1, Co-host voice), Case manager (C5, Host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: Behind a late response (0:00–0:19)
**Visual:** Title card on a plain background: "How Routine Rules Can Create Systemic Inequities." It fades to an illustrated record with one entry stamped "Late." The word is read in the narration.
**Narrator (C3):** When someone misses a deadline, the record usually shows only the final step: a response marked late. How Routine Rules Can Create Systemic Inequities prepares you to look at the path that led there, through the requirements, handoffs and information people had to work with.

## Scene 2: Two names for one document (0:19–1:01)
**Visual:** The community partner (C1) and the case manager (C5) talk on a video call. Two illustrated documents appear side by side, one labeled "Verification summary" and one labeled "Confirmation letter." Both labels are spoken in the dialogue.
**Community partner (C1):** A person I'm helping got your notice asking for a “verification summary” by the deadline. The other office says they don't have anything by that name. What they issue is a “confirmation letter.”
**Case manager (C5):** That's probably the same document. Did they request it?
**Community partner (C1):** They did, once they worked out the mismatch. But that used part of the response window, and now they're waiting on the letter.
**Case manager (C5):** When responses come in late, our team usually just records them as late. Hearing this, I'm not sure where to start.
**Narrator (C3):** The course pauses here and asks what a careful response would examine.

## Closing (1:01–1:37)
**Visual:** The reflection question on screen: "Where might a late or missing response be telling you something about the steps before it?" Then the course's title card.
**Narrator (C3):** In this course, you'll learn how routine rules can create systemic inequity through connected rules, information and authority, and through effects that keep recurring over time. You'll practice tracing how an internal decision travels through a public-service process, recognizing when small burdens accumulate for the same people, and identifying a possible mechanism along with the evidence needed to examine it. A question to carry in with you: where might a late or missing response be telling you something about the steps before it?
