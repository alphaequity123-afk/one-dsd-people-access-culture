- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 216 spoken words)

## Scene 1: Reliable work and the next assignment (0:00–0:20)
**Visual:** The narrator (C3) beside a wall screen showing an assignment grid with no legible names. A title card shows the course title.
**Narrator (C3):** Being reliable at one kind of work can quietly decide which work you are offered next. Making Development, Mentoring, and Sponsorship More Accessible prepares you for conversations about growth, and for noticing how development opportunities actually reach the people on a team.

## Scene 2: Another round of coordination (0:20–1:00)
**Visual:** The case manager (C5), acting as lead worker, and the newer colleague (C7) look at the shared assignment grid together.
**Case manager (C5):** Thanks again for running the meeting logistics last month. Everything went smoothly, so I've put you down for coordination on the next three sessions.
**Newer colleague (C7):** I can do that. I did mention that I'd like practice with legislative analysis, though. The comparison work seems to go to the same people each time.
**Case manager (C5):** You're so good at coordination that I assumed you liked it. And the comparisons are already a heavy load for the people doing them.
**Newer colleague (C7):** I'd still like a chance to try, with some support.
**Case manager (C5):** Let me think about how to answer that before I finalize the list.
**Visual:** The case manager (C5) looks back at the grid. No change is shown.

## Closing (1:00–1:35)
**Visual:** The narrator (C3) in the same office setting.
**Narrator (C3):** The course helps you tell mentoring, advocacy, supervision and formal selection apart, so development opportunities become clearer and more accessible. It covers making an opportunity visible and usable before anyone is chosen, comparing demonstrated capability with relevant criteria and available support, and examining how repeated support work or hidden workload can restrict growth. As you begin, consider what you would need to know about a colleague's goal and current workload before deciding which work comes next.
