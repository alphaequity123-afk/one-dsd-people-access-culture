# Video script: Connecting Equity Goals, Learning, and Everyday Practice

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=F24
- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 229 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Program manager (C2, Host voice), Supervisor (C6, Co-host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: From direction to daily work (0:00–0:24)
**Visual:** Title card on a plain background: "Connecting Equity Goals, Learning, and Everyday Practice." It fades to an illustrated planning board with one card reading "Improve communication and accessibility," which the narrator speaks.
**Narrator (C3):** A goal like “improve communication and accessibility” points in a useful direction, but it doesn't yet say what anyone will examine or what readers need. Connecting Equity Goals, Learning, and Everyday Practice prepares you to link a goal to ordinary work you can describe, and to be honest about what that work can and cannot promise.

## Scene 2: Measuring a clearer notice (0:24–1:00)
**Visual:** The program manager (C2) and the supervisor (C6) review a revised service-change notice on a shared screen. Beside it, a note reads "Success: fewer questions," which is spoken in the dialogue.
**Program manager (C2):** The revised notice is clearer, and we wrote down fewer questions as our sign of success.
**Supervisor (C6):** A participant passed along something you should hear. They said the notice reads better, but they still can't tell how to respond.
**Program manager (C2):** Then fewer questions might not mean what we hoped. People may not have known how to ask.
**Supervisor (C6):** So what do we do with the outcome we wrote down?
**Narrator (C3):** The course pauses here, with that outcome question still open, and offers three ways to approach it.

## Closing (1:00–1:38)
**Visual:** The reflection question on screen: "For one piece of your work, what would people need to experience for it to count as progress?" Then the course's title card.
**Narrator (C3):** In this course, you'll get to know the six ADSA equity goal areas and see how they relate to DSD work. You'll practice explaining a logic model through what informs the work, what people do, what they produce and the change they seek, connecting a relevant learning activity with an everyday question, and distinguishing a proposed contribution from a commitment that needs someone else's decision. A question to carry in with you: for one piece of your work, what would people need to experience for it to count as progress?

## Transcript notes
A short introduction: a program manager and a supervisor learn that a clearer notice still leaves a reader unsure how to respond, and reconsider how they defined success.
