# Video script: Racial Advantage and the Everyday Gates to Opportunity

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=I07
- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 194 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Supervisor (C6, Co-host voice), Case manager (C5, Host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: Opening (0:00–0:23)
**Visual:** A title card reads "Racial Advantage and the Everyday Gates to Opportunity." The narrator (C3) stands beside it in an office corridor.
**Narrator (C3):** Opportunities at work rarely arrive through one front door. Racial Advantage and the Everyday Gates to Opportunity prepares you to look at the ordinary routes, such as who hears about an opening and how early experience later counts as readiness, while recognizing that advantage can coexist with disability, financial hardship, or other barriers.

## Scene 2: A conversation in the hallway (0:23–0:57)
**Visual:** The supervisor (C6) stops the case manager (C5) in a hallway. No one else is present.
**Supervisor (C6):** Do you have a minute? The acting lead role opens up Monday while the lead is out. I thought of you right away. Interested?
**Case manager (C5):** Absolutely. Thank you for thinking of me.
**Visual:** The hallway fades to a meeting room. A calendar on the wall now shows a later season.
**Supervisor (C6):** For the promotion, the case manager stands out. They've already done the acting lead role.
**Narrator (C3):** Months later, that informal offer has become evidence of readiness. The course pauses here and asks what would best examine the pathway that led to it.

## Closing (0:57–1:26)
**Visual:** The narrator (C3) on camera in the corridor.
**Narrator (C3):** In the course, you trace how familiar networks and credibility judgments shape access to information and opportunity, and you compare a stated merit standard with the chances people actually had to demonstrate it. You also work on a supported improvement without assuming that every difference proves racial discrimination. One question to carry in: when someone is described as ready, what opportunities made it possible for them to show it?

## Transcript notes
An acting assignment offered in a hallway conversation is later cited as promotion readiness. An introduction to examining the everyday routes that shape who gains experience and opportunity.
