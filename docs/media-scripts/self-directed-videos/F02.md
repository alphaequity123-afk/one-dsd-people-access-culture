# Video script: Cultural Humility in Everyday Public Service

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=F02
- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 207 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Program manager (C2, Host voice), Newer colleague (C7, Co-host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: A small moment with lasting effects (0:00–0:24)
**Visual:** A plain title card reading “Cultural Humility in Everyday Public Service,” which fades to an illustrated office hallway outside a meeting room.
**Narrator (C3):** Some of the most lasting moments in public service are small ones, such as how a team reads a new colleague who works differently. Cultural Humility in Everyday Public Service prepares you to notice your own assumptions, and the influence you hold, when one familiar style starts to look like the only proof of commitment.

## Scene 2: Comments after the meeting (0:24–0:57)
**Visual:** The newer colleague (C7) and the program manager (C2) talk in the hallway as other staff leave the meeting room behind them.
**Newer colleague (C7):** I didn’t say much in there. I’d like to send my comments this afternoon, once I’ve had a chance to think them through.
**Program manager (C2):** You can do that, but I’ll be honest with you. Around here, people who are committed speak up in the meeting.
**Newer colleague (C7):** I didn’t realize that was the expectation. Nobody mentioned it when I started.
**Program manager (C2):** It’s just how things work on this team.
**Narrator (C3):** The written comments arrive later that afternoon. What would clarify the expectation fairly?

## Closing (0:57–1:29)
**Visual:** The closing question appears on screen as the narrator reads it: “Which of your own working habits might a newcomer experience as an unwritten rule?”
**Narrator (C3):** The course helps you receive a correction without expecting the other person to teach or reassure you, and use information about a community as preparation rather than as a personal profile. It also helps you recognize how a routine can keep a staff member’s interpretation in place after the person has challenged it. A question to carry into the course: which of your own working habits might a newcomer experience as an unwritten rule?

## Transcript notes
A short introduction to Cultural Humility in Everyday Public Service: a new colleague asks to send written comments after a meeting and hears that committed staff speak up in the room. The video stops at the decision.
