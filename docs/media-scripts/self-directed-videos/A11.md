# Video script: Accessible Technology and Learning from the Start

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=A11
- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 218 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Program manager (C2, Host voice), Newer colleague (C7, Co-host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: Decided early (0:00–0:25)
**Visual:** A screen shows a polished software demonstration, with a mouse pointer moving through each step. The course title appears on screen as the narrator (C3) speaks it.
**Narrator (C3):** Whether people can use a tool or a lesson is often shaped early, at the point where it is chosen or built. Accessible Technology and Learning from the Start prepares you for those decisions, and for the moment a polished demonstration or a reassuring report seems to answer a question it never examined.

## Scene 2: The sorting activity (0:25–0:55)
**Visual:** The newer colleague (C7) and the program manager (C2) look at an onboarding module on a laptop. The final activity shows cards to be dragged into categories.
**Newer colleague (C7):** I’ve read every page of the onboarding module on service barriers, but the final activity only accepts answers if I drag cards into categories, and I can’t operate it.
**Program manager (C2):** That activity is how we check whether people have understood the subject.
**Newer colleague (C7):** I did understand it. I just can’t show that by dragging cards.
**Program manager (C2):** Thank you for telling me. I want to handle this well, so let me think about what we should offer.
**Visual:** The program manager (C2) scrolls back to the start of the module. The scene ends there.

## Closing (0:55–1:35)
**Visual:** The narrator (C3) beside the laptop. The closing question appears on screen as the narrator speaks it.
**Narrator (C3):** The course gives you practice describing an access need as part of the task people are meant to complete, not as an optional feature, and comparing evidence of usable access with a vendor’s or author’s general assurance. You will identify how a selection or design decision affects later access and support, and prepare a proposed requirement with a way to examine its use with affected people. A question to carry in: what would “easy to use” mean if you wrote it as a task of entering, correcting, submitting, and understanding a response?

## Transcript notes
A short introduction: a newer colleague has read an onboarding module but cannot operate its drag-and-drop final activity, and a program manager stops to consider what to offer.
