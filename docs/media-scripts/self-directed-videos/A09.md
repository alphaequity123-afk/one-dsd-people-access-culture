# Video script: From Individual Access Fixes to Accessible Systems

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=A09
- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 204 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Supervisor (C6, Co-host voice), Program manager (C2, Host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: Each part reviewed, the whole task missed (0:00–0:20)
**Visual:** A wall chart of a hiring process, with separate sections for recruitment and onboarding. The course title appears on screen as the narrator (C3) speaks it.
**Narrator (C3):** A page can work well and the very next step can still stop a person from going any further. From Individual Access Fixes to Accessible Systems prepares you for the moment you realize that each team reviewed its own part, and no one reviewed the whole task the person had to complete.

## Scene 2: The posting and the interview tool (0:20–0:45)
**Visual:** The program manager (C2) and the supervisor (C6) stand at the wall chart. A blank sticky note marks the interview step.
**Program manager (C2):** The accessibility check on our vacancy page came back clean, so recruitment looks ready to go.
**Supervisor (C6):** A candidate just told us they couldn’t use the timed interview tool with a keyboard, so they couldn’t finish their interview.
**Program manager (C2):** That’s the same tool new staff use during onboarding.
**Supervisor (C6):** Then what should our review actually compare?
**Visual:** Both look along the chart from the recruitment section to the onboarding section. The scene ends on the question.

## Closing (0:45–1:25)
**Visual:** The narrator (C3) beside the wall chart. The closing question appears on screen as the narrator speaks it.
**Narrator (C3):** The course gives you practice mapping barriers across a complete journey rather than a single document, and telling an immediate access response apart from a change that prevents the barrier from repeating. You will compare priorities using impact, reach, urgency, and feasibility, and explain how the people who use a process will help examine a proposed change. Individual employment accommodation stays with its own process, and the course keeps that distinction clear. As you begin, think of a task where you needed extra help: where did the official route begin and end, and what did you do outside it?
