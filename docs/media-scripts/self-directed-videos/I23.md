# Video script: Finding Barriers Across Service Procedures and Handoffs

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=I23
- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 213 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Supervisor (C6, Co-host voice), Program manager (C2, Host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning. The only on-screen text is the course title, which the narrator speaks; documents and screens shown in the video carry no legible text.

## Scene 1: Back at the start (0:00–0:20)
**Visual:** The narrator (C3) beside a whiteboard with a hand-drawn route of boxes and arrows, unlabeled. A title card shows the course title.
**Narrator (C3):** A person can do what a notice asks and still end up back at the start. Finding Barriers Across Service Procedures and Handoffs prepares you to look past a single form or office and trace every step someone has to complete to get through a service.

## Scene 2: One item, two names (0:20–1:00)
**Visual:** The supervisor (C6) and the program manager (C2) stand at the whiteboard route. The program manager points to two boxes.
**Supervisor (C6):** The receiving desk sent the person back again for the supporting item. That's the second time.
**Program manager (C2):** From what the person described, they brought what the notice asked for. The notice and the receiving role just seem to use different names for it.
**Supervisor (C6):** Our log still shows the item as missing. Someone has suggested we record that the person refused to engage.
**Program manager (C2):** Whoever reads that record later will only see that note, not this conversation.
**Supervisor (C6):** So I need to decide what we do about this one, and what goes in the record.
**Visual:** The two look at the boxes on the whiteboard. No decision is shown.

## Closing (1:00–1:30)
**Visual:** The narrator (C3) at the same whiteboard.
**Narrator (C3):** Barriers often hide in the handoffs between steps, so the course follows the whole route rather than one form in isolation. It helps you tell an actual requirement from an added local practice or assumption, identify accumulated burdens in documents, contact routes and handoffs, and compare a feasible revision while keeping accuracy and actual responsibilities intact. As you begin, consider where, in a process you know, one office's finished step becomes someone else's unclear next step.
