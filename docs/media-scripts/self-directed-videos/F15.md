# Video script: Language Access and the Foundations of CLAS

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=F15
- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 216 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Newer colleague (C7, Co-host voice), Case manager (C5, Host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: Planning instead of improvising (0:00–0:24)
**Visual:** Title card on a plain background: "Language Access and the Foundations of CLAS." It fades to an illustrated meeting room with two chairs on one side of a table and a calendar on the wall.
**Narrator (C3):** Some conversations at work carry real consequences for the person on the other side of the table, and they only work if that person can understand the information in the language and format they need. Language Access and the Foundations of CLAS prepares you to plan for that, rather than improvise once the meeting starts.

## Scene 2: An afternoon meeting (0:24–1:02)
**Visual:** The newer colleague (C7) and the case manager (C5) stand by a shared calendar showing an afternoon service discussion. A note beside the entry is marked with a question mark where language assistance would be listed.
**Newer colleague (C7):** The service discussion is this afternoon, and nobody has confirmed language assistance. We don't have much time booked, either.
**Case manager (C5):** The person's family member usually comes along. Someone suggested we just ask them to interpret.
**Newer colleague (C7):** That would be quicker. But it's a detailed conversation about services, and I'm not sure what it means for the person's privacy, or for their own questions.
**Case manager (C5):** Then let's think it through before we decide. What would preparing this conversation actually involve?
**Narrator (C3):** The course leaves that decision with you and offers three ways to prepare.

## Closing (1:02–1:33)
**Visual:** The reflection question on screen: "When you last explained something important, how did you know the person could use it?" Then the course's title card.
**Narrator (C3):** In this course, you'll see why understandable, respectful communication is central to services people can actually use. You'll distinguish translation, interpretation and checking understanding, recognize that communication support involves preparation, time and qualified assistance, and meet the connection the National CLAS Standards draw among communication, workforce support and community-informed improvement. A question to carry in with you: when you last explained something important, how did you know the person could use it?

## Transcript notes
A short introduction: two colleagues face a detailed service discussion with no confirmed language assistance and consider what preparing it would involve.
