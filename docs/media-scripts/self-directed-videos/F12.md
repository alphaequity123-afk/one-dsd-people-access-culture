# Video script: Choice, Self-Direction, and a Life Beyond Services

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=F12
- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 203 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Supervisor (C6, Co-host voice), Case manager (C5, Host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: A full schedule (0:00–0:22)
**Visual:** A plain title card reading “Choice, Self-Direction, and a Life Beyond Services,” which fades to an illustrated desk with a weekly calendar on a monitor.
**Narrator (C3):** A full schedule can look like a good result while a person’s own goal goes unmet. Choice, Self-Direction, and a Life Beyond Services prepares you for the moments when offering support could slide into deciding for someone, or when the familiar option begins to stand in for the life a person wants.

## Scene 2: The music option (0:22–0:49)
**Visual:** The case manager (C5) and the supervisor (C6) sit at a desk with a weekly activity calendar on the monitor. No text on the calendar is readable.
**Case manager (C5):** The person I support told me they want to play music with a community group. It’s the goal they keep coming back to.
**Supervisor (C6):** We already have the music listening activity on the schedule. That’s the music option we offer.
**Case manager (C5):** So you’d start there?
**Supervisor (C6):** It’s what we have available right now.
**Narrator (C3):** The schedule holds one familiar option. What would best support a meaningful comparison?

## Closing (0:49–1:27)
**Visual:** The closing question appears on screen as the narrator reads it: “When you describe a good week for someone you support, whose idea of good are you using?”
**Narrator (C3):** The course explains self-direction and supported decision-making, and why offering support should never be confused with taking control of someone’s choices or life. It helps you distinguish a service delivered from an outcome that matters in the person’s life, explore strengths, relationships, and desired experiences beyond a service list, and recognize when a routine treats one familiar arrangement as the safe or normal option. A question to carry into the course: when you describe a good week for someone you support, whose idea of good are you using?

## Transcript notes
A short introduction to Choice, Self-Direction, and a Life Beyond Services: a person wants to play music with a community group, and staff point to the familiar listening activity. The video stops at the decision.
