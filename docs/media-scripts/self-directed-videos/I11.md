# Video script: Making Meetings and Learning Materials Usable

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=I11
- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 201 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Program manager (C2, Host voice), Newer colleague (C7, Co-host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: Opening (0:00–0:19)
**Visual:** A title card reads "Making Meetings and Learning Materials Usable." The narrator (C3) stands beside it at the back of an empty training room.
**Narrator (C3):** Being in a meeting is not the same as being able to take part in it. Making Meetings and Learning Materials Usable prepares you to plan access across every stage of a meeting or learning session, from the invitation and materials through participation and follow-up.

## Scene 2: A draft shared only as a picture (0:19–1:00)
**Visual:** A process meeting. The shared screen shows the draft as a single flat image of a page. The program manager (C2) leads the meeting; the newer colleague (C7) sits at the table with a laptop.
**Newer colleague (C7):** Before we compare the two options, I should say that the draft came through only as an image. I can't use it to work through the comparison.
**Program manager (C2):** I'm sorry about that. We need everyone's choice in about ten minutes, though, so we can wrap up.
**Newer colleague (C7):** I do have thoughts on the second option. I just can't use this version to check them.
**Narrator (C3):** The newer colleague is in the room, but the comparison is out of reach. The course pauses here and asks what response would best preserve their contribution.

## Closing (1:00–1:29)
**Visual:** The narrator (C3) on camera in the training room.
**Narrator (C3):** In the course, you check whether captions, visuals, and document structure preserve important meaning, and you practice responding to an access failure without placing the whole burden on the person who was excluded. You also compare attendance with the ability to understand and influence a discussion. One question to carry in: in a meeting you run, what would show you that someone's reasoning actually reached the decision?

## Transcript notes
A newer colleague cannot use an image-only draft during a meeting that compares two options. An introduction to planning meetings and learning materials that people can use and contribute to.
