# Video script: Accessible Communication for the Person and the Task

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=I09
- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 208 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Case manager (C5, Host voice), Newer colleague (C7, Co-host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: Opening (0:00–0:24)
**Visual:** A title card reads "Accessible Communication for the Person and the Task." The narrator (C3) stands beside it in an open office area.
**Narrator (C3):** Information helps only when the person can receive it, respond to it, and use it afterward. Accessible Communication for the Person and the Task prepares you for moments when a notice, an instruction, or a recording does not work for the person in front of you, and the method needs to change without losing the meaning.

## Scene 2: An orientation packet that does not work (0:24–1:02)
**Visual:** The newer colleague (C7) sits at a desk with a thick printed instruction sheet and a laptop showing a paused orientation recording. The case manager (C5), who is helping with the newer colleague's onboarding, pulls up a chair.
**Newer colleague (C7):** I've been through the orientation packet twice. The instruction sheet is very dense, and the recorded orientation is hard for me to use. I'm worried I'm missing things I need for the job.
**Case manager (C5):** Thanks for telling me. Everyone on the team started with this same packet.
**Newer colleague (C7):** I know. I want to do the work right. I just can't get what I need from it in this form.
**Narrator (C3):** The course pauses here and asks what response would best support this person's onboarding.

## Closing (1:02–1:32)
**Visual:** The narrator (C3) on camera. The packet from the scene is visible on a desk behind them.
**Narrator (C3):** In the course, you recognize that language and disability access needs can occur together, and you practice keeping essential choices and information intact when you change a format or the pace. You also examine who is left out when one communication method becomes the default. One question to carry in: if one format is the only way your team shares a key instruction, who might it leave without a usable route?

## Transcript notes
A newer colleague says a dense instruction sheet and recorded orientation are hard to use. An introduction to choosing communication that the person can receive, respond to, and use for the task.
