- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 230 spoken words)

- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: Advice built on earlier words (0:00–0:25)
**Visual:** A printed provision with one paragraph marked, beside a dated agency update; neither text is readable. The course title appears and is spoken. No real logos or real people.
**Narrator (C3):** A recommendation can rest on words the law no longer contains. Policy and Legislative Analysis through an Equity Lens is an advanced self-directed course for DSD staff and leaders whose work involves policy, legislation, fiscal analysis, or implementation. It prepares you for the moment you turn a provision into advice someone can use.

## Scene 2: The practice brief (0:25–1:10)
**Visual:** The newer colleague (C7) and the program manager (C2) at a desk with a draft brief.
**Newer colleague (C7):** I finished the practice brief on Waiver Reimagine. My timing conclusion is built on the fixed date in the 2025 provision.
**Program manager (C2):** Have you seen the 2026 amendment? It replaces that fixed date in paragraph (g) with advance information tied to when a person's services become subject to the budget, and adds phased implementation.
**Newer colleague (C7):** I haven't. Is there an implementation update as well?
**Program manager (C2):** DHS posted one in July 2026. It says a new launch date had not been determined.
**Newer colleague (C7):** The purpose is still informed choice, though. So how much of my brief do I need to revise?
**Program manager (C2):** That's the call in front of you.

## Closing (1:10–1:40)
**Visual:** The course title card, then the first slide of the course.
**Narrator (C3):** Using real Minnesota records, the course helps you distinguish policy, legislative, bill, and fiscal analysis, read an actual provision apart from its implementation and observed results, and use community knowledge, staff experience, and evidence to compare alternatives. You'll also write and revise a concise recommendation whose equity reasoning is visible in the work itself. As you begin, ask: when the text behind your advice changes, how will you notice which conclusions depended on it?
