# Video script: Psychological Safety, Respect, and Speaking Up

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=F19
- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 208 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Newer colleague (C7, Co-host voice), Case manager (C5, Host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: The next few seconds (0:00–0:19)
**Visual:** Title card on a plain background: "Psychological Safety, Respect, and Speaking Up." It fades to an illustrated open office with several desks and colleagues at work.
**Narrator (C3):** Every team has moments when someone asks a question, voices uncertainty or admits a mistake. What happens in the next few seconds can shape whether they, or anyone listening, will speak up next time. Psychological Safety, Respect, and Speaking Up prepares you for that response.

## Scene 2: Two instructions (0:19–0:52)
**Visual:** The newer colleague (C7) brings two printed instructions to the case manager's (C5) desk and lays them side by side.
**Newer colleague (C7):** Do you have a minute? I'm trying to understand how these two instructions fit together. Read side by side, they seem to point in different directions.
**Case manager (C5):** Everyone else understands them. I'd try not to overthink it.
**Newer colleague (C7):** Okay. Thanks anyway.
**Visual:** The newer colleague (C7) returns to their own desk. The case manager (C5) looks down at the two pages still lying on the desk.
**Case manager (C5):** Wait, before you go back to that…
**Narrator (C3):** The question is still unanswered, and the next one may now feel riskier to ask. The course stops here and asks you to compare three ways the case manager could respond.

## Closing (0:52–1:29)
**Visual:** The reflection question on screen: "When a question seems already settled to you, what would help you answer its substance first?" Then the course's title card.
**Narrator (C3):** In this course, you'll look at psychological safety as the ability to raise questions and discuss uncertainty while still taking responsibility for the work. You'll practice recognizing how role, identity and past experience can affect the risk of speaking up, responding to a question or mistake without shaming the person, and distinguishing shared learning from a concern that needs the proper formal route. A question to carry in with you: when a question seems already settled to you, what would help you answer its substance first?

## Transcript notes
A short introduction: a newcomer asks how two instructions fit together, hears "Everyone else understands," and the colleague who replied reconsiders.
