# Video script: Communicating Equity Findings with Care and Public Trust

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=A15
- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 213 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Program manager (C2, Host voice), Newer colleague (C7, Co-host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: One sentence in a report (0:00–0:25)
**Visual:** A draft report on a laptop with one sentence highlighted; the text is not readable. The course title appears on screen and is spoken. No real logos or real people.
**Narrator (C3):** One sentence in a report can quietly turn a count into a cause. Communicating Equity Findings with Care and Public Trust is an advanced self-directed course for the moment you explain a research, policy, service, or workforce finding to others, and need to say what the evidence shows and what it does not.

## Scene 2: Eight and eight (0:25–1:00)
**Visual:** The program manager (C2) and the newer colleague (C7) reviewing a draft summary. A small practice table shows two routes; the newer colleague reads its figures aloud.
**Newer colleague (C7):** For the summary, I wrote that the two routes are equal. Route A had eight recorded offers of clarification among ten requests. Route B had eight among twenty. Eight and eight.
**Program manager (C2):** These are practice figures, so nothing here describes a real service. But readers will take that sentence at face value.
**Newer colleague (C7):** Is "equal" the wrong word, then?
**Program manager (C2):** That's what we need to settle before this goes out. What can these numbers actually support, and what would we still need to learn?

## Closing (1:00–1:35)
**Visual:** The course title card, then the first slide of the course.
**Narrator (C3):** The course helps you state what is being compared and the response base behind a finding before you interpret it. You'll practice telling a supported explanation from an assumed cause, revising a narrative that places blame on people instead of examining conditions, and preparing accessible communication that preserves uncertainty and the meaning of individual accounts. As you begin, ask yourself: in the next finding you write, which words describe what was observed, and which ones explain why it happened?

## Transcript notes
Media manifest summary: A short introduction to Communicating Equity Findings with Care and Public Trust: a draft calls two routes equal because each shows eight recorded offers, and two colleagues pause to ask what the figures support.
