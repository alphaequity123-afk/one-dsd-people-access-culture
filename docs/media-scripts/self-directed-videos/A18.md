- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 220 spoken words)

- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: Beyond the single moment (0:00–0:25)
**Visual:** A quiet open office late in the day. The course title appears on screen and is spoken. No real logos or real people.
**Narrator (C3):** When someone describes a hard experience at work or in a service, the response can look at what shaped it, or it can add one more burden. Workplace Culture, Harm, and Conditions for Respectful Participation is an advanced self-directed course for that moment, when you connect a reported experience with the practice or condition behind it.

## Scene 2: A label in the file (0:25–1:00)
**Visual:** The newer colleague (C7) and the case manager (C5) at neighboring desks. The newer colleague is reading a contact note on screen; the text is blurred.
**Newer colleague (C7):** I'm picking up this contact next. The last note calls the person troublesome. From what I can tell, they asked several questions about the next-step instruction.
**Case manager (C5):** Hm. That word will travel with them into every contact after this one.
**Newer colleague (C7):** So should I keep the call short and stick closely to the script?
**Case manager (C5):** I wouldn't decide how to handle the call just yet.
**Newer colleague (C7):** Then where do I start?
**Case manager (C5):** That's the real question. Let's think it through before you pick up the phone.

## Closing (1:00–1:35)
**Visual:** The course title card, then the first slide of the course.
**Narrator (C3):** The course helps you connect a reported experience with a specific practice or organizational condition, compare support and repair options without requiring disclosure or forgiveness, distinguish voluntary learning from formal protections and required responses, and define a bounded question for examining culture across employee experience. Optional learning or repair never delays a required report, and the course sets out that duty. Carry this question in with you: when a concern is raised, what does your response ask of the person who raised it?
