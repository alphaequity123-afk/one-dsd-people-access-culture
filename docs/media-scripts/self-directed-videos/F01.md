# Video script: Intercultural Competence

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=F01
- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 202 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Program manager (C2, Host voice), Community partner (C1, Co-host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: Whose comment counts as enough (0:00–0:18)
**Visual:** A plain title card reading “Intercultural Competence,” which fades to an illustrated DSD meeting room with a printed draft on the table.
**Narrator (C3):** Cultural meaning shapes ordinary exchanges at work, including whose comment a team quietly treats as the final word. Intercultural Competence prepares you for the moment when a familiar way of listening begins to decide whose experience shapes DSD services and workplace routines.

## Scene 2: One familiar response (0:18–0:52)
**Visual:** The program manager (C2) and the community partner (C1) sit at a small table with a printed draft of a proposed service change. No text on the draft is readable.
**Community partner (C1):** I read through the proposed change. From what I can see, it looks workable, and I don’t have any major concerns.
**Program manager (C2):** That helps a lot. You’ve worked with us for years, so I’m comfortable treating that as the community’s view.
**Community partner (C1):** I’m glad it’s useful to you.
**Program manager (C2):** Then I think we can move it forward this month.
**Narrator (C3):** The scene pauses there, with one familiar partner’s response standing in for the community. What should the team consider before it moves on?

## Closing (0:52–1:27)
**Visual:** The closing question appears on screen as the narrator reads it: “In a routine you know well, whose response usually counts as enough?”
**Narrator (C3):** Across its practice situations, the course helps you separate what was observed from what was assumed, check another person’s meaning without predicting them from identity, and choose a useful adjustment to a service conversation or workplace interaction. It also asks you to notice when one familiar communication style has become the standard for everyone, and it does not assess anyone’s intercultural development. A question to carry into the course: in a routine you know well, whose response usually counts as enough?

## Transcript notes
A short introduction to Intercultural Competence: a team is ready to treat one familiar community partner’s comment as the community’s view, and the video stops at the team’s next decision.
