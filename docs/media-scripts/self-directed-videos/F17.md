# Video script: Equity Across the Employee Life Cycle

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=F17
- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 217 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Case manager (C5, Host voice), Supervisor (C6, Co-host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: Where an impression starts (0:00–0:20)
**Visual:** Title card on a plain background: "Equity Across the Employee Life Cycle." It fades to an illustrated hallway with a row of office doors, each a little further along than the last.
**Narrator (C3):** An impression of a colleague often forms early, and it can quietly follow them into later assignments, recognition and development. Equity Across the Employee Life Cycle prepares you for the moment when you are about to act on that kind of impression, and asks where it began.

## Scene 2: Planning the next review (0:20–0:58)
**Visual:** The case manager (C5) and the supervisor (C6) look over a list of upcoming review assignments at the supervisor's desk. No names are visible on the list.
**Case manager (C5):** For the next review, I'd leave our newest team member off. They haven't said a word in our last few meetings. I don't think they're interested.
**Supervisor (C6):** What are we basing that on, exactly?
**Case manager (C5):** Mostly the silence. Although, now that you ask, I'm not sure anyone told them we accept written comments after the meeting.
**Supervisor (C6):** So we have a first impression, and an assignment decision coming up. How do we look at this before we settle it?
**Narrator (C3):** The course pauses at that decision and offers three responses to examine.

## Closing (0:58–1:33)
**Visual:** The reflection question on screen: "When you call a colleague ready or not ready, which earlier chances are you counting?" Then the course's title card.
**Narrator (C3):** In this course, you'll look at recruitment, selection, onboarding, supervision, development, retention and transition as connected stages. You'll practice recognizing how an opportunity at one stage can shape options at a later one, distinguishing individual support from a recurring condition that affects many employees, and identifying a question within your own role and the person responsible for a broader decision. A question to carry in with you: when you call a colleague ready or not ready, which earlier chances are you counting?

## Transcript notes
A short introduction: a case manager wants to leave a quiet newcomer off a review, and a supervisor asks what that impression rests on.
