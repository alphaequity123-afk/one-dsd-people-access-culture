# Video script: Accessibility in Everyday Services and Work

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=F10
- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 197 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Program manager (C2, Host voice), Newer colleague (C7, Co-host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: Barriers in routine steps (0:00–0:23)
**Visual:** A plain title card reading “Accessibility in Everyday Services and Work,” which fades to an illustrated shared desk in a DSD office.
**Narrator (C3):** Many access barriers hide in routine steps, such as a handout, a form, or the materials a new employee receives in the first week. Accessibility in Everyday Services and Work prepares you to spot physical, digital, communication, and participation barriers in those ordinary activities, in services people use and in your own team’s work.

## Scene 2: The only recording (0:23–0:55)
**Visual:** The newer colleague (C7) and the program manager (C2) sit at a shared desk. A video player is open on the screen, with no readable text.
**Newer colleague (C7):** The onboarding steps are all in that one recording, and I can’t use it the way it’s made. Is there another version of the instructions?
**Program manager (C2):** Not that I know of. Most people pick it up by watching the rest of us for a few weeks.
**Newer colleague (C7):** So until then, I’m starting the work without the instructions.
**Narrator (C3):** The essential information exists, but only in a form this employee cannot use. Which response best addresses the barrier?

## Closing (0:55–1:24)
**Visual:** The closing question appears on screen as the narrator reads it: “Which materials in your work reach everyone in a single format, and has anyone checked that people can use them?”
**Narrator (C3):** The course helps you ask about access without demanding unnecessary personal information, and consider accessible choices before someone has to request an exception. It also helps you explain how a recurring inaccessible default shifts effort and delay onto the person seeking access. A question to carry into the course: which materials in your work reach everyone in a single format, and has anyone checked that people can use them?

## Transcript notes
A short introduction to Accessibility in Everyday Services and Work: a new employee cannot use the only recording of essential onboarding instructions and is told to learn by watching others. The video stops at the decision.
