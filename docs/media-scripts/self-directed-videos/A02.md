# Video script: Designing Services around Cultural Meaning and Personal Choice

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=A02
- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 219 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Case manager (C5, Host voice), Newer colleague (C7, Co-host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: Whose picture of independence (0:00–0:25)
**Visual:** A hallway outside a training room, with a printed onboarding guide pinned to a notice board. The course title appears on screen as the narrator (C3) speaks it.
**Narrator (C3):** Every service and workplace process carries a picture of what independence, help, and readiness look like, and that picture is not always shared. Designing Services around Cultural Meaning and Personal Choice prepares you for the moment when a person’s own meaning, or their wish for chosen support, does not fit the route that was designed.

## Scene 2: A question before the first error (0:25–1:00)
**Visual:** The newer colleague (C7) and the case manager (C5) sit at a shared desk with a page of onboarding guidance between them.
**Newer colleague (C7):** The onboarding guide says new staff should ask for help only after we make an error. Could I check my understanding with you before I start on my first requests instead?
**Case manager (C5):** That guidance was written so people would learn to work on their own, and I’ve never questioned it before now.
**Newer colleague (C7):** I’d just like a chance to confirm that I’ve understood the steps before something goes wrong.
**Case manager (C5):** Let me raise it with our supervisor, because I’m not sure yet how we should compare the two approaches.
**Visual:** The case manager (C5) picks up the onboarding page. The scene ends without a decision.

## Closing (1:00–1:35)
**Visual:** The narrator (C3) in the hallway. The closing question appears on screen as the narrator speaks it.
**Narrator (C3):** The course gives you practice mapping a complete service journey, from a person’s own purpose through to follow-through, and finding the point where an organization assumes a shared meaning. You will compare design options using access, choice, clarity, and feasibility, and explain what a walkthrough can establish and what still needs evidence from actual use. A question to carry with you: when does asking for help feel like responsibility to you, and when does it feel like dependence?

## Transcript notes
A short introduction: a newer colleague asks to check their understanding before making an error, against onboarding guidance that says to ask only afterward, and a case manager stops at how to compare the two routes.
