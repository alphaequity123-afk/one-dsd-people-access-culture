# Video script: An Equity Lens for Ordinary Decisions

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=F21
- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 200 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Program manager (C2, Host voice), Supervisor (C6, Co-host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: Quick does not mean neutral (0:00–0:18)
**Visual:** Title card on a plain background: "An Equity Lens for Ordinary Decisions." It fades to an illustrated inbox with a new message about a training opportunity. The message text is not legible.
**Narrator (C3):** When a decision is made quickly, it can feel neutral simply because nobody stopped to ask who it works for. An Equity Lens for Ordinary Decisions prepares you for that pause, the moment before a routine choice is treated as settled.

## Scene 2: A training spot for tomorrow (0:18–0:52)
**Visual:** The program manager (C2) and the supervisor (C6) talk in a hallway. The program manager holds a phone showing the training message.
**Program manager (C2):** A spot just opened in that development training. It's tomorrow, in person, so I'll offer it to whoever can travel on short notice.
**Supervisor (C6):** It would fill quickly that way. But several people on my team couldn't go tomorrow, and not because they lack interest.
**Program manager (C2):** I hadn't thought of it like that. It just seemed like the fastest way to fill it.
**Supervisor (C6):** Before you send it, what should we be weighing?
**Narrator (C3):** The course pauses here and gives you three responses to compare.

## Closing (0:52–1:26)
**Visual:** The reflection question on screen: "Before your next routine choice is settled, who would have to do more because of it?" Then the course's title card.
**Narrator (C3):** In this course, you'll learn to look at ordinary decisions through an equity lens built on six questions, about purpose, participation, requirements, access, evidence and consequences. You'll practice identifying whose experience is relevant before a decision is settled, distinguishing a question for examination from an unsupported finding, and connecting evidence with a considered next step. A question to carry in with you: before your next routine choice is settled, who would have to do more because of it?

## Transcript notes
A short introduction: a program manager plans to offer a training spot only to staff who can travel tomorrow, and a supervisor asks what to weigh first.
