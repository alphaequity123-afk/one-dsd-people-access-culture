# Video script: Personal Choice within the Rules and Resources of a Service System

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=A10
- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 196 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Supervisor (C6, Co-host voice), Case manager (C5, Host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: A recorded choice (0:00–0:20)
**Visual:** A desk with a service plan open on a monitor; one field shows a selected option. The course title appears on screen as the narrator (C3) speaks it.
**Narrator (C3):** A completed field can show that a choice was recorded without showing whether the person understood what they were choosing between. Personal Choice within the Rules and Resources of a Service System prepares you for the moments when rules, limited options, and offered help meet a person’s own direction.

## Scene 2: Who chooses the time (0:20–0:50)
**Visual:** The case manager (C5) and the supervisor (C6) review a contact note on screen.
**Case manager (C5):** This note is from yesterday’s contact. The person asked a trusted friend to help explain two appointment times and to write down the questions they had.
**Supervisor (C6):** That sounds like a reasonable kind of help to ask for.
**Case manager (C5):** The last line of the note says the friend will select the time, and the appointment is next week.
**Supervisor (C6):** I’d like us to get this right before the appointment. What should the correction say?
**Visual:** The case manager (C5) places the cursor at the end of the note. The scene ends before anything is typed.

## Closing (0:50–1:25)
**Visual:** The narrator (C3) at the desk. The closing question appears on screen as the narrator speaks it.
**Narrator (C3):** The course helps you distinguish recorded consent from a person’s meaningful understanding of their actual options, and identify a condition that restricts their ability to direct a decision. You will compare explanations and support that preserve the person’s own choice, and describe the evidence needed to know whether a proposed change is useful. To carry with you: before you offer someone support with a choice they are making, what would you want to ask them first?
