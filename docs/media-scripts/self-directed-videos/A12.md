# Video script: Learning from Complaints, Incidents, and Repeated Service Barriers

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=A12
- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 195 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Supervisor (C6, Co-host voice), Program manager (C2, Host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: Part of what happened (0:00–0:25)
**Visual:** A team workroom with printed charts of feedback counts on the wall. The course title appears on screen as the narrator (C3) speaks it.
**Narrator (C3):** When someone tells us something went wrong, the record that results holds only part of what happened. Learning from Complaints, Incidents, and Repeated Service Barriers prepares you for the moment you need to read a count, an incident note, or a concern that keeps returning, and decide what it can and cannot tell you.

## Scene 2: Three onboarding accounts (0:25–0:50)
**Visual:** The program manager (C2) and the supervisor (C6) sit at a table with three short printed feedback accounts and three review notes. No names are visible.
**Program manager (C2):** I’ve been reading our onboarding feedback. Three new employees described the same problem: the instructions for asking to have an assignment clarified weren’t clear.
**Supervisor (C6):** Those same three employees appear in later review notes, and each note says they came in unprepared.
**Program manager (C2):** So we have three accounts and three review notes, and they seem to point in different directions.
**Supervisor (C6):** Then where should our inquiry start?
**Visual:** The two sets of pages lie side by side on the table. The scene ends on the question.

## Closing (0:50–1:25)
**Visual:** The narrator (C3) in the workroom. The closing question appears on screen as the narrator speaks it.
**Narrator (C3):** The course helps you explain what a complaint or incident count measures and what it leaves unknown, trace a recurring barrier beyond the last person in the process, and compare records with the particular accounts of people affected. You will prepare improvement questions while required complaint and incident procedures stay in place. One question to carry with you: when a record says a person would not cooperate, what did someone actually observe, and what is still unanswered?

## Transcript notes
A short introduction: a program manager and a supervisor set three onboarding accounts of unclear instructions beside three review notes that call the same employees unprepared, and stop at where the inquiry should start.
