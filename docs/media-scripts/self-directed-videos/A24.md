# Video script: Evaluating System Change with a Logic Model

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=A24
- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 210 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Program manager (C2, Host voice), Community partner (C1, Co-host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: Five connected boxes (0:00–0:25)
**Visual:** A simple logic model with five boxes joined by arrows, labeled inputs, activities, outputs, outcomes, and impact. The narrator reads the labels. The course title follows and is spoken. No real logos or real people.
**Narrator (C3):** Inputs, activities, outputs, outcomes, and impact: a logic model lays out what a change is meant to accomplish and how the pieces are supposed to connect. Evaluating System Change with a Logic Model is an advanced self-directed course for the moment you are asked whether a program, policy, or workforce change is doing what it was meant to do.

## Scene 2: A clear guide and a silent route (0:25–0:55)
**Visual:** The program manager (C2) and the community partner (C1) at a table with a printed practice service guide; its text is not readable.
**Program manager (C2):** The new service guide is out. It clearly names the next contact, and our draft evaluation says access has improved.
**Community partner (C1):** People I work with have read it. They tell me the guide is clear, but when they use the contact route it names, nobody responds.
**Program manager (C2):** Every time?
**Community partner (C1):** That's what I'm hearing. I can't tell you how widespread it is.
**Program manager (C2):** Then I need to decide what the evaluation can actually conclude right now.

## Closing (0:55–1:30)
**Visual:** The course title card, then the first slide of the course.
**Narrator (C3):** The course helps you connect the inputs, activities, outputs, outcomes, and intended impact of a clearly defined system change, identify a condition that may help or interrupt those connections, and compare periods or groups without assuming a causal explanation. You'll also prepare an evaluation account that includes people's experience, its limits, and a justified next inquiry. As you begin, consider a change you are working on: which result would you count as an output, and which as an outcome?
