# Video script: Intersectional Barriers: Examining the Combined Experience

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=A06
- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 203 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Case manager (C5, Host voice), Community partner (C1, Co-host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: Every part checked, the task unfinished (0:00–0:20)
**Visual:** A community meeting room after a listening session, chairs in a loose circle. The course title appears on screen as the narrator (C3) speaks it.
**Narrator (C3):** A process can pass every separate check and still leave a person with no way to finish what they came to do. Intersectional Barriers: Examining the Combined Experience prepares you for the moment when two or more conditions combine into a barrier that a review of any single factor would miss.

## Scene 2: Two contacts, one concern (0:20–0:55)
**Visual:** The community partner (C1) and the case manager (C5) talk near the door as the room empties.
**Community partner (C1):** One person in tonight’s session described explaining their service concern to two different contacts. Each contact handled only one part of it, so they had to start over the second time.
**Case manager (C5):** The second contact was someone on my team. I can find out what they did.
**Community partner (C1):** I’d appreciate that. I’m also wondering how we should describe this when we write up the session.
**Case manager (C5):** So am I. What would be the most useful way to explain what happened?
**Visual:** The community partner (C1) opens a notebook to a blank page. The scene ends on the open question.

## Closing (0:55–1:25)
**Visual:** The narrator (C3) in the meeting room. The closing question appears on screen as the narrator speaks it.
**Narrator (C3):** The course gives you practice mapping how two or more process conditions interact, and explaining why separate category comparisons may miss the combined experience. You will compare remedies aimed at the interaction itself rather than at a list of identities, and write a bounded conclusion that keeps alternative explanations in view. Before you start, consider one question: when two requirements have worked poorly together in your own work, what did examining each one separately miss?

## Transcript notes
A short introduction: after a listening session, a community partner and a case manager consider a person who had to restart a concern with a second contact, and stop at how to explain what happened.
