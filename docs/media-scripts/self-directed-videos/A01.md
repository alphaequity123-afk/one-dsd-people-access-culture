# Video script: An Intercultural Lens for Policy and Organizational Rules

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=A01
- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 222 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Supervisor (C6, Co-host voice), Program manager (C2, Host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: A sign that became a rule (0:00–0:25)
**Visual:** A quiet open-plan office in the morning. The narrator (C3) stands beside a whiteboard listing a team’s weekly meetings. The course title appears on screen as the narrator speaks it.
**Narrator (C3):** Every team has a familiar sign that someone is ready for more, and over time that sign can harden into a rule that nobody questions. An Intercultural Lens for Policy and Organizational Rules prepares you for the moment you review a requirement, or decide who gets an opportunity, and need to ask what the rule assumes about how people communicate.

## Scene 2: Who gets the assignment (0:25–1:05)
**Visual:** A small meeting room. The supervisor (C6) and the program manager (C2) sit at a table with a printed list of upcoming development assignments and a short stack of written proposals.
**Supervisor (C6):** When a development assignment opens up, I give it to someone who answers right away in our meetings. Quick answers have always told me who is ready.
**Program manager (C2):** Our newest team member rarely speaks up in the moment, but the written proposals they send afterward are careful and thorough. The way we do things now, they never make the list.
**Supervisor (C6):** I hadn’t thought of it as a rule, but I suppose that’s what it is. Before we change it or defend it, what should we be examining first?
**Visual:** The program manager (C2) looks down at the proposals. The scene holds on the question; no answer is given.

## Closing (1:05–1:35)
**Visual:** The narrator (C3) back in the open office. The closing question appears on screen as the narrator speaks it.
**Narrator (C3):** In this course you will practice spotting an assumption about readiness, communication, or family roles inside a rule, and comparing alternatives against the same purpose and evidence. You will explain how a requirement that looks neutral can place heavier burdens on some people, and prepare a recommendation that keeps evidence, interpretation, and open questions apart. As you begin, consider one question: which familiar sign of readiness or respect have you treated as the natural standard?

## Transcript notes
A short introduction: a supervisor and a program manager notice that a habit of rewarding quick meeting answers has become the rule for development assignments, and stop at the question of what to examine first.
