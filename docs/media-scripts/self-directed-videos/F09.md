# Video script: Disability, Rights, Identity, and Belonging

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=F09
- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 212 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Program manager (C2, Host voice), Community partner (C1, Co-host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: Who is described as the problem (0:00–0:18)
**Visual:** A plain title card reading “Disability, Rights, Identity, and Belonging,” which fades to an illustrated meeting table with a printed page draft.
**Narrator (C3):** The way a page describes people can shape what the next reader expects of them. Disability, Rights, Identity, and Belonging prepares you to understand disability through more than limitations, and to notice what a description may lead the next reader to do.

## Scene 2: A program page under review (0:18–0:50)
**Visual:** The program manager (C2) and the community partner (C1) review a printed draft of a public program page. No text on the page is readable.
**Community partner (C1):** I read the program page draft. It describes people mostly as unable to manage ordinary life, and it says staff decide what is best.
**Program manager (C2):** People do come to this program needing real support. Whatever we change, I want the page to keep saying that honestly.
**Community partner (C1):** I understand. It’s the rest of the description I keep getting stuck on.
**Narrator (C3):** The page is due for revision. Which change would improve the account without denying support needs?

## Closing (0:50–1:31)
**Visual:** The closing question appears on screen as the narrator reads it: “When you describe someone’s support needs in writing, what does the next reader learn about their strengths and choices?”
**Narrator (C3):** The course compares medical, social, cultural, and rights-based ways of understanding disability, and how each shapes questions of identity, rights, and belonging. It helps you recognize when a deficit description obscures strengths and surrounding barriers, distinguish an individual support need from a condition the organization can change, and respect disability identity without expecting anyone to represent all disabled people. Actual rights, service, or accommodation questions use the responsible agency routes. A question to carry into the course: when you describe someone’s support needs in writing, what does the next reader learn about their strengths and choices?

## Transcript notes
A short introduction to Disability, Rights, Identity, and Belonging: a community partner reviews a program page that describes people mainly as unable to manage life. The video stops at the revision decision.
