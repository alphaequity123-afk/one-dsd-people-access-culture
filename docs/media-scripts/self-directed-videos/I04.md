# Video script: Identity, Language, and Belonging in Everyday Work

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=I04
- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 199 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Program manager (C2, Host voice), Newer colleague (C7, Co-host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: Opening (0:00–0:24)
**Visual:** A title card reads "Identity, Language, and Belonging in Everyday Work." The narrator (C3) stands beside it in an open office area.
**Narrator (C3):** Feedback about how someone sounds can be hard to act on, and it can blur the line between the work and the person doing it. Identity, Language, and Belonging in Everyday Work prepares you for moments when names, accents, and ways of presenting come up at work, and the actual task needs to stay in view.

## Scene 2: A practice run before a presentation (0:24–0:56)
**Visual:** An empty meeting room. The newer colleague (C7) stands beside a screen showing the last slide of a policy presentation, with plain placeholder text. The program manager (C2) sits at the table with notes.
**Newer colleague (C7):** And that's the end. That's everything on the policy change.
**Program manager (C2):** The content is accurate. I checked it against the policy. Before you present to the full team, though, you need to sound more polished.
**Newer colleague (C7):** More polished how? What should I change?
**Visual:** The program manager (C2) pauses and looks at their notes.
**Narrator (C3):** One detail matters here: the presentation never said what happens next. The course stops at this point and asks which response would best improve the work.

## Closing (0:56–1:28)
**Visual:** The narrator (C3) on camera in the open office area.
**Narrator (C3):** The course offers practice in respecting names and identities without requiring disclosure or assigning anyone a cultural role, and in separating communication evidence from judgments based on accent or familiarity. It also asks you to review a participation expectation for its purpose and for any unequal burden it may carry. A question to take with you: which expectations about how people speak or present at work are needed for the task, and which are simply familiar?

## Transcript notes
A newer colleague is told to sound more polished after an accurate practice presentation. An introduction to keeping the task in view when names, accents, and self-presentation come up at work.
