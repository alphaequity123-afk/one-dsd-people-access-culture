# Video script: Designing and Evaluating Changes that Reduce Bias

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=A08
- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 216 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Supervisor (C6, Co-host voice), Case manager (C5, Host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: A vague word in a decision (0:00–0:25)
**Visual:** A hiring panel’s workspace with a printed scoring sheet. The course title appears on screen as the narrator (C3) speaks it.
**Narrator (C3):** When a decision rests on a vague word such as cooperative or fit, familiar expectations can quietly stand in for the actual question. Designing and Evaluating Changes that Reduce Bias prepares you for the moment you need to find where that could happen, and judge whether a proposed change would actually address it.

## Scene 2: Scoring overall fit (0:25–1:00)
**Visual:** The supervisor (C6) and the case manager (C5) review the scoring sheet. One column is labeled “Overall fit,” and the label is spoken in the scene.
**Supervisor (C6):** All the panel scores for overall fit are in. If we average them, we’ll have a clear ranking by this afternoon.
**Case manager (C5):** When I scored fit, I was looking for evidence of analysis in each candidate’s answers. What were you looking for?
**Supervisor (C6):** Mostly confidence, and whether the candidate used the terms we use every day. Our third panelist told me something different again.
**Case manager (C5):** Then I’m not sure these numbers mean the same thing. What should we do with them before we rank anyone?
**Visual:** The supervisor (C6) looks back at the column of scores. The scene ends without a ranking.

## Closing (1:00–1:35)
**Visual:** The narrator (C3) beside the panel table. The closing question appears on screen as the narrator speaks it.
**Narrator (C3):** The course helps you describe the specific decision condition through which bias might operate, and compare a change to the process with a response that relies on learning alone. You will choose measures of behavior, benefit, burden, and implementation, and interpret a comparison without claiming it proves a lasting causal effect. One question to carry into the course: when you rely on a criterion such as readiness or professionalism, what does it actually mean for the task in front of you?

## Transcript notes
A short introduction: two hiring panel members find they scored “overall fit” with different meanings in mind, and stop at what to do with the numbers before ranking anyone.
