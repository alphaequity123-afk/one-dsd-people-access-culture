# Video script: CLAS and Language Access as Organizational Practice

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=A14
- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 227 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Supervisor (C6, Co-host voice), Case manager (C5, Host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: More than one document (0:00–0:25)
**Visual:** A translated service notice on a screen beside a desk phone. No readable text other than the course title, which is spoken. No real logos or real people.
**Narrator (C3):** A translated notice can feel like the end of a communication task, when it is often the start of an exchange. CLAS and Language Access as Organizational Practice is an advanced self-directed course for the moment you follow a language access need through a whole service process, including the staff who are asked to provide support.

## Scene 2: Another request to interpret (0:25–1:10)
**Visual:** The supervisor (C6) stands in the doorway of the case manager's (C5) office. The case manager has a review file open.
**Supervisor (C6):** Do you have time this afternoon? There's a complex discussion on the schedule, and the person asked for the language you speak.
**Case manager (C5):** I can, but honestly, these requests keep coming, and my review work keeps slipping. I grew up speaking the language, but no one has established that I'm qualified to interpret something this complex.
**Supervisor (C6):** I hadn't realized how much it had added up. You've become the person we turn to.
**Case manager (C5):** I'm glad to help people. I just don't think it should land on me by default.
**Supervisor (C6):** That's fair. Before I ask you again, I need to work out what I should actually be looking at here.

## Closing (1:10–1:35)
**Visual:** The course title card, then the first slide of the course.
**Narrator (C3):** The course helps you trace a language access need across a complete service process, compare the availability of assistance with evidence of actual understanding, and prepare a communication improvement that respects the person's preferred method and actual requirements. Keep one question with you as you start: when a record says assistance was provided, what would you want to know before calling the communication complete?

## Transcript notes
Media manifest summary: A short introduction to CLAS and Language Access as Organizational Practice: a supervisor and a bilingual case manager stop to ask what a repeated request to interpret says about role, workload and support.
