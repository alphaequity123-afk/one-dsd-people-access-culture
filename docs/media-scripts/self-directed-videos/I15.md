# Video script: Community Engagement With an Honest Account of Influence

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=I15
- **Length:** about 1.6 minutes (about 219 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Supervisor (C6, Co-host voice), Program manager (C2, Host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning. The only on-screen text is the course title, which the narrator speaks; documents and screens shown in the video carry no legible text.

## Scene 1: An invitation and what it promises (0:00–0:20)
**Visual:** The narrator (C3) at a desk with a blank sign-up sheet. A title card shows the course title.
**Narrator (C3):** Being asked for your input feels different once you learn the decision was already made. Community Engagement With an Honest Account of Influence prepares you for the moment before an invitation goes out, when you decide how to describe what people can actually shape.

## Scene 2: A session for a finished guide (0:20–1:05)
**Visual:** The supervisor (C6) and the program manager (C2) at a small table reviewing a draft invitation on a laptop; the screen text is not legible.
**Supervisor (C6):** The new onboarding guide was approved last week, so its content is set. I still want the team to feel some ownership of it.
**Program manager (C2):** The draft invitation says, help us design the new guide. People have already started signing up for the session.
**Supervisor (C6):** Good. That kind of turnout tells me they care. Can we leave the wording as it is?
**Program manager (C2):** They could still tell us where the guide is hard to use once they're working with it. They just can't choose what goes into this first version.
**Supervisor (C6):** So what do we call the session, and what do we tell people it's for?
**Visual:** The two look at the draft on the screen. No decision is shown.

## Closing (1:05–1:35)
**Visual:** The narrator (C3) in the same office setting.
**Narrator (C3):** The course helps you be clear, before inviting anyone, about what they can actually shape. It compares ways to include people whose experience is missing, looks at participation costs, access needs and the value of community expertise, and shows how to explain what input changed and what could not change. As you begin, consider this: if someone asked you after a meeting what their contribution had changed, what could you honestly tell them?
