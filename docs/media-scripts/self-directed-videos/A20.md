## Scene 1: Three rows in a work plan (0:00–0:20)
**Visual:** A work-plan template on screen with three empty goal rows. The course title appears and is spoken. No real logos or real people.
**Narrator (C3):** A work plan can name three equity goals and still leave open what will actually happen. Putting Three ADSA Equity Goals into Practice is an advanced self-directed course, mainly for supervisors and managers developing their three-goal work plans, for the moment you turn selected goals into action and describe the progress truthfully.

## Scene 2: The progress update (0:20–0:55)
**Visual:** The supervisor (C6) and the program manager (C2) reviewing a draft progress update on a shared screen; the text is not readable.
**Supervisor (C6):** Here's my draft progress update. I wrote that the community agreed with our plan and that the disparity has been eliminated.
**Program manager (C2):** Walk me through where that comes from.
**Supervisor (C6):** A participant suggested a clearer question route for people with follow-up questions. It was a good suggestion, and we built it into the proposal.
**Program manager (C2):** Has anyone used the new route yet?
**Supervisor (C6):** Not yet. It's still proposed.
**Program manager (C2):** Then before this goes anywhere, we need to agree on what the update can honestly say.

## Closing (0:55–1:30)
**Visual:** The course title card, then the first slide of the course.
**Narrator (C3):** The course helps you explain the six ADSA goals in the program's documented wording, select three relevant goals and connect each with a real organizational condition, and examine how the three reinforce one another or create competing demands, using relevant evidence to guide follow-through. It also helps you identify where consultation and guidance would clarify the plan. As you begin, consider: in a progress account you might write, how would you keep what was done separate from what it achieved?
