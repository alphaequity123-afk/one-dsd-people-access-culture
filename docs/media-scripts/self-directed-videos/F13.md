# Video script: Community Engagement: Listening with Purpose and Respect

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=F13
- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 217 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Program manager (C2, Host voice), Newer colleague (C7, Co-host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: Before the invitation goes out (0:00–0:20)
**Visual:** Title card on a plain background: "Community Engagement: Listening with Purpose and Respect." It fades to an illustrated office where a draft service notice sits open on a laptop beside a printed copy.
**Narrator (C3):** When we ask people for their views, they give us their time, usually hoping it will count for something. Community Engagement: Listening with Purpose and Respect prepares you for the moment before an invitation goes out, when you decide what you are really asking people to influence.

## Scene 2: A notice that is nearly final (0:20–1:03)
**Visual:** The newer colleague (C7) and the program manager (C2) sit together at a shared desk. On the laptop, the draft invitation's one line reads "Tell us what you think." The newer colleague reads that line aloud in the dialogue.
**Newer colleague (C7):** The service notice is almost final, and I'm drafting the invitation for comments. Right now it says, “Tell us what you think.” Is that enough?
**Program manager (C2):** Let's look at what's actually open first. The wording of the notice can still change. The underlying rule isn't part of this review.
**Newer colleague (C7):** So if people write in about the rule itself, what happens to those comments? I don't want to promise something we can't do, but I don't want to shut anyone out either.
**Program manager (C2):** That's the real question. What should the invitation say?
**Narrator (C3):** The course pauses here and gives you three possible invitations to weigh.

## Closing (1:03–1:33)
**Visual:** The reflection question on screen: "When people gave you their input, how did they find out what happened to it?" Then the course's title card.
**Narrator (C3):** In this course, you'll learn to tell informing people, seeking their input and sharing influence apart. You'll practice explaining what is open before you ask anyone to take part, identifying barriers to being present and contributing, and returning an honest account of what happened to people's input. A question to carry in with you: when people gave you their input, how did they find out what happened to it?

## Transcript notes
A short introduction: a program manager and a newer colleague draft an invitation for comments on a nearly final notice and ask what people can actually influence.
