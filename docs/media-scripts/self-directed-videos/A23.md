# Video script: Responsible Data and Automated Decisions: Evidence before Conclusions

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=A23
- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 205 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Supervisor (C6, Co-host voice), Case manager (C5, Host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: A tool that looks precise (0:00–0:20)
**Visual:** A practice dashboard with a ranked list and a large accuracy figure; neither is readable. The course title appears and is spoken. No real logos or real people.
**Narrator (C3):** A decision tool can look precise while carrying forward conditions it never measured. Responsible Data and Automated Decisions: Evidence before Conclusions is an advanced self-directed course for the moment you are asked to rely on a record, a report, or an automated rule, and need to know first what it actually measures.

## Scene 2: Before the tool goes into use (0:20–0:50)
**Visual:** The supervisor (C6) and the case manager (C5) at the supervisor's desk, looking at the dashboard.
**Supervisor (C6):** Have you seen the practice contact-priority tool? It ranks people higher if they've had earlier successful contacts.
**Case manager (C5):** I've heard about it. How did it do in testing?
**Supervisor (C6):** High overall accuracy. The test left out unsuccessful attempts, though.
**Case manager (C5):** Some of the people I work with took several tries before anyone reached them.
**Supervisor (C6):** We're asked for a recommendation before anyone decides to use it. What should we say?
**Case manager (C5):** Give me a minute with that before I answer.

## Closing (0:50–1:25)
**Visual:** The course title card, then the first slide of the course.
**Narrator (C3):** The course helps you define a clear decision question and identify what the available data actually measures before relying on it. You'll work through missingness, response base, aggregation, and privacy limits, examine how an automated rule may repeat an existing process barrier, and practice communicating a supported finding with its uncertainty and the actual responsibility for its use. Bring one question into the course: before you rely on a score, who could tell you what it leaves out?

## Transcript notes
Media manifest summary: A short introduction to Responsible Data and Automated Decisions: a practice tool favors people with earlier successful contacts, its test leaves out unsuccessful attempts, and two colleagues must recommend.
