# Video script: Belonging and Everyday Workplace Culture

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=F18
- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 214 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Newer colleague (C7, Co-host voice), Case manager (C5, Host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: Welcome and contribution (0:00–0:21)
**Visual:** Title card on a plain background: "Belonging and Everyday Workplace Culture." It fades to an illustrated break room where colleagues share lunch, then to a meeting room with a draft on the screen.
**Narrator (C3):** Being warmly welcomed onto a team and having your ideas taken seriously are not always the same experience. Belonging and Everyday Workplace Culture prepares you for the everyday moments when a colleague offers something to the work, and the response tells them what kind of place they have in it.

## Scene 2: After the review meeting (0:21–1:02)
**Visual:** The newer colleague (C7) and the case manager (C5) walk back to their desks after a meeting, carrying notebooks.
**Newer colleague (C7):** Everyone's been really welcoming. I've been to the team lunches, and people check in on me. But in the review, my suggestion about organizing the draft differently was set aside again, because it isn't the usual approach.
**Case manager (C5):** I was there. Nobody really asked what it was meant to do.
**Newer colleague (C7):** I'm not asking for it to be adopted. I'd just like to know it was considered on its merits.
**Case manager (C5):** I'm leading next week's review, so the next response is partly mine.
**Narrator (C3):** The course pauses here and asks you to weigh what that next response could be.

## Closing (1:02–1:32)
**Visual:** The reflection question on screen: "What does your usual response to an unfamiliar idea teach the person who offered it?" Then the course's title card.
**Narrator (C3):** In this course, you'll explore belonging as meaningful participation and connection, not conformity. You'll practice recognizing how everyday responses communicate unwritten expectations, considering a contribution that helps a colleague participate without taking over, and connecting workplace habits with the division's capacity to understand and serve different people. A question to carry in with you: what does your usual response to an unfamiliar idea teach the person who offered it?

## Transcript notes
A short introduction: a newcomer who feels socially welcomed describes a proposal set aside as unfamiliar, and a case manager considers the team's next response.
