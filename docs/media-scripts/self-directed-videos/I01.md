# Video script: Adapting Communication Without Losing the Meaning

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=I01
- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 200 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Case manager (C5, Host voice), Community partner (C1, Co-host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: Opening (0:00–0:23)
**Visual:** A title card reads "Adapting Communication Without Losing the Meaning." The narrator (C3) stands beside it in a plain office setting with no real logos.
**Narrator (C3):** A letter can be accurate and still leave someone unsure what to do next. Adapting Communication Without Losing the Meaning prepares you for that gap, when a service explanation or a work expectation has to reach someone in a different way, and the facts, choices, and responsibilities in it have to stay the same.

## Scene 2: A letter people keep asking about (0:23–0:58)
**Visual:** The community partner (C1) and the case manager (C5) sit at a small table with a printed service letter between them. The letter has no real letterhead.
**Community partner (C1):** People who got this letter keep calling me. They send in the information it asks for, and then they don't know what happens next or who is going to respond.
**Case manager (C5):** The address on it is correct, though. I checked it myself.
**Community partner (C1):** I'm not saying the address is wrong. I'm telling you what people say after they mail it.
**Visual:** The case manager (C5) looks down at the letter. The scene holds without an answer.
**Narrator (C3):** Both statements are true. The course pauses here and asks which response would best address the concern.

## Closing (0:58–1:29)
**Visual:** The narrator (C3) on camera. Behind them, the letter from the scene, out of focus.
**Narrator (C3):** In the course, you practice asking a clarifying question without assigning a communication style to a community, and comparing two ways to explain the same service or work expectation while keeping its meaning. You also look for participation rules that reward familiar speech rather than relevant knowledge. One question to carry in with you: when someone tells you a message is unclear, how would you find out which part they mean?

## Transcript notes
A community partner reports that people do not know what follows after they answer a service letter. An introduction to adapting communication while keeping facts, choices, and responsibilities intact.
