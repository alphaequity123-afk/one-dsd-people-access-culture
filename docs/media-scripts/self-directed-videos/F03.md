## Scene 1: Where normal comes from (0:00–0:22)
**Visual:** A plain title card reading “Cultural Identity, Assumptions, and the Meaning of Normal,” which fades to an illustrated interview room after a panel has finished.
**Narrator (C3):** Every team carries a sense of what normal looks like, and that sense is learned. Cultural Identity, Assumptions, and the Meaning of Normal prepares you for the moments when a familiar expectation, on a service form or in a hiring conversation, starts to work like a standard that everyone must meet.

## Scene 2: A good fit (0:22–0:53)
**Visual:** The supervisor (C6) and the case manager (C5) sit at a table with interview score sheets. No text on the sheets is readable.
**Supervisor (C6):** I liked the last candidate. They’re into the same things we are, and the conversation just flowed. That feels like a good fit for this team.
**Case manager (C5):** It was an easy conversation. I still have a few of the work questions open on my sheet, though.
**Supervisor (C6):** Sure, but you can usually tell when someone fits.
**Narrator (C3):** Shared interests are on the table, and the work evidence is still incomplete. What would improve this comparison?

## Closing (0:53–1:23)
**Visual:** The closing question appears on screen as the narrator reads it: “Which expectation on your team feels so normal that no one has ever written it down?”
**Narrator (C3):** The course helps you notice how your own experience shapes expectations about time, family, communication, and work. It helps you distinguish a carefully qualified group pattern from an unsupported conclusion about one person, and explain how an expectation becomes consequential once it is built into a public process. A question to carry into the course: which expectation on your team feels so normal that no one has ever written it down?
