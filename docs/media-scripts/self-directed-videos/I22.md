# Video script: Reading Disparities With Numbers and Lived Experience

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=I22
- **Length:** about 1.6 minutes (about 217 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Program manager (C2, Host voice), Newer colleague (C7, Co-host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning. The only on-screen text is the course title, which the narrator speaks; documents and screens shown in the video carry no legible text.

## Scene 1: A gap that looks settled (0:00–0:20)
**Visual:** The narrator (C3) beside a simple two-bar chart with no numbers, labels or agency data. A title card shows the course title.
**Narrator (C3):** A chart can make a gap look settled before anyone has asked what was counted. Reading Disparities With Numbers and Lived Experience prepares you for the moment a finding reaches your desk, with figures on one side and people's own accounts on the other.

## Scene 2: Two accounts of one acknowledgment (0:20–0:55)
**Visual:** The newer colleague (C7) shows the program manager (C2) the same unlabeled two-bar chart on a laptop.
**Newer colleague (C7):** In this practice comparison, Setting A has the higher share of requests with a recorded acknowledgment. I also have two accounts from participants.
**Program manager (C2):** Go ahead.
**Newer colleague (C7):** One said the acknowledgment left them unsure what to do next. The other said it was useful and told them what to expect.
**Program manager (C2):** The second one lines up with the chart. That makes for a cleaner summary.
**Newer colleague (C7):** And the first one? I'm not sure where it belongs in the write-up.
**Program manager (C2):** Then let's decide how we read these two accounts before the summary goes anywhere.
**Visual:** The chart stays on the screen between them. No summary is shown.

## Closing (0:55–1:35)
**Visual:** The narrator (C3) in the same office setting.
**Narrator (C3):** The course helps you check what was counted, which groups are being compared and what the relevant denominator is, then read those numbers alongside lived experience. It keeps missing information and uncertain explanations visible, connects particular accounts with quantitative evidence without turning stories into rates, and frames a supported question about a possible process barrier rather than a conclusion about a community. As you start, consider what you would want to know about who was counted before explaining the next gap you see.
