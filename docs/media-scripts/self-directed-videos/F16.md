# Video script: Plain Language as an Equity Practice

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=F16
- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 219 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Community partner (C1, Co-host voice), Program manager (C2, Host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: Accurate, but what next? (0:00–0:22)
**Visual:** Title card on a plain background: "Plain Language as an Equity Practice." It fades to an illustrated printed notice with its last line highlighted. The highlighted line is read aloud in the next scene.
**Narrator (C3):** A notice can be accurate in every detail and still leave a reader unsure what to do next. Plain Language as an Equity Practice prepares you for the moment you write or review a message someone has to act on, and asks who carries the work when the next step is unclear.

## Scene 2: The last line of a notice (0:22–1:00)
**Visual:** The community partner (C1) and the program manager (C2) talk on a video call. The community partner holds the printed notice up to the camera, pointing to the highlighted last line.
**Community partner (C1):** The notice explains the service change clearly. But at the end it just says, “Contact your usual representative.”
**Program manager (C2):** That's our standard line. Most people who get this notice know who to call.
**Community partner (C1):** Most, maybe. Some of the people I work with are getting a notice like this for the first time. They don't have a usual representative. Who are they supposed to contact?
**Program manager (C2):** That's a fair point. So what would actually close that gap?
**Narrator (C3):** The course pauses on that question and asks you to weigh three possible revisions.

## Closing (1:00–1:34)
**Visual:** The reflection question on screen: "What phrase do you use at work that only makes sense if someone already knows the process?" Then the course's title card.
**Narrator (C3):** In this course, you'll see how wording, structure, format and timing all affect whether a person can act on information. You'll practice telling plain language apart from oversimplifying a consequential choice, spotting an unclear instruction, a hidden assumption or a missing next step, and revising a short message so it supports the reader's actual task. A question to carry in with you: what phrase do you use at work that only makes sense if someone already knows the process?

## Transcript notes
A short introduction: a community partner points out that a notice ends with "Contact your usual representative," which a first-time reader may not be able to use.
