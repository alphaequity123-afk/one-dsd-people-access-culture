## Scene 1: Small choices that add up (0:00–0:23)
**Visual:** Title card on a plain background: "Inclusive Supervision: A Supportive Starting Point." It fades to an illustrated team workspace with a whiteboard of upcoming tasks.
**Narrator (C3):** Many supervisory choices feel small at the time: who gets an assignment, how feedback is worded, how a meeting invites input. Over months, those choices shape who gets the chance to become ready for more. Inclusive Supervision: A Supportive Starting Point prepares supervisors and managers, and staff exploring supervisory work, for those everyday decisions.

## Scene 2: Background work for a briefing (0:23–0:58)
**Visual:** The case manager (C5) hands a folder of background analysis to the supervisor (C6) at the supervisor's desk.
**Case manager (C5):** Here's the background analysis for the briefing. The main sections are finished, and I flagged the access questions we still need to check.
**Supervisor (C6):** Thanks, this is thorough. I'll pass it to our usual presenter. They volunteered first again.
**Case manager (C5):** Sure, that's fine.
**Visual:** The case manager (C5) returns to their desk. The supervisor (C6) glances at a shelf of earlier briefing folders, each marked with the same presenter's initials. The initials are not legible.
**Narrator (C3):** The first volunteer has given every recent briefing, while the case manager has done substantial work behind them. The supervisor's choice for the next one is coming up. The course stops here and asks you to weigh what should guide it.

## Closing (0:58–1:33)
**Visual:** The reflection question on screen: "In your own routines, which assignments go to whoever is fastest?" Then the course's title card.
**Narrator (C3):** In this course, you'll look at how clear expectations, listening and access support make everyday supervision stronger. You'll practice distinguishing relevant performance evidence from familiarity, visibility and communication preferences, and considering how an ordinary assignment or development conversation can expand or limit opportunity. You'll also recognize when a request or concern, such as an accommodation question, belongs with the responsible formal process. A question to carry in with you: in your own routines, which assignments go to whoever is fastest?
