# Video script: Racial Advantage and the Organizational Standards We Take for Granted

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=A05
- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 220 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Supervisor (C6, Co-host voice), Program manager (C2, Host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: A standard that feels normal (0:00–0:25)
**Visual:** An interview room after a panel session, with three chairs on one side of the table. The course title appears on screen as the narrator (C3) speaks it.
**Narrator (C3):** Some workplace standards feel so normal that no one remembers where they came from or who first had the chance to learn them. Racial Advantage and the Organizational Standards We Take for Granted prepares you for the moment a familiar expectation, such as how a professional is supposed to sound, decides who is trusted or chosen.

## Scene 2: Professional presence (0:25–1:00)
**Visual:** The supervisor (C6) and the program manager (C2) remain at the panel table with interview notes spread in front of them.
**Supervisor (C6):** The first candidate briefed us without any notes and handled every follow-up question. That’s the professional presence I want in this role.
**Program manager (C2):** This role is mostly careful analysis and follow-through. The second candidate gave a prepared explanation that was sound and answered the same questions.
**Supervisor (C6):** It came across as less confident, though, and I think the whole panel noticed.
**Program manager (C2):** Maybe so. I’m just not sure the two of us are judging the same thing. What should we be comparing here?
**Visual:** The two look at their separate notes. The scene ends without a ranking.

## Closing (1:00–1:35)
**Visual:** The narrator (C3) in the interview room. The closing question appears on screen as the narrator speaks it.
**Narrator (C3):** The course helps you distinguish three things that are often blurred together: a person’s racial identity, an institutional norm, and racial advantage that has been documented. You will trace how one familiar expectation became an organizational standard, compare who receives the benefit of the doubt under it, and propose an alternative tied to the work’s purpose without creating another unexamined gate. One question to carry in: which expectation felt obvious to you because someone taught it to you through a network or an example?

## Transcript notes
A short introduction: after an interview, two panel members disagree about whether an unscripted briefing style or a sound prepared explanation shows what the role needs, and stop at what they should be comparing.
