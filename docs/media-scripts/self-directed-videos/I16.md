# Video script: Clear Public Information That People Can Use

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=I16
- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 216 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Program manager (C2, Host voice), Community partner (C1, Co-host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning. The only on-screen text is the course title, which the narrator speaks; documents and screens shown in the video carry no legible text.

## Scene 1: Accurate words, unclear action (0:00–0:20)
**Visual:** The narrator (C3) holding a printed notice with no legible text. A title card shows the course title.
**Narrator (C3):** Every word in a notice can be accurate and the reader can still be unsure what to do next. Clear Public Information That People Can Use prepares you to write and review explanations that people outside the office have to understand and act on.

## Scene 2: A promise in a friendly draft (0:20–1:00)
**Visual:** The program manager (C2) and the community partner (C1) review a printed practice draft side by side; its text is not legible on screen.
**Community partner (C1):** This reads warmly, and I like that it invites people in. One sentence stopped me, though. It says every response will change the decision.
**Program manager (C2):** We wrote that to encourage people to reply. In the actual process, staff consider every comment, but nothing guarantees a change.
**Community partner (C1):** People I work with will read that line as a promise, and some of them will remember it.
**Program manager (C2):** If we take it out, I worry the message stops sounding welcoming at all. The draft goes out this week, and I have to decide what happens to that sentence.
**Visual:** The program manager (C2) looks down at the draft, pen in hand. No revision is shown.

## Closing (1:00–1:35)
**Visual:** The narrator (C3) in the same office setting.
**Narrator (C3):** The course shows how to organize an explanation around the reader's question and the next step they need to take. It helps you keep eligibility conditions, duties and uncertainties while making them clearer, compare the meaning carried by text, images and accessible formats, and use feedback about understanding to revise the information rather than judge the reader. As you start, think of one piece of your own writing that a newcomer to the service might need to read twice.

## Transcript notes
Summary for the media manifest: A short introduction: a community partner questions a warm draft that promises every response will change a decision, and the narrator describes what the course offers for writing clear public information that people can use.
