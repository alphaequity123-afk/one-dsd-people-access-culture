# Video script: Understanding Evidence and Asking Better Equity Questions

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=F23
- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 230 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Community partner (C1, Co-host voice), Program manager (C2, Host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: A report on the desk (0:00–0:19)
**Visual:** Title card on a plain background: "Understanding Evidence and Asking Better Equity Questions." It fades to an illustrated report with a column of entries. No figures are legible.
**Narrator (C3):** A report can be accurate and still answer a different question from the one you are asking. Understanding Evidence and Asking Better Equity Questions prepares you for the moment when a number or a personal account reaches your desk and a decision is waiting.

## Scene 2: An account and a blank entry (0:19–1:00)
**Visual:** The community partner (C1) and the program manager (C2) meet at a small table. The program manager has the report open on a laptop; several entries in one column read "No response," which is spoken in the dialogue.
**Community partner (C1):** One of the participants told me the online confirmation page didn't work with the technology they use. In your report, they just show up as “no response.”
**Program manager (C2):** There are quite a few of those. I'd been reading them as people who decided not to continue.
**Community partner (C1):** This person wanted to continue. The page just didn't work for them.
**Program manager (C2):** Now I have one account and a column of blanks, and I'm writing the review note. What can I say?
**Narrator (C3):** The course stops at that note and asks what a careful review would do with both kinds of evidence.

## Closing (1:00–1:39)
**Visual:** The reflection question on screen: "In a report you rely on, who could never appear in the count?" Then the course's title card.
**Narrator (C3):** In this course, you'll practice separating an observation, a possible explanation and a proposed decision, so your equity questions rest on evidence rather than leaps in reasoning. You'll ask who is counted, who is missing and whether a comparison fits the question, recognize that numbers and personal accounts illuminate different parts of an issue, and state what the evidence supports and what remains uncertain, without treating missing information as reassurance. A question to carry in with you: in a report you rely on, who could never appear in the count?

## Transcript notes
A short introduction: a community partner reports that a participant could not use an online confirmation page, while a program manager's report shows only "no response."
