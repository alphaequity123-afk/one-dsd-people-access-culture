# Video script: Language Access Across a Whole Service Encounter

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=I14
- **Length:** about 1.6 minutes (about 227 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Supervisor (C6, Co-host voice), Case manager (C5, Host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning. The only on-screen text is the course title, which the narrator speaks; documents and screens shown in the video carry no legible text.

## Scene 1: More than one translated page (0:00–0:20)
**Visual:** The narrator (C3) beside a plain notice on a table, its text not legible. A title card shows the course title.
**Narrator (C3):** A notice can be translated well and still leave someone stuck at the next step. Language Access Across a Whole Service Encounter prepares you to follow the whole route, from the first information through questions, responses and follow-up, including colleagues who are asked to carry that work.

## Scene 2: Another meeting to interpret (0:20–1:05)
**Visual:** The supervisor (C6) stops at the case manager's (C5) desk. A draft document is open on the screen, with no legible text.
**Supervisor (C6):** Could you sit in on the planning meeting this afternoon and interpret? You're the only one on the team who speaks the language.
**Case manager (C5):** That would be the third meeting like this in a week. I set aside today for the policy comparison you assigned me, and it keeps getting pushed back.
**Supervisor (C6):** I know, but you've never said it was a problem, and you're good at it. People are comfortable with you.
**Case manager (C5):** I'm glad to help. I'd just like to know where the comparison fits, because it's the development work I asked for.
**Supervisor (C6):** Let me think about what to tell the meeting organizer.
**Visual:** The supervisor (C6) pauses at the desk. No decision is shown.

## Closing (1:05–1:35)
**Visual:** The narrator (C3) in the same office setting.
**Narrator (C3):** The course helps you prepare a conversation with qualified language support in place, so you speak directly to the person rather than to the interpreter. It also covers writing source text that keeps conditions and decisions intact through translation, tracing access from invitation to follow-up, and recognizing when reliance on family or bilingual coworkers shifts an inappropriate burden. Consider, too, where one person's language knowledge may be quietly filling a gap in a route you know.

## Transcript notes
Summary for the media manifest: A short introduction: a supervisor asks a bilingual case manager to interpret another unrelated meeting while their policy assignment waits, and the narrator describes what the course offers for language access across a whole service encounter.
