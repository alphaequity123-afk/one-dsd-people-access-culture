# Video script: Using Community Briefs to Ask Better Questions

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=F14
- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 226 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Case manager (C5, Host voice), Supervisor (C6, Co-host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: When preparation meets a person (0:00–0:21)
**Visual:** Title card on a plain background: "Using Community Briefs to Ask Better Questions." It fades to an illustrated desk with a short printed brief, a notepad and a calendar reminder for an intake conversation. No text on the brief is legible.
**Narrator (C3):** A few pages of background can help you walk into a conversation better prepared. They can also tempt you to decide what someone wants before they have said a word. Using Community Briefs to Ask Better Questions prepares you for the moment when your reading meets a real person.

## Scene 2: The day before an intake (0:21–1:01)
**Visual:** The case manager (C5) stops by the supervisor's (C6) office door, holding the printed brief.
**Case manager (C5):** I read the community brief before tomorrow's intake. It talks a lot about family relationships, so I was planning to direct the service questions to the relative who's coming along.
**Supervisor (C6):** What does the brief tell you about this particular person?
**Case manager (C5):** Honestly, nothing about them in particular. It describes a community, a place and a period. And I haven't asked the person who they want involved.
**Supervisor (C6):** So the brief raised a question for you. How do you want to use it tomorrow?
**Narrator (C3):** The course stops at that choice and lets you explore three possible responses.

## Closing (1:01–1:37)
**Visual:** The reflection question on screen: "What might you assume about a person that one open question could let them tell you instead?" Then the course's title card.
**Narrator (C3):** In this course, you'll look at what a community brief can offer and what it cannot establish about an individual. You'll practice separating historical or structural context from assumptions about personal beliefs and behavior, developing respectful questions about meaning, access and preferred support, and recognizing when a group description is being used to predict credibility, compliance or service need. A question to carry in with you: what might you assume about a person that one open question could let them tell you instead?

## Transcript notes
A short introduction: a case manager reads a community brief before an intake and asks a supervisor how to use it without deciding for the person.
