# Video script: Race, Disability, and Overlapping Barriers

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=F08
- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 201 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Case manager (C5, Host voice), Community partner (C1, Co-host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: Two supports that do not connect (0:00–0:18)
**Visual:** A plain title card reading “Race, Disability, and Overlapping Barriers,” which fades to an illustrated office with a laptop on a shared table.
**Narrator (C3):** Two supports can each work on their own and still fail the same person. Race, Disability, and Overlapping Barriers prepares you for moments when racial expectations, disability access, and the way work is divided interact, so that a barrier slips between separate processes.

## Scene 2: After the invitation (0:18–0:49)
**Visual:** The community partner (C1) and the case manager (C5) look at a laptop showing the outline of a registration page. No text on the page is readable.
**Community partner (C1):** The translated invitation went out, and it’s accessible. But when people click through to register, the form is only in English.
**Case manager (C5):** And the access request?
**Community partner (C1):** That only appears after you’ve registered. So some people stop before they ever get to ask.
**Case manager (C5):** We do have translation, and we do have access support. Both are in the plan.
**Narrator (C3):** Each support exists somewhere in the process. Which response best addresses the barrier that connects them?

## Closing (0:49–1:26)
**Visual:** The closing question appears on screen as the narrator reads it: “When your team calls a task complete, whose experience of the whole route tells you it worked?”
**Narrator (C3):** The course treats intersectionality as a way to see how conditions such as race and disability interact, not as a list or ranking of identities. It helps you identify how separate support processes may miss a combined barrier, ask what information could clarify a difference in experience without assuming its cause, and consider an improvement that addresses access and racial equity together. A question to carry into the course: when your team calls a task complete, whose experience of the whole route tells you it worked?

## Transcript notes
A short introduction to Race, Disability, and Overlapping Barriers: a translated, accessible invitation leads to an English-only registration form, with access requests only afterward. The video stops at the decision.
