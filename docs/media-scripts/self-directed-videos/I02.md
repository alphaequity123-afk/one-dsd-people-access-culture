# Video script: Understanding and Repairing Intercultural Misunderstandings

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=I02
- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 195 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Supervisor (C6, Co-host voice), Case manager (C5, Host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: Opening (0:00–0:22)
**Visual:** A title card reads "Understanding and Repairing Intercultural Misunderstandings." The narrator (C3) stands beside it in a quiet office.
**Narrator (C3):** Some conversations seem to go well, and only later does it become clear that a concern went unheard. Understanding and Repairing Intercultural Misunderstandings prepares you for those moments in services, supervision, and partner work, when it helps to separate what you observed from what you inferred before you try to repair anything.

## Scene 2: An answer that sounds like yes (0:22–0:52)
**Visual:** The supervisor (C6) and the case manager (C5) sit across a desk in the supervisor's office. A one-page assignment outline lies between them.
**Supervisor (C6):** So the development assignment would start next month. You'd lead the review of the provider updates. How does that sound?
**Case manager (C5):** It's a good opportunity. It may be difficult to fit, with everything that's already on my calendar.
**Supervisor (C6):** Great. I'll send you the details this afternoon.
**Visual:** The case manager (C5) starts to speak, then stops. The supervisor (C6) is already writing a note.
**Narrator (C3):** The supervisor heard agreement. Here the course stops and asks which response would best clarify the concern.

## Closing (0:52–1:27)
**Visual:** The narrator (C3) on camera in the same quiet office.
**Narrator (C3):** The course gives you practice exploring a disagreement without treating culture as the whole explanation, and offering a specific repair that leaves the other person free to respond in their own way. It also helps you tell a conversation about understanding apart from a concern that belongs in an established reporting route. A question to take with you: when someone answers that something may be difficult, what would you need to ask before treating it as a yes or a no?

## Transcript notes
A supervisor hears agreement when a case manager says a development assignment may be difficult to fit. An introduction to separating what happened from what was inferred, and to making a specific repair.
