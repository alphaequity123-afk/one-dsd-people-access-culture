# Video script: Microaggressions: Recognizing Messages and Responding with Care

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=F07
- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 193 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Case manager (C5, Host voice), Newer colleague (C7, Co-host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: A message inside a comment (0:00–0:22)
**Visual:** A plain title card reading “Microaggressions: Recognizing Messages and Responding with Care,” which fades to an illustrated team meeting.
**Narrator (C3):** A comment that sounds like praise can still carry a message about who is expected to know things. Microaggressions: Recognizing Messages and Responding with Care prepares you for the moment such a comment lands in a meeting, and for choosing a response that keeps attention on the person and on their work.

## Scene 2: The missing address (0:22–0:48)
**Visual:** The case manager (C5) leads a team meeting from the head of the table. The newer colleague (C7) holds a draft notice. Another colleague, shown only from behind, does not speak on camera.
**Newer colleague (C7):** Before we approve the notice, one of the service addresses is missing. People won’t know where to go.
**Narrator (C3):** Another colleague remarks that the newer colleague’s English is “unexpectedly good.” No one returns to the address.
**Case manager (C5):** All right. Let’s move on to the next item.
**Narrator (C3):** The contribution is about to drop out of the discussion. What response would best preserve it?

## Closing (0:48–1:23)
**Visual:** The closing question appears on screen as the narrator reads it: “What would specific, useful praise for a colleague’s work sound like, with no comparison hidden inside it?”
**Narrator (C3):** The course helps you recognize how an everyday comment can communicate exclusion or diminished credibility based on identity. You practice holding stated intent and reported impact together without requiring agreement about motive, and choosing a proportionate response that respects the affected person’s preferences. Questions about whether conduct meets a workplace standard stay with current agency reporting routes. A question to carry into the course: what would specific, useful praise for a colleague’s work sound like, with no comparison hidden inside it?

## Transcript notes
A short introduction to Microaggressions: a newer colleague points out a missing service address, a remark about their English follows, and the meeting moves on. The video stops at the decision.
