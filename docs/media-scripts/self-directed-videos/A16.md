# Video script: Community-Informed Research and Questions that People Can Answer

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=A16
- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 218 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Community partner (C1, Co-host voice), Program manager (C2, Host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: A question that looks finished (0:00–0:25)
**Visual:** A short survey on a tablet. One question appears on screen: "Was the service easy?" The narrator reads it. The course title follows and is spoken. No real logos or real people.
**Narrator (C3):** A question like "Was the service easy?" can look finished long before anyone asks whether people hear it the same way. Community-Informed Research and Questions that People Can Answer is an advanced self-directed course for the moment you write a survey, feedback, research, or evaluation question, or rely on the answers to one.

## Scene 2: A draft that can still change (0:25–1:05)
**Visual:** The community partner (C1) and the program manager (C2) at a table with a printed draft survey. The text on the page is not readable.
**Community partner (C1):** I read through the draft. The item about how quickly staff responded is clear enough, but it misses what the people I work with struggle with most. Often they don't know whom to contact in the first place.
**Program manager (C2):** That's helpful. And the item can still change; we haven't finalized anything.
**Community partner (C1):** I'm not speaking for everyone, and I'd want you to hear from others too. I just don't think speed is the whole story.
**Program manager (C2):** Understood. Now I need to decide how we use what you've told me before the draft closes.

## Closing (1:05–1:35)
**Visual:** The course title card, then the first slide of the course.
**Narrator (C3):** The course helps you define a question that fits the proposed decision and the evidence that is actually available. You'll examine whether people understand a question in different ways, identify missing voices, response conditions, and inappropriate generalization, and prepare a revised question and interpretation that preserve people's particular accounts. Here is a question to bring with you: if two people give your question the same answer, how would you know they meant the same thing?

## Transcript notes
Media manifest summary: A short introduction to Community-Informed Research and Questions that People Can Answer: a community partner says a draft survey item misses the difficulty of knowing whom to contact, while the item can still change.
