# Video script: Responding to Microaggressions With Care and Purpose

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=I06
- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 200 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Program manager (C2, Host voice), Newer colleague (C7, Co-host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: Opening (0:00–0:21)
**Visual:** A title card reads "Responding to Microaggressions With Care and Purpose." The narrator (C3) stands beside it in a hallway outside a meeting room.
**Narrator (C3):** A remark or a small moment in a meeting can send a message about whose knowledge is expected and whose contribution counts. Responding to Microaggressions With Care and Purpose prepares you for the moment after you notice it, when you need to decide what a response should accomplish.

## Scene 2: An idea that changes hands (0:21–0:53)
**Visual:** A team meeting. The newer colleague (C7) and the program manager (C2) sit at the table; other attendees are seen from behind and do not speak. One chair facing the table is empty, standing for the viewer's seat.
**Newer colleague (C7):** For the policy update, I'd propose a short summary at the top, so people can see what changed before they read the full...
**Program manager (C2):** Let's hold that for now. We're short on time.
**Visual:** The wall clock moves ahead several minutes. The newer colleague (C7) sits back.
**Program manager (C2):** One idea I keep coming back to: a short summary at the top, so people can see what changed first.
**Visual:** The camera settles on the empty chair.
**Narrator (C3):** You heard the original proposal. The course stops here and asks which response would best restore it.

## Closing (0:53–1:29)
**Visual:** The narrator (C3) on camera in the hallway.
**Narrator (C3):** The course gives you practice identifying the message and effect of a subtle exclusion without claiming to know another person's intentions, and choosing a response that fits the setting, the relationship, and any safety concerns. It also looks at offering support without speaking for the affected person, and at the routines that let an interaction recur, while formal reporting responsibilities stay in place. A question to take with you: in the meetings you attend, what happens to a contribution after someone is interrupted?

## Transcript notes
A newer colleague's proposal is interrupted and later repeated as someone else's idea. An introduction to deciding what a response to a subtle exclusion needs to accomplish.
