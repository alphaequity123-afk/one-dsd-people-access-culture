# Video script: Dialogue across Difference When Power and Conflict Matter

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learning/self-directed/index.html?course=A03
- **Length:** about 1.5 minutes (about 223 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Supervisor (C6, Co-host voice), Case manager (C5, Host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: When the same question lands differently (0:00–0:20)
**Visual:** A conference room set for a one-to-one meeting, with two chairs at a corner of the table. The course title appears on screen as the narrator (C3) speaks it.
**Narrator (C3):** Some of the hardest conversations at work happen when two people read the same question differently and one of them holds more authority. Dialogue across Difference When Power and Conflict Matter prepares you for those conversations, whether you are facilitating a discussion with community partners or giving feedback to someone you supervise.

## Scene 2: A question about the expectation (0:20–0:55)
**Visual:** The supervisor (C6) and the case manager (C5) sit down for a scheduled check-in. A printed assignment description lies on the table between them.
**Case manager (C5):** Before we go further, can I ask why this assignment is judged by how quickly people respond in meetings?
**Supervisor (C6):** Honestly, that sounds like you’re pushing back on the expectation itself.
**Case manager (C5):** I’m trying to understand how to meet it. If I could give a considered explanation, I think you would see my reasoning more clearly than a quick answer shows.
**Supervisor (C6):** I heard your question as a challenge, and now I’m hearing something different. I’m not sure where to take this conversation from here.
**Visual:** The supervisor (C6) sets down the assignment description. The scene pauses on the two of them, with no resolution.

## Closing (0:55–1:35)
**Visual:** The narrator (C3) in the empty conference room. The closing question appears on screen as the narrator speaks it.
**Narrator (C3):** The course helps you tell a learning conversation apart from a formal decision or complaint process, which keeps its own responsible route, especially when power and conflict are present. You will analyze how authority, communication style, and ways to participate shape a discussion, and choose questions that reveal different meanings without requiring personal disclosure. You will also write an honest account of agreement, disagreement, and matters still open. To carry into the course: when does a question feel respectful to you, and when does it feel like a challenge?

## Transcript notes
A short introduction: during a feedback check-in, a case manager asks why an assignment is judged by rapid meeting responses, the supervisor first hears a challenge, and the scene stops as the supervisor considers where to take the conversation.
