## What is it for?

**Host (female):** A manager gets funding for one navigator and wants them to help any family, in any language, at any step. What's the problem?

**Co-host (male):** One person can't do that. They'd be spread so thin you'd never see a difference.

**Host (female):** And the evidence points the other way. Navigation tends to help most when it's tied to a specific step. A 2021 trial of family navigation for autism found more families got a diagnostic answer within a year, though results varied by clinic. So start by finding where people get lost in your own process, and focus there.


## Writing the scope

**Co-host (male):** Then you write the job.

**Host (female):** Write the duties, and write what the role is not. The C3 Project's list of community health worker roles is a good menu: cultural mediation, system navigation, advocacy, outreach. And the not list matters just as much. Not an interpreter unless they're qualified and hired for it. Not a decision maker. Not overflow case management.


## The hidden version

**Co-host (male):** Here's the one I see. No budget, so we ask our bilingual staff to just come along on a few visits.

**Host (female):** That's navigation without the job. It's unpaid, nobody counts it, and it can leave that person caught between their community and their work. Name it, count it, and either make it part of a recognized role or move it to one.


## Inside or outside

**Co-host (male):** Should the navigator work for the agency, or for a community organization?

**Host (female):** Both can work. Inside, you get easier access to systems. Through a community partner, you often get deeper trust. M Health Fairview's cultural brokers, for example, spend most of their time at trusted community organizations.


## Keeping it going

**Co-host (male):** And once they're hired?

**Host (female):** Supervise both sides of the work. Keep navigators out of enforcement and bad-news roles, so they keep the community's trust. And plan caseloads with secondary traumatic stress in mind. Hearing hard stories every day has a cost.

**Co-host (male):** A clear purpose, a written scope, fair pay and real support. A role, not a favor.
