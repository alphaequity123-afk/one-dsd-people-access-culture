# Video script: Family Takes Care of Family

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast. Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /courses/cn-what-a-navigator-does (What a Cultural Navigator Does)
- **Length:** about 3 minutes
- **Voices:** Each character uses their approved named reference from `config/character-voices.json`; see `docs/media-scripts/CAST.md`.
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken.

**Cast:**

- **Narrator:** C3 (Kelly voice)
- **Assessor:** C8 (Noor voice)
- **Navigator:** C1 (Brian voice)
- **Case manager:** C5 (Susan voice)
- **The person's mother:** voice only, off camera (Host voice, a different delivery from the assessor). The family is not shown, so no cast member is placed in a community.
- **Interpreter:** present in the scene, off camera, no separate lines. The mother's words are heard as the interpreter renders them.

**Scene 1. A family's living room, afternoon. The assessor sits with a laptop. The navigator sits beside the family, off frame.**

NARRATOR: This is an assessment for disability services. A qualified interpreter is here for the words. The family asked for a navigator from their community to come too. Watch what happens with one question.

ASSESSOR: Who helps at home during the day, and at night?

MOTHER (voice only, through the interpreter): We all help. Family takes care of family.

ASSESSOR: (typing) Thank you. So daily help is covered by family.

**Scene 2. Same room, a moment later.**

NAVIGATOR: (to the mother, gently) Would you like to tell them about your work schedule?

MOTHER (voice only, through the interpreter): I work nights. We did not want you to think we cannot manage.

NAVIGATOR: (to the assessor) Asking for help can feel like admitting you're not managing. It may help to ask about the nights.

ASSESSOR: (stops typing) Thank you. That changes things. Can we go night by night? Who is home on the nights you work, and what does the day after look like?

NARRATOR: The navigator didn't answer for the family. They helped the family say what was true, and helped the assessor ask the question that found it.

**Scene 3. An office, the next day. The assessor and the case manager.**

CASE MANAGER: I read your notes. The first answer would have left nights uncovered.

ASSESSOR: It would have. And I would have written it down with total confidence.

CASE MANAGER: Did the navigator interpret?

ASSESSOR: No. We had an interpreter for that. The navigator helped the family understand why I was asking, and helped me understand what I was hearing.

CASE MANAGER: I'll let the navigator know what happens next, if the family is OK with that.

**Scene 4. Closing.**

NARRATOR: A cultural navigator is a trusted go-between who helps a person and a system understand each other. The family decides whether a navigator joins, and what is shared. When an answer could mean more than one thing, check it before it becomes part of a plan.

NARRATOR: Think of one question you ask often. How could the answer mean more than one thing?
