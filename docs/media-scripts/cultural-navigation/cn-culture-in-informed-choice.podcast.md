## A blank that isn't blank

**Host (female):** I want to start with a field on a form. Cultural needs. And the answer, again and again, is none identified.

**Co-host (male):** Honestly, that question feels awkward to ask. People usually mention what matters to them.

**Host (female):** Usually they don't, at least not to someone from an agency. And Minnesota law treats it as part of the process. For people using waiver services, informed decision making includes identification of the person's cultural needs and access to culturally responsive services and providers.


## Asking well

**Co-host (male):** So how do you ask without it being awkward or stereotyping?

**Host (female):** Open questions about what matters. What's important to you in how people help you at home? Anything about your faith, food or routines helpers should know? Who do you want with you when decisions are made? Which language are you most comfortable in?

**Co-host (male):** And not, people from that community usually prefer this.

**Host (female):** Exactly. Background can suggest a question. It never answers it.


## Finding a provider

**Co-host (male):** Say the person wants helpers who speak their language, and there aren't any in the county.

**Host (female):** Widen the search. Ask navigators and community organizations what they know. Ask providers specific questions about what they could adapt. Bring the options back and let the person choose. And record honestly what you looked for and didn't find.

**Co-host (male):** Why record the gap?

**Host (female):** Because a hidden gap never gets fixed. One record is a person's story. A pattern of the same gap is something the program can act on.


## Writing it down

**Co-host (male):** And in the plan itself?

**Host (female):** Be specific, in the person's words. Prefers family tells a new helper almost nothing. Wants their sister present for decisions, prefers a female helper for bathing, tells them what to do. And ask again when life changes.
