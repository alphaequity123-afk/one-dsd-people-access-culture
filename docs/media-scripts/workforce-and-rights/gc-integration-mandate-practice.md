# Video script: The Integration Mandate in Practice: Olmstead and the HCBS Settings Rule

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /courses/gc-integration-mandate-practice
- **Length:** about 3 minutes (about 415 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Discussion host 1 (C8, Host voice), Discussion host 2 (C4, Co-host voice), Program manager (C2, Host voice), Community partner (C1, Co-host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: A claim that travels fast (0:00–0:30)
**Visual:** A plain title card reading "The Integration Mandate in Practice," which fades to an illustrated video call. The program manager (C2) sits at a desk in a DSD office; the community partner (C1) appears on the laptop screen from a county office. No text on either screen is readable.
**Discussion host 1 (C8):** In 2026, the federal government changed how it reads the law on community integration. For people working in disability services, that change did not stay in Washington. It showed up in phone calls, provider meetings and support plans, often before anyone had read the documents themselves.

## Scene 2: The county partner's question (0:30–1:50)
**Visual:** The community partner (C1) holds up a printed news article, its headline blurred. The program manager (C2) takes notes.
**Community partner (C1):** Thanks for making time. One of our residential providers told our case managers last week that the integration mandate is gone. They pointed to this federal opinion from June.
**Program manager (C2):** I've seen the coverage, and I've had two other calls about it this week. What is the provider planning to change?
**Community partner (C1):** They want to stop supporting residents' activities outside the house. Group outings only, on the provider's schedule. They say staffing is thin and the law no longer requires more.
**Program manager (C2):** And your case managers are hearing this from residents too?
**Community partner (C1):** One resident has a job interview across town next week. Another goes to a faith community on Sunday mornings. Both asked their case managers what happens now. I need to tell my team something by Friday, and I'd rather it be something DSD stands behind.
**Program manager (C2):** That's fair. I don't want to tell you the law is unchanged if it isn't. But I also don't want to treat one federal opinion as if it settled everything.
**Community partner (C1):** So which is it? Does the provider have a point or not?
**Program manager (C2):** That depends on which rule we're talking about. Some of what governs that house didn't come from the opinion at all. And I want to be careful not to answer a legal question I'm not the one to answer.
**Discussion host 2 (C4):** The partner needs a clear answer. The program manager needs to be accurate about what changed, what did not, and who decides.

## Closing (1:50–3:00)
**Visual:** The call freezes on both faces. On screen, four labeled layers stack one above another as the hosts name them: "Americans with Disabilities Act," "HCBS settings rule," "Minnesota law," "Olmstead Plan."
**Discussion host 1 (C8):** This course works through that moment and others like it. You will separate four layers that an integration question can rest on: the Americans with Disabilities Act and how courts and federal agencies read it, the federal home and community-based services settings rule, Minnesota law on informed choice, and Minnesota's Olmstead Plan. Then you will take on the hard cases a DSD leader meets: settings that isolate people even when they look like homes, a person whose informed choice surprises you, and a choice the system cannot yet meet because providers lack capacity. Formal legal and compliance decisions stay with the people and areas responsible. Your part is to ask the right question and get it to them. The course begins with lesson one: Four layers of the integration duty after 2026.

## Transcript notes
A county partner asks DSD whether a provider can cut community activities after the 2026 federal opinion on the integration mandate. The video introduces the layers of law involved and stops before the program manager answers.
