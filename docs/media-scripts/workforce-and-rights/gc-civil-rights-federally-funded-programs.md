# Video script: Civil Rights in Federally Funded Programs: Title VI, Section 504 and Section 1557

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /courses/gc-civil-rights-federally-funded-programs
- **Length:** about 3 minutes (about 425 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Discussion host 1 (C8, Host voice), Discussion host 2 (C4, Co-host voice), Program manager (C2, Host voice), Community partner (C1, Co-host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: The template on the screen (0:00–0:30)
**Visual:** A small DSD conference room with a laptop on the table. The program manager (C2) sits at the table; the community partner (C1) appears on a video call on a wall screen. A draft intake notice is shared on the screen. The heading reads "Draft statewide notice," which the hosts speak.
**Discussion host 1 (C8):** This is a draft statewide notice. DHS writes it, counties mail it, and the service behind it is paid for partly with federal funds. Before it goes out, the program manager has asked a county partner to look at it.

## Scene 2: Two counties, one template (0:30–1:50)
**Visual:** The program manager (C2) scrolls through the notice. The community partner (C1) leans toward the camera.
**Community partner (C1):** I'll be honest about how this works on our end. When the state sends a notice like this in English only, we translate it ourselves for the languages we see most. It takes us about two weeks, and our budget for it is gone by spring.
**Program manager (C2):** And the counties that can't do that?
**Community partner (C1):** They mail the English version. The county next to ours does. Same notice, same deadline, and a family over there may not understand it until the deadline has passed.
**Program manager (C2):** There's also a signature step on page two. You have to sign in person at the county office.
**Community partner (C1):** We take signatures by mail when someone can't travel. I don't know that anyone told us we could. We just started doing it.
**Program manager (C2):** Someone on my team said civil rights at intake is the county's job, because you're the ones meeting people.
**Community partner (C1):** We do carry our part. But we didn't write this notice. If the barrier is in the template, every county gets it. And whoever fixes it, people need the notice before the deadline, not after.
**Program manager (C2):** And the federal guidance on language access changed last year. I'm not sure what we're still required to do, and what we'd simply be choosing to do.

## Scene 3: The question on the table (1:50–2:20)
**Visual:** The program manager (C2) looks from the notice to a notepad with three lines written on it, which the hosts read: "Who carries the duty? What changed? What do we build in now?"
**Discussion host 2 (C4):** Three questions are on the table. Who carries a civil rights duty when federal money passes from DHS to counties, lead agencies and providers? What has changed in the federal rules, and what has not? And what should a program build in before launch, rather than repair after a complaint? None of these has a one-line answer, and the rules behind them have moved more than once since 2024.

## Closing (2:20–3:00)
**Visual:** The course title appears on screen, followed by the four lesson titles, each read aloud.
**Discussion host 1 (C8):** This course works through Title VI, Section 504 and Section 1557 from a program leader's seat. You'll trace how duties travel down the chain of funding, look at language access now that federal guidance has shifted, work through program access, reasonable modification and the most integrated setting, and see what is and is not settled about Section 1557, ending with a short review you can run on your own program. Formal decisions about whether a law applies stay with legal counsel and the people responsible for civil rights at DHS. Your part is to see the questions early. We begin with lesson one: Who carries the duty: recipients, subrecipients and the chain of funding.

## Transcript notes
A county partner and a DSD program manager review a state notice and find barriers built into the template. The course covers Title VI, Section 504 and Section 1557 duties for state agencies and subrecipients, and how to build compliance into program design.
