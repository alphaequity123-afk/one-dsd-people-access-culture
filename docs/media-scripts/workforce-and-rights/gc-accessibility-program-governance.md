# Video script: Running an Accessibility Program: Standards, Exceptions and Accountability

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /courses/gc-accessibility-program-governance
- **Length:** about 3 minutes (about 430 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Program manager (C2, Host voice), Supervisor (C6, Co-host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: A question from leadership (0:00–0:30)
**Visual:** A DSD office late in the afternoon. The narrator (C3) stands beside a wall calendar. The course title appears on screen as the narrator speaks it.
**Narrator (C3):** Running an Accessibility Program: Standards, Exceptions and Accountability. Every accessibility program eventually gets a simple question from leadership: are we going to make it? The honest answer is rarely simple, because the program answers to several rules at once, and different people decide different parts of it. Some of those rules come from a federal regulation, some from a state standard, and some from a contract or a building lease.

## Scene 2: Drafting the answer (0:30–2:05)
**Visual:** A small meeting room. The supervisor (C6) sits with a laptop open to a draft briefing. The program manager (C2) has a printed spreadsheet with several rows highlighted. The draft line on screen is the one the supervisor reads aloud.
**Supervisor (C6):** Leadership wants one line for the quarterly briefing. I've drafted, "On track for the federal web deadline." The date moved once already, so we have some room.
**Program manager (C2):** I'd be careful with that line. The federal date isn't our starting point. The state standard already applies to us, and so do the communication duties we've always had.
**Supervisor (C6):** Fair. But the web team says most of the pages they've checked pass.
**Program manager (C2):** Most of the sixty pages they scanned pass. We have about nine hundred. And nobody has tested the vendor's intake form, because nobody is sure whose it is. The web team thinks procurement owns it, and procurement thinks we do. And facilities books the rooms for our listening sessions, but nobody checks the hearing loop before we use them.
**Supervisor (C6):** What are the highlighted rows?
**Program manager (C2):** Exceptions. Four of them are past their review dates. One is a scheduling tool we bought last year knowing people couldn't book by keyboard. The plan was to revisit it at renewal, and renewal came and went.
**Supervisor (C6):** So if I write "on track," I'm not exactly lying, but I'm describing sixty pages as if they were the whole program.
**Program manager (C2):** And if you write that we're behind, leadership will ask who's responsible for each piece. Right now I can't answer that for the form, or for the meeting rooms.
**Supervisor (C6):** Then the real question is what we can honestly say, and who actually decides the hard parts.
**Visual:** The supervisor (C6) looks at the draft line, cursor blinking after the word "track." The scene holds; nothing is changed.

## Closing (2:05–3:00)
**Visual:** The narrator (C3) back beside the wall calendar. Each lesson title appears on screen as the narrator speaks it.
**Narrator (C3):** This course is for leaders who own digital and physical access across a program. You will map each duty to an owner and to the person who decides it, set priorities you can defend from an inventory that will never be complete, keep exceptions narrow and reviewed, use purchasing to prevent barriers rather than buy them, and report progress so that a reader can check it. Rules and dates change, so the course also shows you where to confirm the current requirement and who to ask. As you begin, think about the program you know best and whether you could name, for each piece of it, who decides. The first lesson is One program, several sources of duty: who answers for what.

## Transcript notes
A supervisor drafting a quarterly line saying an accessibility program is on track learns from the program manager that the figure covers 60 of about 900 pages, a vendor form has no clear owner, and four exceptions are overdue for review.
