# Video script: When an Accessibility Barrier Is Reported: Triage, Remediation and Equally Effective Access

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /courses/gc-accessibility-barrier-remediation
- **Length:** about 3 minutes (about 420 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Case manager (C5, Host voice), Community partner (C1, Co-host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: The call (0:00–0:25)
**Visual:** A DSD workspace in the morning. The narrator (C3) stands near a row of desks. The course title appears on screen as the narrator speaks it.
**Narrator (C3):** When an Accessibility Barrier Is Reported: Triage, Remediation and Equally Effective Access. A barrier report usually arrives as an ordinary call or email. What happens in the next hour, and in the next month, decides whether the person gets access and whether the barrier is still there for the next person. Some barriers are found by testing. Many more are found by people who were simply trying to get something done.

## Scene 2: Twelve days (0:25–2:05)
**Visual:** The case manager (C5) at a desk with a headset, a service notice open on screen. The notice is a scanned image of a printed page, with no selectable text. A split screen shows the community partner (C1) on a video call from a county office.
**Community partner (C1):** I'm calling for someone I work with who uses a screen reader. They got a notice about a change to their services, and it's a scanned PDF. Their software reads it as a blank page.
**Case manager (C5):** I'm looking at the same notice now. You're right, it's just an image. Do you know when they have to respond?
**Community partner (C1):** The notice says they can ask for a meeting within the next twelve days. They want to, but they don't know what's changing. And they'd rather not explain their situation to three different people to get there.
**Case manager (C5):** Here's my problem. That notice comes out of a template another unit manages. I can report it today, but a fix to the template could take weeks.
**Community partner (C1):** They don't have weeks. Could you just read it to me now, and I'll pass it along?
**Case manager (C5):** I could. It would be fast. But then you'd be hearing their private details, and they'd be getting their own notice secondhand.
**Community partner (C1):** And this is the third time this year someone in our group has called about a letter like this. Each time somebody fixes the one letter.
**Case manager (C5):** So there are really two problems on two different timelines. This person needs to read their notice before the deadline, and the template keeps producing the same barrier for everyone else.
**Community partner (C1):** Which one do you start with?
**Visual:** The case manager (C5) looks from the notice to a sticky note reading "12 days." The scene holds on the question; no answer is given.

## Closing (2:05–3:00)
**Visual:** The narrator (C3) back in the workspace. Each lesson title appears on screen as the narrator speaks it.
**Narrator (C3):** This course follows a barrier report from the first call to the program change it should lead to. You will give the person access now, in the format they ask for and in time to act, and test whether an alternative is truly as effective as what others receive. You will triage competing reports, trace a barrier to the template, tool or process that made it, and assign the repair to the owner who can change it. You will also decide when an interim workaround is acceptable, and what keeps it from quietly becoming permanent. Finally, you will tell the person what changed and learn from the pattern of reports, including the reports that never arrive. The first lesson is Access now: the first response to a reported barrier.

## Transcript notes
A case manager takes a call from a community partner about a scanned notice a screen reader user cannot read, with twelve days to respond, and realizes the person's access and the template that keeps producing the barrier run on different timelines.
