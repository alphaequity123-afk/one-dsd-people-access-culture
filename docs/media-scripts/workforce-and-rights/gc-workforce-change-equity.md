# Video script: Reorganizations, Reassignments and Layoffs With an Equity Lens

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /courses/gc-workforce-change-equity
- **Length:** about 3 minutes (about 406 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Program manager (C2, Host voice), Supervisor (C6, Co-host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: Boxes and lines (0:00–0:22)
**Visual:** A plain title card reading "Reorganizations, Reassignments and Layoffs With an Equity Lens," which fades to an illustrated organization chart. Two boxes slide together into one, and an arrow points to a drawing of a different office building.
**Narrator (C3):** Reorganizations, Reassignments and Layoffs With an Equity Lens. On paper, a reorganization is boxes and lines. For the people inside it, it is a commute, a caseload, a supervisor, and sometimes a job. Whether the change is fair often depends on questions nobody asked while the boxes were still being drawn.

## Scene 2: The draft plan (0:22–2:07)
**Visual:** The program manager (C2) and the supervisor (C6) sit at a small table with the organization chart on a laptop between them. One box is labeled on screen "Liaison duties absorbed," and the supervisor reads the label aloud.
**Program manager (C2):** Leadership wants the merged structure by the end of next week. Functions, positions, reporting lines. I think this covers it.
**Supervisor (C6):** It covers the boxes. Can I ask about the people in them?
**Program manager (C2):** Go ahead. Better now than after it's announced.
**Supervisor (C6):** The new site is across the metro. Some of my team built their schedules around where we are now, and at least a couple have accommodations tied to this location. I don't know the details, and I shouldn't.
**Program manager (C2):** Right. That's for the ADA coordinator, not a planning spreadsheet. But you're saying the move could break arrangements that work today.
**Supervisor (C6):** It could, and nobody would have decided to break them. And this box says "liaison duties absorbed." That's two positions. If the reduction follows seniority, those are the newest people in the class, the ones we hired for the language and county work.
**Program manager (C2):** The contract sets the order. I can't change that.
**Supervisor (C6):** I know, and I'm not asking you to. I'm asking whether anyone has looked at what the counties lose if that work goes, or whether it's being cut because someone labeled it non-core.
**Program manager (C2):** Nobody has looked. And if I raise it now, I'm the one slowing everything down.
**Supervisor (C6):** If we don't raise it, we find out in six months, from families waiting for callbacks and from the people left covering the work.
**Narrator (C3):** The contract sets some of the rules. The design is still open. What should the program manager bring to leadership next week?

## Closing (2:07–2:55)
**Visual:** The organization chart returns, and five small icons without text appear beside it one at a time: a group of people, a document, a wheelchair access symbol, a speech bubble and a calendar. The screen then shows the first lesson title, which the narrator reads.
**Narrator (C3):** This course is for people who plan workforce change. You will map who bears the effects of a reorganization, reassignment or layoff before names are attached, including effects that are easy to miss. You will look at how seniority and contract rules shape what is possible, what federal law says about seniority systems, and why equity analysis does its work in the decisions made before seniority applies, with labor relations interpreting the rules. You will plan for accommodations to carry through a change, communicate fairly with the people affected, and check the result afterward against what you expected, then report back honestly. The first lesson is "Who bears the impact."

## Transcript notes
A program manager and a supervisor review a draft reorganization that merges units, moves sites and absorbs liaison work. The video stops before a decision and introduces the course on planning workforce change with an equity lens.
