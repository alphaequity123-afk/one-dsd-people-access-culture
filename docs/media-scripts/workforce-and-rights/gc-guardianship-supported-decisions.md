# Video script: Guardianship, Supported Decision-Making and Less Restrictive Alternatives

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /courses/gc-guardianship-supported-decisions
- **Length:** about 3 minutes (about 405 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Case manager (C5, Host voice), Supervisor (C6, Co-host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: A checklist item (0:00–0:30)
**Visual:** A plain title card reading "Guardianship, Supported Decision-Making and Less Restrictive Alternatives," which fades to an illustrated DSD office. The case manager (C5) knocks on the open door of the supervisor's (C6) office, holding a printed checklist. No text on the page is readable.
**Narrator (C3):** For many families, the year a young person turns eighteen comes with a list of things to do. Sometimes one item on that list is guardianship, and it arrives as if it were simply the next step. Teachers, doctors and providers may all repeat it, each meaning well.

## Scene 2: What the family heard (0:30–1:50)
**Visual:** The case manager (C5) sits across from the supervisor (C6) and sets the checklist on the desk between them.
**Case manager (C5):** Do you have a minute? I just got off the phone with a parent. Their young adult turns eighteen in March, and the school gave the family this checklist. Guardianship is the third item, right after the state ID.
**Supervisor (C6):** What did the parent ask you?
**Case manager (C5):** Whether they should file now or wait until spring. The school told them it's required. Their doctor said the same thing.
**Supervisor (C6):** And the young adult? Have you talked with them about any of this?
**Case manager (C5):** Last month, at their planning meeting. They want to keep working at the grocery store and pick their own apartment someday. They ask an older sibling for help with paperwork, and the sibling walks through the letters with them. Guardianship never came up.
**Supervisor (C6):** So the family is hearing one message from everyone around them, and the young adult may hear it for the first time from the family.
**Case manager (C5):** That's what worries me. I want to tell the parent it isn't required. But I'm not a lawyer, and I don't want to give legal advice.
**Supervisor (C6):** You're right to be careful. Telling someone what to file isn't our role. But saying nothing leaves them with only what the school said.
**Case manager (C5):** So where is the line? What can I actually say to this parent when I call back this afternoon?
**Narrator (C3):** The case manager wants to help without overstepping. The supervisor sees that silence is not neutral either.

## Closing (1:50–3:00)
**Visual:** The two colleagues look at the checklist. On screen, the words "Less restrictive alternatives" appear above four short labels as the narrator names them: "Supported decision making," "Health care directive," "Power of attorney," "Representative payee."
**Narrator (C3):** This course works through that line. You will learn what Minnesota law requires a court to find before it appoints a guardian, including why less restrictive means such as supported decision making must be considered first. You will compare alternatives such as supported decision making, health care directives, powers of attorney and representative payees, and what each one actually authorizes. You will look at the rights a person keeps under guardianship and how a guardianship can change or end. And you will practice supporting a person's own decisions in daily work. Legal and guardianship decisions stay with the courts and the people responsible; staff share public information and route legal questions. The course begins with lesson one: What a court must find before guardianship.

## Transcript notes
A family has been told guardianship is required at eighteen, while the young adult wants to make their own choices with a sibling's help. The video introduces what Minnesota law requires and the alternatives, and stops before the staff decide what to say.
