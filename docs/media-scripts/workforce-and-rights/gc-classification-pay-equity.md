# Video script: Classification, Pay and Unrecognized Work

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /courses/gc-classification-pay-equity
- **Length:** about 3 minutes (about 406 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Supervisor (C6, Co-host voice), Case manager (C5, Host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: Work between the assignments (0:00–0:20)
**Visual:** A plain title card reading "Classification, Pay and Unrecognized Work," which fades to an illustrated open-plan office in the afternoon. A desk phone, a laptop and a stack of program letters sit on one desk.
**Narrator (C3):** Classification, Pay and Unrecognized Work. Some of the most important work on a team never appears in writing. It happens between assignments, a few minutes at a time, and the people who rely on it may not notice how much of it there is.

## Scene 2: After the call (0:20–2:00)
**Visual:** The case manager (C5) sets down the desk phone and turns back to the laptop. The supervisor (C6) stops beside the desk holding a printed work plan. No text on the plan is readable.
**Supervisor (C6):** That sounded like a hard call.
**Case manager (C5):** It was a family from another unit. Their letter said one service was ending, and they thought it meant everything was ending. I explained it in our language, and then I let their worker know what they were worried about.
**Supervisor (C6):** How often does that happen?
**Case manager (C5):** Most weeks. A few calls, sometimes a meeting. And people ask me how to approach families from that community before they call. I don't mind. It matters.
**Supervisor (C6):** I'm updating work plans, and none of that is in your position description.
**Case manager (C5):** No. It just sort of grew. Nobody asked me to do it, and nobody asked me to stop. And my own case notes are behind, which is the part that shows up in my review.
**Supervisor (C6):** So the work that helps everyone isn't written down anywhere, and the work that slips because of it is.
**Case manager (C5):** I've wondered whether it should count for something. Is that a classification question? A pay question? I don't even know who I'd ask.
**Supervisor (C6):** I'm not sure either. I don't want to promise you something I can't deliver. I also don't want to keep sending you calls just because you're good at them, and I'm not sure every one of those calls should be coming to a staff member at all.
**Narrator (C3):** Two colleagues, both acting in good faith. What should the supervisor do with what they just learned?

## Closing (2:00–2:55)
**Visual:** The office scene softens. Four short phrases appear one at a time as the narrator names them: "Interpreting," "Cultural brokering," "Informal training," "Emotional labor." The screen then shows the first lesson title, which the narrator reads.
**Narrator (C3):** This course looks at how classification and pay decisions can miss work like interpreting, cultural brokering, informal training and emotional labor. You will practice describing duties so the record matches the work, without shrinking or inflating it. You will learn when Minnesota's procedure on work out of class applies, and what a classification review can and cannot do, including what it can mean for the person in the position. You will also look at Minnesota's pay equity history for state employees: what that law compares and what it leaves open. Classification and pay decisions belong to Human Resources and labor relations, and contracts and plans differ, so the course focuses on bringing them accurate facts and asking the right questions. The first lesson is "Work the classification does not name."

## Transcript notes
A case manager explains a program letter to a family in a shared language, work that appears nowhere in writing, and a supervisor wonders how to respond. The video stops at the decision and introduces the course on classification, pay and unrecognized work.
