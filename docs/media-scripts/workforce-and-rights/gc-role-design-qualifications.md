# Video script: Role Design and Minimum Qualifications That Measure the Job

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /courses/gc-role-design-qualifications
- **Length:** about 3 minutes (about 420 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Supervisor (C6, Co-host voice), Case manager (C5, Host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: A vacancy and an old document (0:00–0:25)
**Visual:** An open DSD workspace in the afternoon. The supervisor (C6) sits at a desk with a printed position description, several lines highlighted in yellow. The case manager (C5) pulls up a chair. No names or real documents appear; the highlighted lines are read aloud in the dialogue.

**Narrator (C3):** A lead worker has just accepted a job in another division. Their supervisor wants the vacancy posted by Friday, and Human Resources can do that, as long as the old posting is reused exactly as written.

## Scene 2: Reading the requirements aloud (0:25–1:55)
**Visual:** The supervisor (C6) reads from the highlighted page. The case manager (C5) listens, then points to lines on the page as they respond.

**Supervisor (C6):** Thanks for coming over. You've done half this job for two years, so tell me if this still sounds right. "Lift fifty pounds. Valid driver's license. Bachelor's degree required. Excellent verbal communication."

**Case manager (C5):** Fifty pounds? The heaviest thing we lift is the training binders, maybe twice a year.

**Supervisor (C6):** It's been in there forever. I figured somebody had a reason.

**Case manager (C5):** And the license. We do site visits once a month, but people get there all kinds of ways. The person who just left didn't have a degree when they started, either. They came up through a county.

**Supervisor (C6):** So do I take it all out? If I post something vague, I'll get two hundred applications and no way to sort them.

**Case manager (C5):** I'm not saying vague. I'm saying it should describe what we actually do, and what someone needs on day one versus what we can teach. About a third of our calls this year were with Somali-speaking families, and we wait days for interpreters.

**Supervisor (C6):** Then I want someone who speaks Somali. Can I even put that in a posting?

**Case manager (C5):** Maybe? But how would we check it, and at what level? Talking with a family on the phone isn't the same as translating a notice.

**Supervisor (C6):** And if I change too much, Human Resources might tell me it's a different classification.

**Case manager (C5):** Probably worth asking them before Friday, not after.

**Supervisor (C6):** Friday's starting to look optimistic.

## Scene 3: What this course covers (1:55–2:50)
**Visual:** On-screen text, read by the narrator: "Position description. Essential functions. Minimum qualifications. Language skills." Each phrase appears as it is spoken.

**Narrator (C3):** Every requirement on that page will decide who gets considered, so it has to measure the job. This course is for the people who write and review position descriptions and postings. You'll check a position description against the real work before a vacancy is posted. You'll sort essential functions from marginal ones and describe them in a way that leaves room for accommodation. You'll test minimum qualifications, including when a degree can be required and how equivalent experience counts. And you'll decide when a language is a genuine qualification, at what level, and who needs to review the design before it goes out. Each lesson includes a practice case, a decision with feedback and a job aid you can use on your next posting. Formal decisions on classification, qualifications and accommodation stay with Human Resources and the people and areas responsible for them.

## Closing (2:50–3:05)
**Visual:** The case manager (C5) and the supervisor (C6) set the old posting aside and start a fresh page headed with the position's purpose, written in a single line.

**Narrator (C3):** We begin with the first lesson: The position description comes before the posting.

## Transcript notes
Summary for the media manifest: A supervisor and a case manager read an old posting aloud and find requirements that no longer fit the job. The course covers position descriptions, essential functions, minimum qualifications and language skills as qualifications.
