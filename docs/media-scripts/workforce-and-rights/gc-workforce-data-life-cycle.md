# Video script: Reading Workforce Data Across the Employee Life Cycle

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /courses/gc-workforce-data-life-cycle
- **Length:** about 3 minutes (about 420 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Discussion host 1 (C8, Host voice), Discussion host 2 (C4, Co-host voice), Supervisor (C6, Co-host voice), Program manager (C2, Host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: A report before the meeting (0:00–0:25)
**Visual:** A small DSD conference room in the morning. The supervisor (C6) sits at a laptop showing a bar chart of promotions into lead roles over three years, broken out by race. The bars are visibly uneven. The program manager (C2) comes in carrying a printed table. The chart's title and labels are described in the narration; no names or real data appear.

**Discussion host 1 (C8):** A division's workforce report has just arrived. On the screen is a chart of promotions into lead roles over three years, broken out by race. The bars are not even, and a leadership meeting starts in an hour.

## Scene 2: Two readings of one chart (0:25–1:55)
**Visual:** The program manager (C2) sits beside the supervisor (C6) and sets the printed table next to the laptop. They lean in toward the screen as they talk.

**Supervisor (C6):** Have you seen this? Our promotion rate for staff of color is well below everyone else's. I want to walk in with a plan. Maybe mentoring, maybe a hard look at who's been doing the interviewing.

**Program manager (C2):** I've been staring at it too. Before we pick a fix, can I show you what's underneath? Human Resources sent the interview results. Once people reach the interview, the rates look pretty similar.

**Supervisor (C6):** So where's the gap coming from?

**Program manager (C2):** Maybe earlier. Acting lead assignments. Those get arranged informally, and the only record is a pay adjustment. I can't tell from this who was even considered.

**Supervisor (C6):** That's a lot of maybes. People are going to ask me which teams are the problem.

**Program manager (C2):** I know. And some of these bars are tiny. One unit had three promotions total. If I put that up by race, everyone in the room will know exactly who we mean.

**Supervisor (C6):** Then what about disability? Somebody will ask.

**Program manager (C2):** A lot of people never answered that question, or answered it years ago. The unknown column is bigger than the group we'd be measuring. I'm not sure the number means what it looks like.

**Supervisor (C6):** So I walk in with nothing?

**Program manager (C2):** Not nothing. I just don't want a chart to become a verdict about somebody before we know what it's telling us.

**Supervisor (C6):** And I don't want us sitting on something real because the data are messy.

## Scene 3: What this course covers (1:55–2:50)
**Visual:** On-screen text, read by the hosts: "Stages. Small numbers. Missing data. What you can conclude." Each phrase appears as it is spoken.

**Discussion host 2 (C4):** Both of them have a fair point. This course is for leaders who have to read workforce data and act on it responsibly. You'll follow people through the life cycle as a chain of decisions, from application and interview through acting assignments, promotion, discipline and separation, each with its own denominator. You'll see why small numbers swing, and how a table can expose someone even without names. You'll work with missing data, including disability self-identification, and test whether a conclusion survives what you don't know. And you'll practice writing what a pattern can and cannot show, then turning it into a question for the process rather than a verdict on a person. Each lesson includes a practice case and a decision with feedback. Formal employment and civil-rights decisions stay with the people and areas responsible for them.

## Closing (2:50–3:05)
**Visual:** The supervisor (C6) and the program manager (C2) look at the chart together. The program manager sketches a row of boxes on a notepad, one for each stage, with an arrow running from the first box to the last.

**Discussion host 1 (C8):** We begin with the first lesson: The life cycle as a chain of decisions.

## Transcript notes
Summary for the media manifest: A supervisor and a program manager read a promotion chart an hour before a meeting and disagree about what it shows. The course covers life cycle stages, small numbers, missing data and what a leader can conclude.
