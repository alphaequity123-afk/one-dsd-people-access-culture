# Video script: Employing and Keeping People With Disabilities in State Service

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /courses/gc-disability-employment-goals
- **Length:** about 3 minutes (about 420 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Supervisor (C6, Co-host voice), Program manager (C2, Host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: A goal and a summary (0:00–0:30)
**Visual:** An open-plan DSD office in the morning. A laptop shows a simple bar chart with the title "Self-identified disability, division summary." The bars carry no numbers. No real logos or real people.
**Narrator (C3):** Minnesota has set goals for employing people with disabilities in state service. Goals are easy to quote and harder to keep. On the screen is a division summary of how many staff self-identify as having a disability. A supervisor and a program manager are reading it together, and they are reaching for different next steps. The goal behind that chart is a target for the whole executive branch, and every count on it depends on people choosing to disclose.

## Scene 2: A moment at work (0:30–1:55)
**Visual:** The supervisor (C6) and the program manager (C2) sit at a small table beside the laptop. The supervisor has a notepad; the program manager has a coffee cup and looks at the chart.
**Supervisor (C6):** Leadership wants our disability numbers up before the next report. I was thinking I'd send the team a note asking everyone to update their Self Service records. Maybe mention it at staff meeting, too.
**Program manager (C2):** A general reminder might be fine. But I keep thinking about the analyst who left our section in the spring. They waited most of the winter for the software they needed. If people saw that, would they want to be counted?
**Supervisor (C6):** That's fair, but it was one delay. And the goal is ten percent. That's what the order says.
**Program manager (C2):** Is it still? I'm not sure anyone has checked what's current. And our section is small. If two people update their records, the number jumps, and everyone can guess who it was.
**Supervisor (C6):** So what do I tell leadership? That we're not going to try?
**Program manager (C2):** No. I think the question is what we're trying to move. The count, or whether people stay here and move up.
**Supervisor (C6):** Those should be the same thing.
**Program manager (C2):** Maybe. I'm just not sure they are yet. If we fix how long accommodations take, the count might follow. If we push the count first, I don't know that anything else changes.

## Scene 3: What this course covers (1:55–2:45)
**Visual:** The two colleagues fade back. Four lesson titles appear one at a time, each read aloud by the narrator as it appears.
**Narrator (C3):** Neither of them is wrong to care about the number. This course is about what sits underneath it. You'll read the state's goal as official sources state it, including the executive order behind it and why its current status has to be confirmed before anyone cites it. You'll look at self-identification, and why a count built on voluntary disclosure is uncertain in predictable ways. You'll examine retention and advancement, where goals are kept or quietly lost, and accommodation as a system of routing, timing and budget. Throughout, you'll sort what a leader answers for from what belongs to Human Resources, the ADA coordinator and the Equal Opportunity and Access Division.

## Closing (2:45–3:05)
**Visual:** The first lesson title stays on screen: "What the state's goal says, and how long it lasts."
**Narrator (C3):** Keep the program manager's question with you as you go: what are we actually trying to move? The first lesson is: What the state's goal says, and how long it lasts.

## Transcript notes
Media manifest summary: A supervisor and a program manager read a disability self-identification summary and disagree about what to do next, setting up a course on Minnesota's disability employment goals, uncertain counts, retention, accommodation systems and leader accountability.
