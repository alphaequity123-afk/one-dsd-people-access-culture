# Video script: Affirmative Action Plans and Workforce Goals in Minnesota State Government

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /courses/gc-affirmative-action-workforce-goals
- **Length:** about 3 minutes (about 420 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Supervisor (C6, Co-host voice), Case manager (C5, Host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: A plan most leaders have heard of (0:00–0:30)
**Visual:** A hallway in a DSD office, then a small meeting room with a whiteboard and a stack of interview score sheets. No real logos or real people.
**Narrator (C3):** Every executive branch agency in Minnesota has an affirmative action plan. Most leaders have heard of it. Fewer have read it. The plan is a public document, approved by the state, and it touches more of the hiring process than many people realize. In this room, a supervisor who is finishing a hiring process is about to meet it in a way that matters.

## Scene 2: A moment at work (0:30–1:55)
**Visual:** The supervisor (C6) and the case manager (C5), who served on the interview panel, sit across from each other. The supervisor turns a laptop so both can see an email; its text is not readable on screen.
**Supervisor (C6):** Scores are in, and our top two are close. Then this email arrives from the affirmative action officer. It says this job category has an unmet goal.
**Case manager (C5):** What does that mean for us?
**Supervisor (C6):** Honestly, I'm not sure. Does it mean we're supposed to pick the candidate who would help the numbers?
**Case manager (C5):** I've always heard goals aren't quotas. But then why tell us now, right when we're choosing?
**Supervisor (C6):** Maybe it's just a reminder. Or maybe there's a review before I'm allowed to make an offer.
**Case manager (C5):** And if there is, what are they checking? Us, or the candidate?
**Supervisor (C6):** I'd guess the process. Whether the posting reached people, whether our questions were fair. But I don't actually know.
**Case manager (C5):** We did add that timed writing exercise this round. I never asked why we needed it.
**Supervisor (C6):** I don't want to get this wrong in either direction. I don't want to ignore the plan, and I don't want anyone hired or passed over for the wrong reason.
**Case manager (C5):** Then maybe the first question isn't which one we pick. It's what the goal is actually supposed to change.

## Scene 3: What this course covers (1:55–2:45)
**Visual:** The two colleagues fade back. Four lesson titles appear one at a time, each read aloud by the narrator as it appears.
**Narrator (C3):** This course takes up that question. You'll learn what an agency affirmative action plan is and is not under Minnesota statute and rule, and who prepares, approves and posts it. You'll separate goals from quotas, see how goals are set for groups of jobs, and look honestly at how federal law and current federal guidance limit the use of protected characteristics in an individual decision. You'll see where goals properly shape recruitment, criteria and review without deciding a hire, and how to read your unit's numbers with their limits in view. You'll also look at what a leader answers for when the numbers do not move. Questions about the plan, its goals or a specific hire go to the agency's affirmative action officer and Human Resources, and formal employment decisions stay with them.

## Closing (2:45–3:05)
**Visual:** The first lesson title stays on screen: "What an agency affirmative action plan is, and what it is not."
**Narrator (C3):** Keep the case manager's question in mind: what is the goal supposed to change? The first lesson is: What an agency affirmative action plan is, and what it is not.

## Transcript notes
Media manifest summary: A supervisor and a panel member learn that a job category has an unmet affirmative action goal just as they choose a finalist, setting up a course on what agency plans are, goals versus quotas, goals in recruitment and selection, and reading unit numbers.
