- **Voices:** a female voice (Host, `host1`) and a male voice (Co-host, `geffen`). Neither host is named on air.

## A word that ends the inquiry

**Host (female):** Three months after a new intake process launched, a manager in the leadership meeting says frontline staff are resisting it. Two other supervisors say the same thing.

**Co-host (male):** As a director, I'd hear that as a change management problem. More training, more communication.

**Host (female):** Maybe. But resistance is often information arriving in a form leaders don't like. What are staff seeing that we aren't? The research on learning organizations says culture is built by systems: how we gather information, how we review, how we decide.


## Five minutes with the director

**Co-host (male):** Then a case manager asks for five minutes and says the new form is causing families to give up partway through. And I championed that form.

**Host (female):** That's the moment that decides whether the next person comes to you. What's your first sentence?

**Co-host (male):** Honestly? Probably a defense of why we built it.

**Host (female):** Most of us would. Try, thank you for bringing this to me directly. Show me where families stop. Then the follow-up matters even more: tell them what you did with it.

**Co-host (male):** Because the whole floor will know how that conversation went by lunch.


## A review that blames no one

**Host (female):** Now a blameless review of a missed deadline. Partway through, a supervisor starts to say which case manager made the error.

**Co-host (male):** And the facilitator has to step in.

**Host (female):** Kindly and quickly. Something like, let's stay with how the process worked, and come back to individual follow-up separately. The point of the review is to find what the system made easy to get wrong.

**Co-host (male):** Which is why the facilitator shouldn't be the person whose decision is under review.


## Before the decision is final

**Host (female):** Last one. The program manager has a draft policy ready. It changes how families submit paperwork. The county partner and the counties haven't seen it.

**Co-host (male):** Sending it as final saves weeks.

**Host (female):** It might. But the people closest to families will find the problems eventually. The only question is whether they find them before launch or after. Share it as a draft, say what can still change, and set a date.

**Co-host (male):** So a learning organization isn't a slogan. It's a set of routines leaders design and protect.

**Host (female):** That's what this course is for: building those routines in your own division.
