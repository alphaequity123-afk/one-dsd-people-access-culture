## The 4 p.m. request

**Co-host (male):** Four in the afternoon. A supervisor gets a request: send updated guidance to every case manager by end of day. The guidance changes how a form is filled out, and the supervisor hasn't read it closely.

**Host (female):** First question: who set the end of day, and what happens if it goes out tomorrow morning instead?

**Co-host (male):** Sometimes the answer is, a law says so. Often it's, someone just said by end of day.

**Host (female):** And that difference matters. Research on decisions under time pressure finds people shift to shortcuts. They look at less information and lean on what's quick. Fine for low stakes. Risky for guidance a whole division will follow.


## Counting the right thing

**Host (female):** Here's another. The monthly report shows four hundred cases closed, a record. The program manager is preparing remarks.

**Co-host (male):** Celebrate it, surely. People worked hard.

**Host (female):** Celebrate the effort, yes. But ask what four hundred closures means for people. How many closed because services started, and how many because a form wasn't returned?

**Co-host (male):** Ah. A closed case isn't the same as a family that got help.

**Host (female):** Output is what we produce. Outcomes are what changes for people. Under pressure, output is easier to count, so it quietly becomes the goal.


## A pause before closing a case

**Co-host (male):** So what do you actually do in the moment? End of day, a case manager is about to close a case because a form never came back.

**Host (female):** A short pause routine. Three questions: who does this affect, what would I need to know to be confident, and is anything about this situation unusual?

**Co-host (male):** And in this case a colleague mentions the household's preferred language isn't English, and the reminder went out in English only.

**Host (female):** Then the pause just did its job. The right next step is a reminder they can read, not a closure.


## When the date really is fixed

**Co-host (male):** But some deadlines can't move. A new eligibility process launches on a date set by law, and notices in Somali and Hmong won't be ready for two more weeks.

**Host (female):** Then you protect quality another way. A quick premortem helps: imagine it's a month after launch and it went badly for families who read Somali or Hmong. What happened?

**Co-host (male):** They never heard about the change. Or they heard and didn't know what to do.

**Host (female):** So you plan for that now: community partners, interpreters on the phone line, extra time to respond. The date holds. The harm doesn't have to.

**Co-host (male):** That's the whole course: tell a true deadline from a habit, and protect the decisions that matter most.
