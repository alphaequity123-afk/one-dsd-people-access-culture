- **Length:** about 2.5 minutes (380 spoken words)

## The default assignment

**Co-host (male):** A team lost two positions last year, and the work stayed the same. Two people are on leave. Then a difficult new case comes in, the kind one case manager always handles well.

**Host (female):** And that case manager is already covering more than anyone. So it goes to them. Again.

**Co-host (male):** It's efficient, though.

**Host (female):** Today. The burnout research points to six areas of work life that matter: workload, control, reward, community, fairness and values. Giving the hard case to the same person every time touches at least three of them.


## I'm fine, it's a busy season

**Co-host (male):** So the supervisor raises it in a one-on-one, and the case manager says, I'm fine, it's just a busy season.

**Host (female):** But the supervisor has seen late-night emails for most of the month. The mistake would be to argue about whether they're fine.

**Co-host (male):** Because they'll just dig in.

**Host (female):** Right. Name what you've noticed, without judgment, and make it about the work. I've seen a lot of late emails this month. That tells me the load isn't working. What's one thing I could take off or move?

**Co-host (male):** So the supervisor owns the problem, instead of asking the person to admit they're struggling.

**Host (female):** Exactly. Workload is a design problem. The supervisor has the levers.


## Sharing the load without surveillance

**Co-host (male):** Here's where I get nervous. A supervisor proposes a shared weekly view of caseloads. As a newer person, I'd worry it'll be used to judge whoever is slower.

**Host (female):** That worry is the whole design question. Say clearly what the view is for: spotting when one person is overloaded and moving work. Not ranking anyone. Agree it as a team, and review it after a month.

**Co-host (male):** And notice who doesn't speak. If someone with years on the team stays silent, that's worth a private follow-up.


## Taking it up the chain

**Host (female):** Last piece. The program manager asks the supervisor how the team is doing. Caseloads up thirty percent, two people on leave, two reports late.

**Co-host (male):** The instinct is to say, we're managing.

**Host (female):** And then nothing changes above you. Give the facts plainly, say what's at risk, and bring a choice: here's what we can keep on time, here's what will slip, which would you protect?

**Co-host (male):** That's not complaining. That's giving your manager something to decide.

**Host (female):** And it protects your team. That's what this course practices: seeing the drivers, holding the conversation, and changing how the work is shared.
