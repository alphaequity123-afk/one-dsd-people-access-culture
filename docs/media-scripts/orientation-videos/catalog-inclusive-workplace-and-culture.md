## Scene 1: What this pathway is for (0:00–0:27)
**Visual:** A DSD workspace through a workday: a job posting, an interview panel, a one-on-one check-in.
**Narrator (C3):** Whether people feel they belong at work often comes down to ordinary moments: who hears about an opportunity, how an interview is run, what happens after someone raises a concern. The Inclusive Workplace and Culture pathway looks at those moments across the employee life cycle, from hiring and onboarding to feedback, conflict, and staying well while doing demanding work.

## Scene 2: A moment at work (0:27–0:50)
**Visual:** A supervisor and a newer colleague talk in a small meeting room.
**Newer colleague (C7):** I didn't know the workgroup was looking for help until it was already staffed.
**Supervisor (C6):** I mentioned it to the people who happened to be in the office that day. That's on me, not you. Next time it goes to the whole team, with the same deadline for everyone.

## Scene 3: How the pathway is organized (0:50–1:26)
**Visual:** On-screen text, read aloud: "29 courses. 7 Foundations, 18 Intermediate, 4 Advanced."
**Narrator (C3):** The pathway has 29 courses: 7 at the Foundations level, 18 at Intermediate, and 4 at Advanced. Most are written for supervisors and leaders, with courses on affirmative action in state employment, Connect 700, reducing bias in hiring, structured interviews, inclusive onboarding, fair performance reviews, stretch assignments, mentoring and sponsorship, and psychological safety. Staff courses cover the Respectful Workplace Policy, knowing your rights about discrimination, harassment and retaliation, requesting a reasonable accommodation, navigating conflict, and talking about race at work.

## Scene 4: Choosing a starting point (1:26–2:01)
**Visual:** Course cards for "The Respectful Workplace Policy for staff" and "Equitable access to stretch assignments and promotion."
**Narrator (C3):** Staff can begin with "The Respectful Workplace Policy for staff." Supervisors can begin with "Leaders' duties under the Respectful Workplace Policy," and the moment you just saw connects with "Equitable access to stretch assignments and promotion." The Advanced courses take up retention, restorative approaches to workplace harm, and facilitating difficult conversations about race and equity. Reports, investigations and accommodation decisions go through Human Resources or the DHS Equal Opportunity and Access Division, not through a course.

## Closing: How the courses work (2:01–2:22)
**Visual:** A lesson page showing a practice case and a reflection prompt.
**Narrator (C3):** Each course has six lessons with DSD practice cases, scenario choices with feedback, knowledge checks, private reflection and a job aid. Learning is voluntary and not scored, and your progress stays in your own browser. Start with the part of the work you touch most often.
