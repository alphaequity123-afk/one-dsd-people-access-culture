## Scene 1: What this pathway is for (0:00–0:28)
**Visual:** Staff arriving for a team meeting, some in person and some joining on screen.
**{{NARRATOR}}:** Shared Foundations is where the catalog builds a common starting point. Its courses explain what People, Access and Culture means at DSD, what the DHS Equity Policy asks of staff, and the plain-language terms we use together. They also turn to everyday respect: checking assumptions before they become decisions, and noticing how small moments shape whether people feel they belong.

## Scene 3: How the pathway is organized (0:52–1:34)
**Visual:** On-screen text, read aloud: "21 courses. 12 Foundations, 9 Intermediate."
**{{NARRATOR}}:** The pathway has 21 courses: 12 at the Foundations level and 9 at Intermediate. All of them are written for staff in any role. Foundations courses include the DHS Equity Policy for staff, equity versus equality, protected classes under the Minnesota Human Rights Act, Executive Order 23-12 for staff, recognizing microaggressions, inclusive language, and pronouns and gender inclusion. Intermediate courses include how systems create unequal outcomes, your role in equity whatever your job, responding to microaggressions as a bystander, apologizing well when you have caused harm, and where to get help with equity questions.

## Scene 4: Choosing a starting point (1:34–2:06)
**Visual:** Course cards for "People, Access and Culture at DSD: what it means and why it matters" and "Responding to microaggressions as a bystander."
**{{NARRATOR}}:** If you are new to DSD or to this work, begin with "People, Access and Culture at DSD: what it means and why it matters," or "Key People, Access and Culture terms in plain language." If the moment you just saw feels familiar, "Responding to microaggressions as a bystander" picks it up. This pathway has no Advanced level; when you are ready to go further, the other pathways build on it.

## Closing: How the courses work (2:06–2:35)
**Visual:** A lesson page showing a key terms card and a reflection prompt.
**{{NARRATOR}}:** Each course has six lessons with DSD practice cases, scenario choices with feedback, knowledge checks, private reflection and a job aid. Learning is voluntary and not scored, and your progress stays in your own browser. Questions about a formal complaint or a civil-rights concern go to the responsible office, and "Where to get help with equity questions" explains how to find it.
