# Video script: Team Building and Cross-Collaboration pathway orientation

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /courses/dc-t1-what-makes-a-dsd-team-inclusive, /courses/dc-t71-how-dsd-is-organized-and-how-the-work-connects
- **Length:** about 2.5 minutes (2:29; about 324 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Program manager (C2, Host voice), Supervisor (C6, Co-host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: What this pathway is for (0:00–0:27)
**Visual:** A hybrid team meeting: some colleagues in a conference room, others on a large screen.
**Narrator (C3):** Most of what DSD does depends on people working well together, within a team, across units, and with counties, providers and community partners. The Team Building and Cross-Collaboration pathway is about the everyday habits that make that possible: trust, clear roles, meetings where everyone can contribute, honest feedback, and handoffs that do not leave work stranded between groups.

## Scene 2: A moment at work (0:27–0:50)
**Visual:** A program manager and a supervisor meet in a hallway, each holding a printed draft.
**Program manager (C2):** Our unit sent the draft over two weeks ago. We took the silence to mean it was fine.
**Supervisor (C6):** My team thought it was for information only. Nobody told us a decision was waiting on us.
**Program manager (C2):** So we each did our part, and the handoff is where it fell apart.

## Scene 3: How the pathway is organized (0:50–1:27)
**Visual:** On-screen text, read aloud: "99 courses. 32 Foundations, 53 Intermediate, 14 Advanced."
**Narrator (C3):** The pathway has 99 courses: 32 at the Foundations level, 53 at Intermediate, and 14 at Advanced. They are grouped into ten topic areas, including inclusive team culture; trust, belonging and psychological safety; team roles and ways of working; communication across difference; inclusive meetings and decisions; hybrid teams; conflict, feedback and repair; collaboration across DSD and ADSA; and collaboration with agencies, counties and communities. Most courses are written for everyone, and the Advanced courses are mainly for leaders who shape cross-unit work.

## Scene 4: Choosing a starting point (1:27–2:02)
**Visual:** Course cards for "How DSD is organized and how the work connects," "Handoffs that work" and "Clarifying roles and responsibilities."
**Narrator (C3):** If you are new to DSD, "How DSD is organized and how the work connects" gives you the map. If the moment you just saw sounds familiar, "Handoffs that work" and "Clarifying roles and responsibilities" speak to it directly. Supervisors and team leads may want "Creating psychological safety as a leader" or "Writing a team agreement." One planned course on collaborating with Tribal Nations is not offered here; that learning is referred to the appropriate office within ADSA.

## Closing: How the courses work (2:02–2:29)
**Visual:** A lesson page with a team practice case and a sorting activity.
**Narrator (C3):** Each course has six lessons with DSD practice cases, scenario choices with feedback, knowledge checks, sorting activities, private reflection and a job aid you can bring to your next team meeting. Learning is voluntary and not scored, and your progress stays in your own browser. Pick one habit your team could try this month and start there.
