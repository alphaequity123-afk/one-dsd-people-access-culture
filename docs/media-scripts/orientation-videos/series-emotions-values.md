# Video script: Getting started with Emotions, Values, and Everyday Practice

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /learn/emotions-and-values (the series page, linked from /courses as "Explore the four-course series")
- **Length:** about 2.5 minutes (about 360 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Case manager (C5, Host voice), Supervisor (C6, Co-host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: What the series is for (0:00–0:26)
**Visual:** A shared calendar on a screen with a deadline moved twice, then two colleagues talking across a table. Title card reads "Emotions, Values, and Everyday Practice." No real logos or real people.
**Narrator (C3):** This is Emotions, Values, and Everyday Practice, a series of four courses for directors, managers, supervisors and staff across One DHS and One DSD. Two courses look at emotional intelligence, and two look at cultural values. Together they help you understand your own responses, consider other perspectives, and put that understanding to work in conversations and decisions.

## Scene 2: A moment at work (0:26–0:54)
**Visual:** A case manager and a supervisor at a desk, the moved deadline visible on the screen between them.
**Case manager (C5):** The deadline moved again. Honestly, my first thought was that the coordinator doesn't care whether we finish.
**Supervisor (C6):** That's a real frustration. What do we actually know about why it moved?
**Case manager (C5):** Only that it changed twice. I don't know the reason.
**Supervisor (C6):** Then let's ask about the schedule and plan from what we know. We can solve the timing without deciding what anyone intended.

## Scene 3: How the series is organized (0:54–1:39)
**Visual:** Four course cards in two rows, labeled "Foundation" and "Intermediate." Each label and course title is read aloud.
**Narrator (C3):** Two courses are at the Foundation level. Emotional Intelligence: Understanding Emotions and Choosing a Response treats an emotion as information to examine alongside the facts. Cultural Values: Understanding Your Own Lens explores ten dimensions of cultural values and the assumptions built into your own lens, and shows how to ask people about their individual preferences instead of predicting them. Two courses are at the Intermediate level. Emotional Intelligence in Conversations, Teams, and Leadership practices listening, feedback, repair and accountable decisions. Cultural Values in Decisions, Services, and Team Practice turns that understanding into clearer choices and services people can more easily use.

## Scene 4: Choosing a starting point (1:39–2:21)
**Visual:** A course page showing a two-person audio conversation with a transcript, a decision with feedback, and a reusable guide.
**Narrator (C3):** Begin with emotional awareness or with your own cultural-value lens, then move into conversations and decisions. Each course stands on its own. Each has seven lessons and takes about 75 to 90 minutes including practice, or you can return for one lesson at a time. You can listen to a two-person conversation or read it, then work through worked examples, decisions with feedback, knowledge checks, reflection, a final practice challenge and a guide to take into your work. You choose what to reflect on and share, and your progress is saved in your browser.

## Closing (2:21–2:42)
**Visual:** The series page heading, read aloud: "Emotions, Values, and Everyday Practice."
**Narrator (C3):** This learning is kept separate from personnel ratings and from any judgment about disability, and no one needs to disclose a diagnosis or a painful experience to take part. It does not replace required agency training or formal advice. Choose a situation you recognize, and start there.
