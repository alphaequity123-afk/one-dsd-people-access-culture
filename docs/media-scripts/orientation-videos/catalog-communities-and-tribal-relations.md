## Scene 1: What this pathway is for (0:00–0:29)
**Visual:** A community meeting room set up with chairs in a circle, a sign-in table and an interpreter's chair near the front.
**{{NARRATOR}}:** The people DSD serves live in communities with their own histories, languages and ways of building trust. The Community engagement pathway helps staff and leaders work respectfully with Minnesota's communities and involve people with lived experience in the decisions that affect them. Its community courses offer context for preparing good questions. They do not predict what any one person believes or needs.

## Scene 2: A moment at work (0:29–0:54)

**{{PROGRAM_MANAGER}}:** That's fair. What times and places would make it possible, and what would make it worth their time?
**{{COMMUNITY_PARTNER}}:** Start with that question next time, before the date is set.

## Scene 3: How the pathway is organized (0:54–1:28)
**Visual:** On-screen text, read aloud: "18 courses. 10 Foundations, 7 Intermediate, 1 Advanced."
**{{NARRATOR}}:** The pathway has 18 courses: 10 at the Foundations level, 7 at Intermediate, and 1 at Advanced. The staff courses begin with cultural humility in public service and include courses on working respectfully with Somali, Hmong, Latino, Karen and other Burmese, Oromo and Ethiopian, African American, and Vietnamese, Russian and Ukrainian-speaking communities. Leadership courses cover meaningful engagement, advisory councils, fair compensation for community partners, accessible public meetings and, at the Advanced level, repairing trust after harm.

## Scene 4: Tribal Nations and where to go (1:28–1:59)
**Visual:** Plain text on screen, read aloud: "Native American and Tribal-specific learning: contact the appropriate office within ADSA."
**{{NARRATOR}}:** By the program owner's direction, this catalog does not offer Native American or Tribal-specific courses. For that learning, and for questions or engagement, contact the appropriate office within ADSA, the Aging and Disability Services Administration, or the responsible Tribal liaison. No course here replaces government-to-government consultation with Tribal Nations. When work touches a Tribal Nation, consultation comes first and goes through the DHS Tribal consultation process.

## Closing: Choosing a starting point (1:59–2:22)
**Visual:** Course cards for "Cultural humility in public service" and "Meaningful community engagement for leaders."
**{{NARRATOR}}:** Staff can start with "Cultural humility in public service," and leaders with "Meaningful community engagement for leaders." Each course has six lessons with practice cases, scenario choices with feedback, knowledge checks, private reflection and a job aid. Learning is voluntary and not scored, and your progress stays in your own browser.
