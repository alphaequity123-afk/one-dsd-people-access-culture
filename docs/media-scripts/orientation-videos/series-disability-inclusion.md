# Video script: Getting started with the Disability Inclusion series

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /courses/di-disability-diversity-belonging (first course in the series), /learn/collections/disability-access (the staff collection that opens with the series' Foundations courses)
- **Length:** about 3 minutes (about 380 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Supervisor (C6, Co-host voice), Newer colleague (C7, Co-host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: What the series is for (0:00–0:29)
**Visual:** An ordinary state office: a meeting room being set up, a form on a screen, a hallway with a stair and an elevator. Title card reads "Disability Inclusion." No real logos or real people.
**Narrator (C3):** This is the Disability Inclusion series. Disabled people are already part of this agency as colleagues, applicants, partners and the households we serve, and many disabilities are not visible. The series looks at how ordinary work, such as booking a room, saving a document or writing a policy, can create barriers, and at the practical steps that remove them before anyone has to ask.

## Scene 2: A moment at work (0:29–1:00)
**Visual:** Two colleagues at a desk, looking at a draft meeting invitation on a laptop screen.
**Newer colleague (C7):** I'm sending the invitation for the provider listening session. Should I add something about access, or wait and see if anyone asks?
**Supervisor (C6):** Add it now. Tell people how to request what they need and who to contact, and plan captions and accessible materials from the start.
**Newer colleague (C7):** So we don't make people do the extra work of asking first.
**Supervisor (C6):** Right. Then anything individual, we work out with the person.

## Scene 3: How the series is organized (1:00–1:36)
**Visual:** Five labeled bands appear one at a time: "Foundations," "Applied inclusion," "Practitioner," "Strategic leadership," "Capstone." Each label is read aloud as it appears.
**Narrator (C3):** The series has 25 courses in five groups. Foundations, five courses written for everyone, builds the shared language the rest of the series depends on. Applied inclusion, seven courses, works through access needs, communication, accommodation, de-escalation, inclusive meetings and conflict with neurodivergent colleagues. Practitioner, eight courses, is for people who audit, design, measure or lead change. Strategic leadership, four courses, is for senior leaders who govern and fund the work. The capstone guides a cross-functional team through removing one real barrier.

## Scene 4: Choosing a starting point (1:36–2:25)
**Visual:** A course page showing a lesson list, a scenario with three response choices, a knowledge check with feedback, and a one-page job aid.
**Narrator (C3):** If the subject is new to you, Disability, Diversity and Belonging is a good first course. If a question is already in front of you, such as an inaccessible form, go straight to the course that fits it. The group names describe the depth of the material; they do not label you or limit where you begin. Most courses take about 45 to 55 minutes across four or five short lessons, with scenarios, practice activities with feedback, knowledge checks and a job aid. Keep any reflection notes in your own document, without naming coworkers or the people you serve. Your choices and progress are saved in your browser, and nothing is graded.

## Closing (2:25–2:50)
**Visual:** The first course card, with its title read aloud: "Disability, Diversity and Belonging."
**Narrator (C3):** The series is voluntary and does not replace required training. Decisions about a specific accommodation or civil-rights concern stay with the responsible office, such as your ADA coordinator, Human Resources or the DHS Equal Opportunity and Access Division. When you are ready, open the first course, or the one closest to your work.
