# Video script: Getting started with the Program Integrity courses

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /courses/pi-what-fraud-enforcement-costs (first course in the series; the next two courses build on it)
- **Length:** about 3 minutes (about 370 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Supervisor (C6, Co-host voice), Program manager (C2, Host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: What the series is for (0:00–0:32)
**Visual:** A review team's workspace: a stack of case files, a spreadsheet on a screen, a draft public summary. Title card reads "Program Integrity." No real logos or real people.
**Narrator (C3):** This is the Program Integrity series. Medicaid fraud is real, it takes resources from people who need them, and enforcement against it is necessary. These courses hold a second truth beside that one: enforcement that moves fast and broad can also disrupt services for people who did nothing wrong, and public talk about fraud can slide into suspicion of a whole community. The series helps DHS staff keep both in view.

## Scene 2: A moment at work (0:32–1:00)
**Visual:** Two colleagues reviewing a draft public summary on a shared screen.
**Supervisor (C6):** Before this goes out, read the second paragraph with me. It names the providers who were charged, and then it describes the community they come from.
**Program manager (C2):** I put that in as background.
**Supervisor (C6):** I understand why. But a reader could take it to mean the whole community is under suspicion. The facts are about specific providers, so the summary should be too.

## Scene 3: How the series is organized (1:00–1:50)
**Visual:** Four course titles appear in order, each read aloud as it appears.
**Narrator (C3):** The series has four courses. What Fraud Enforcement Actually Costs is for all DHS staff and looks at what careful and careless enforcement each cost. Public Perception, Misperception, and the Communities We Serve builds on it, showing how a story about individual wrongdoing can grow into a story about a community. Protecting the Program Without Becoming the Harm builds on both, with practical habits for communication, for noticing bias risk in how scrutiny is applied, and for using the Equity Analysis Toolkit. The fourth course, Equitable Oversight Design for Program Integrity Leaders, is for leaders and staff who design reviews, and covers proportionate reviews, due process, continuity of care and responsible use of data.

## Scene 4: Choosing a starting point (1:50–2:25)
**Visual:** A course page showing a scenario, flashcards and a knowledge check with feedback.
**Narrator (C3):** Most people should take the first three courses in order, because each one builds on the last. If you lead or design oversight work, continue to the fourth. Supervisors will also find a related course, Fair Oversight in Program Integrity, among the courses for supervisors and managers. Each course runs about 40 to 52 minutes across four lessons, with scenarios, practice activities and knowledge checks with explanations. Your progress is saved in your browser, and nothing is graded.

## Closing (2:25–2:47)
**Visual:** The first course card, with its title read aloud: "What Fraud Enforcement Actually Costs."
**Narrator (C3):** The series is voluntary. It does not replace any required agency training on fraud, waste and abuse, and decisions in an individual case stay with the responsible office. Start with the first course and the question it asks: what would it take to describe both kinds of harm accurately?
