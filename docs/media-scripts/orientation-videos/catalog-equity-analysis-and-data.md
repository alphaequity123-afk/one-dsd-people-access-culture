## Scene 1: What this pathway is for (0:00–0:28)
**Visual:** An invented bar chart with no numbers or labels: one tall statewide bar splits into several bars of different heights, one for each region.
**{{NARRATOR}}:** Policies, budgets and programs affect people differently, and numbers can either reveal those differences or hide them. The Equity Analysis and Data pathway helps staff and leaders ask better questions of data, pair numbers with what people describe, and think through who a decision affects before it is final. The courses are preparation. Actual decisions use the applicable agency equity-analysis process.

## Scene 2: A moment at work (0:28–0:52)
**Visual:** A program manager and a case manager look at a dashboard on a wall screen.
**{{PROGRAM_MANAGER}}:** The statewide average time to start services looks fine. I was ready to call it good.
**{{CASE_MANAGER}}:** In the counties I work with, families describe something different. Can we look at it by region and by language before we decide?
**{{PROGRAM_MANAGER}}:** Yes. And let's write down what the numbers can't tell us.

## Scene 3: How the pathway is organized (0:52–1:31)
**Visual:** On-screen text, read aloud: "26 courses. 3 Foundations, 11 Intermediate, 12 Advanced."
**{{NARRATOR}}:** The pathway has 26 courses: 3 at the Foundations level, 11 at Intermediate, and 12 at Advanced, so much of it is for people who design, analyze or decide. Data, disparities and evaluation courses cover disaggregated data, responsible use of demographic data, measures and targets, program evaluation and dashboards. Equity analysis courses apply the same thinking to policy changes, waiver and HCBS program design, rate setting and budgets, grants and contracting, legislative proposals and fiscal notes. A third group brings an equity lens into daily DSD work.

## Scene 4: Choosing a starting point (1:31–2:03)
**Visual:** Course cards for "Disaggregated data: why averages hide inequity" and "Using an equity lens in your work."
**{{NARRATOR}}:** The three Foundations courses are written for leaders, but anyone can take them; "Disaggregated data: why averages hide inequity" speaks directly to the moment you just saw. Staff in any role can start with "Using an equity lens in your work" or "Hearing community voices." Analysts may go on to "Equity in data analysis," and leaders who write for decision-makers may want "Writing equity impact statements leaders can stand behind."

## Closing: How the courses work (2:03–2:22)
**Visual:** A lesson page with a practice data table and a knowledge check.
**{{NARRATOR}}:** Each course has six lessons with DSD practice cases, scenario choices with feedback, knowledge checks, private reflection and a job aid. The examples are invented for practice. Learning is voluntary and not scored, and your progress stays in your own browser.
