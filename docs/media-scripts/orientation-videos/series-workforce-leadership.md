## Scene 1: What these courses are for (0:00–0:28)
**Visual:** A hybrid meeting: several people around a table and a screen showing colleagues who have joined online. Title cards read "Workforce and culture" and "Leadership practice." No real logos or real people.
**{{NARRATOR}}:** These are the workforce and leadership courses. They are about how DHS and DSD work together as a workplace: whether people are treated with respect, whose careers move forward, how hybrid teams share work, and how anyone, with or without a title, can lead well. They are written for all employees, and some are aimed at supervisors, managers and senior leaders.

## Scene 3: How the courses are organized (0:56–1:38)
**Visual:** Two columns appear. The first is headed "Workforce and culture," the second "Leadership practice," with level names underneath. Each heading and level name is read aloud.
**{{NARRATOR}}:** There are 20 courses in two groups. Workforce and culture has seven. At the Foundation level they cover a respectful workplace, age inclusion, and secondary trauma and staff well-being; at the Intermediate level, career pathways, employee resource groups, accommodation across transitions, and equity in remote and hybrid work. Leadership practice has thirteen: two Foundation courses on purpose and accountability, three Supervisor foundations courses on inclusive leadership, delegation and coaching, five Intermediate courses including leading without formal authority and systems thinking, and three Advanced courses on setting direction, leading culture across a division, and foresight.

## Scene 4: Choosing a starting point (1:38–2:16)
**Visual:** A course page showing a practice case, a decision with feedback, a knowledge check, flashcards and a job aid.
**{{NARRATOR}}:** Start with the question you are facing, not with the level. Levels describe the depth of the material, not the standing of the person taking it. Each course has four lessons, about 48 minutes plus optional practice, and the lessons can be opened in any order. You will find explanations, practice cases with feedback, knowledge checks, flashcards, private reflection and a practical job aid. Keep reflection notes without identifying coworkers or members of the public. Your progress is saved in your browser, and nothing is graded.

## Closing (2:16–2:35)
**Visual:** The two first course cards, with their titles read aloud: "Respectful Workplace: Harassment, Bystander Action and Psychological Safety" and "Leading With Purpose and Values."
**{{NARRATOR}}:** Participation is voluntary. The courses support learning, and formal employment, accommodation and civil-rights decisions stay with the responsible office, such as Human Resources or the DHS Equal Opportunity and Access Division. Pick one situation from your own week and begin there.
