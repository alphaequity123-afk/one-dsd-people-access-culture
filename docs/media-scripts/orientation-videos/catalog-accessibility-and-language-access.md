# Video script: Accessibility and Language Access pathway orientation

## Scene 1: What this pathway is for (0:00–0:24)
**Visual:** A notice on a laptop screen. A screen reader's focus outline moves across it; beside it, a colleague on a call with an interpreter.
**Narrator (C3):** Information only helps people if they can use it. The Accessibility and Language Access pathway covers the practical skills that make DSD communication usable: plain language, accessible documents and meetings, working with interpreters and translations, and the leadership and accommodation responsibilities that keep access from depending on one person's extra effort.

## Scene 2: A moment at work (0:24–0:51)
**Visual:** A newer colleague turns a laptop toward a program manager.
**Newer colleague (C7):** The provider update went out as a scanned PDF. Someone using a screen reader just wrote back that it reads as a blank page.
**Program manager (C2):** And we haven't arranged translations yet. So for some of the people it was meant for, the update hasn't really gone out.
**Newer colleague (C7):** Then let's fix the source document before the next one, not after.

## Scene 3: How the pathway is organized (0:51–1:30)
**Visual:** On-screen text, read aloud: "31 courses. 12 Foundations, 16 Intermediate, 3 Advanced."
**Narrator (C3):** The pathway has 31 courses: 12 at the Foundations level, 16 at Intermediate, and 3 at Advanced. Digital and document accessibility courses walk through Word, PowerPoint, Excel, PDFs, alt text, color and contrast, Teams meetings, video and audio, and accessibility checkers. Language access and plain language courses cover language access rights and duties, interpreters, requesting translations at DHS, writing for translation, and Easy Read formats. Leadership courses address accessible organizations, ADA Title II, Minnesota's accessibility standards for state agencies, and the supervisor's role in reasonable accommodation.

## Scene 4: Choosing a starting point (1:30–1:59)
**Visual:** Course cards for "Why digital accessibility matters," "Plain language basics" and "Working with interpreters."
**Narrator (C3):** If you write or share documents, start with "Why digital accessibility matters" or "Plain language basics," then pick the course for the tool you use most. If you talk with people who use another language, "Language access rights and duties" and "Working with interpreters" are good first steps. Leaders can start with "Leading an accessible organization." Accommodation decisions themselves stay with the responsible office.

## Closing: How the courses work (1:59–2:23)
**Visual:** A lesson page with a sorting activity and a downloadable job aid.
**Narrator (C3):** Each course has six lessons with DSD practice cases, scenario choices with feedback, knowledge checks, private reflection and a job aid you can keep beside your work. Learning is voluntary and not scored, and your progress stays in your own browser. Choose the course for the next thing you plan to send.
