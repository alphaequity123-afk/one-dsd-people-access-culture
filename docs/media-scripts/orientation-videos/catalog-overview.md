## Scene 1: What the catalog is for (0:00–0:26)
**Visual:** An open-plan DSD office and a home workspace side by side. Colleagues read, write and talk on a video call. Title card: "People, Access and Culture catalog."
**{{NARRATOR}}:** The People, Access and Culture catalog is a library of short courses for everyone in the Disability Services Division. It covers how we communicate, decide and work together, and how people experience the services we provide. There are more than 380 courses, and each one has six lessons that most people finish in about an hour.

## Scene 2: Three levels (0:26–0:53)
**Visual:** Three labeled steps appear one at a time: "Foundations," "Intermediate," "Advanced."
**{{NARRATOR}}:** Courses come at three levels. Foundations courses build shared language, awareness and basic practices, and nothing needs to come first. Intermediate courses apply those ideas to real DSD situations through practice, tools and scenarios. Advanced courses are for leading, designing, coaching or changing systems and practices. A level describes the depth of a course, not the person taking it.

## Scene 3: Nine pathways (0:53–1:18)
**Visual:** Nine pathway names appear as a list while the narrator reads each one.
**{{NARRATOR}}:** The courses are also grouped into nine learning pathways: Shared Foundations; Intercultural Learning; Team Building and Cross-Collaboration; Community engagement; Disability Rights and Person-Centered Practice; Accessibility and Language Access; Inclusive Workplace and Culture; Equity Analysis and Data; and Equity Leadership. Some courses are written for leaders, some for staff, and many for everyone.

## Scene 4: Choosing where to start (1:18–1:36)
**Visual:** Two colleagues talk at a shared desk with a coffee mug and a printed letter between them.
**{{SUPERVISOR}}:** There are hundreds of these. Where did you even start?
**{{NEW_COLLEAGUE}}:** With whatever was closest to my work that week. I picked the one on explaining acronyms and program terms, and I tried it on a letter that same afternoon.

## Closing: How the courses work (1:36–2:10)
**Visual:** A course page with its six lessons listed, then the Courses page search box and level filter.
**{{NARRATOR}}:** Each course uses DSD practice cases, scenario decisions with feedback, knowledge checks and private reflection, and ends with a job aid you can keep. Learning is voluntary, and lessons open in any order. Scenario choices are not scored, and your progress is saved in your own browser. Formal decisions stay with the responsible office. To begin, open Courses, search for a subject or choose a level, and start with something you can use this week.
