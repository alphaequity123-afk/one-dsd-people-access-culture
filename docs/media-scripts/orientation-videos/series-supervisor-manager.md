## Scene 1: What the series is for (0:00–0:28)
**Visual:** A supervisor's desk with a team schedule, a list of assignments and a calendar. Title card reads "For supervisors and managers." No real logos or real people.
**{{NARRATOR}}:** This series is for supervisors, leads and managers at DHS and DSD. Its 19 courses deal with the decisions that come with supervising people: who gets which assignment, how a rating is written, what happens after a complaint, how leave and return to work are handled, and how equity becomes part of a supervisor's ordinary work rather than an extra project.

**{{SUPERVISOR}}:** They're fast, and I knew they'd deliver. But you're right that it shapes who gets seen at review time.
**{{CASE_MANAGER}}:** Maybe we could talk about how those get assigned before the next one comes in.
**{{SUPERVISOR}}:** Let's do that. I'd rather decide how before I decide who.

## Scene 3: How the series is organized (0:58–1:44)
**Visual:** Three groups of course titles appear in turn under the headings "Starting out," "Everyday decisions," and "Leading supervisors." Each heading is read aloud.
**{{NARRATOR}}:** The courses fall into three broad groups. Starting out includes your first week as a supervisor, why equity is a supervisor's work, and what a supervisor should know about the rules as of fall 2026. Everyday decisions covers assignments and visibility, ratings, corrective action and labor agreements, hiring, leave, budget-driven change, supervising with Deaf, DeafBlind and hard of hearing colleagues, being named in a complaint, and your own load. For managers of supervisors and senior leaders, courses cover backing your supervisors, consistency across ratings and pay, reading patterns, fair oversight and senior leader decision sessions. A facilitator guide supports an optional live practice session.

## Scene 4: Choosing a starting point (1:44–2:25)
**Visual:** The leading area's "Suggested paths" section, with path names read aloud: "First-year supervisors," "Experienced supervisors," "Managers of managers," "Senior leaders."
**{{NARRATOR}}:** If you are new to supervising, start with Your First Week as a Supervisor. Otherwise, start from the decision in front of you, or follow a suggested path in the leaders' area. Each course takes about 50 to 80 minutes across four to six lessons, each with an explanation, a practice situation, a knowledge check and a plan to try, plus a one-page job aid. Your progress stays in your browser and nothing is sent to your supervisor or employer. The leaders' area also shows you no information about your team's learning.

## Closing (2:25–2:44)
**Visual:** The first course card, with its title read aloud: "Your First Week as a Supervisor: What You Now Answer For."
**{{NARRATOR}}:** The courses are voluntary and do not replace the supervisor training the state requires. Where they mention law or contracts, they offer general information, not legal advice. For a real situation, ask Human Resources, labor relations or your ADA coordinator.
