## Scene 1: What this pathway is for (0:00–0:31)
**Visual:** A planning meeting in a small DSD conference room. A case manager reviews notes afterward, looking thoughtful.
**{{NARRATOR}}:** Every conversation in our work carries culture, including our own workplace and professional cultures. The Intercultural Learning pathway helps you notice how culture shapes meaning, consider another perspective, and adapt how you work, without assuming that a description of a group tells you who a person is. It is the largest pathway in the catalog, and most of its courses are written for staff in any role.

## Scene 3: How the pathway is organized (0:55–1:34)
**Visual:** On-screen text, read aloud: "111 courses. 36 Foundations, 57 Intermediate, 18 Advanced."
**{{NARRATOR}}:** The pathway has 111 courses: 36 at the Foundations level, 57 at Intermediate, and 18 at Advanced. Foundations courses explore what culture is, your own cultural identity, cultural humility, cultural intelligence and the Intercultural Development Continuum. Intermediate courses go deeper into communication, conflict, identity and language, cultural views of disability and care, and Minnesota's communities. Advanced courses apply these skills to policy, program design, outreach, data, coaching and facilitation. The continuum courses describe ideas about development; they never assign you an orientation or measure your growth.

## Scene 4: Choosing a starting point (1:34–2:10)
**Visual:** A course card titled "What is culture?" and a second card titled "Saying no and disagreeing across cultures."
**{{NARRATOR}}:** If you are new to this subject, "What is culture?" and "Your own cultural identity" are good places to begin. If a situation like the one you just saw sounds familiar, you could go straight to "Saying no and disagreeing across cultures." If you have an IDI development plan, you may use it to choose a focus, and the same courses are available if you have not taken the IDI. Leaders can start with "Intercultural development for leaders."

## Closing: How the courses work (2:10–2:33)
**Visual:** A lesson page showing a practice case, a knowledge check and a reflection prompt.
**{{NARRATOR}}:** Each course has six lessons with DSD practice cases, scenario choices with feedback, knowledge checks, private reflection and a job aid. Learning is voluntary, choices are not scored, and your progress stays in your own browser. Start with one course that fits a conversation you are about to have.
