# Video script: Getting started with Intercultural Practice and Equity

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /courses/ipe-01-intercultural-practice (first course in the series), /courses/ipe-08-choosing-a-learning-focus (the course written to help learners choose where to start in this curriculum)
- **Length:** about 3 minutes (about 380 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Case manager (C5, Host voice), Program manager (C2, Host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: What the series is for (0:00–0:28)
**Visual:** A printed letter on a desk, a web form on a screen, and a slide in a public meeting room. Title card reads "Intercultural Practice and Equity." No real logos or real people.
**Narrator (C3):** This is the Intercultural Practice and Equity series. It looks at the public-administration side of disability services: the policies, letters, forms, data, contracts and meetings through which a state division reaches people. Culture, identity, disability and power travel through those ordinary decisions. The series is for DHS and DSD staff whose work shapes them, and it is not direct-service or provider training.

## Scene 2: A moment at work (0:28–0:58)
**Visual:** Two colleagues in a small meeting room with a printed letter between them.
**Case manager (C5):** Three families called about the same letter this week. Each of them thought the deadline meant their services would stop.
**Program manager (C2):** Does the letter say that, or is that how it reads to someone who has never been through this before?
**Case manager (C5):** It doesn't say it. But nothing in it says what happens next, either.
**Program manager (C2):** Then the letter is what we need to look at, not the families.

## Scene 3: How the series is organized (0:58–1:47)
**Visual:** Five labeled bands appear one at a time: "Foundations," "Public-service practice," "Complex systems," "Internal leadership," "Capstone." Each label is read aloud as it appears.
**Narrator (C3):** The series has 38 courses in five groups. Foundations, eight courses, offers shared language for culture, disability, power, bias and voice. Public-service practice, ten courses, applies it to communications, language access, meetings, policy writing, service design and partnership. Complex systems, nine courses, takes up history, race and disability, immigration, LGBTQIA2S+ inclusion, religion, trauma, conflict and ethics. For Tribal sovereignty, a referral page connects you with the responsible DHS office or Tribal liaison, because One DSD refers that work rather than teaching it. Internal leadership, ten courses, covers how teams, governance, data and resources are run. The capstone applies earlier courses to a real process, with a small team and a sponsor.

## Scene 4: Choosing a starting point (1:47–2:32)
**Visual:** A course page showing a scenario, a sorting exercise, a knowledge check with feedback, and a review record that can be copied into a learner's own work.
**Narrator (C3):** You can begin with the first course, or with Choosing a Learning Focus, which helps you pick a starting point from the work already in front of you without rating yourself or anyone else. Each course has four short lessons, about 40 to 45 minutes in all, that you can take in any order. You will find Minnesota examples, practice with feedback, a knowledge check in every lesson, private reflection prompts and a practical tool for your own work. Keep reflections in your own notes. Your choices and progress are saved in your browser, and there is no score or completion requirement.

## Closing (2:32–2:50)
**Visual:** The first course card, with its title read aloud: "Intercultural Practice in Public Disability Services."
**Narrator (C3):** The series is voluntary and supports learning; formal employment, civil-rights and eligibility decisions stay with the responsible office. Start with one ordinary exchange from your own work, and see what changes when you look at it again.
