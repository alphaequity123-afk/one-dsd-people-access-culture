## Scene 1: What this pathway is for (0:00–0:28)
**Visual:** Archival-style illustration of a disability rights march, then a present-day planning conversation at a kitchen table. No real people or logos.
**Narrator (C3):** Disability services in Minnesota rest on hard-won rights: to live in the community, to make informed choices, to work, and to take reasonable risks like anyone else. The Disability Rights and Person-Centered Practice pathway connects that history and those values to the decisions DSD staff and leaders make every day, so that services are shaped with people rather than around them.

## Scene 2: A moment at work (0:28–0:51)
**Visual:** A case manager and a supervisor review a support plan on a shared screen.
**Case manager (C5):** The person wants to try a job in the community. The team keeps saying it's safer to wait another year.
**Supervisor (C6):** Safer for whom? Has anyone laid out the options in a way the person can actually weigh?
**Case manager (C5):** Not yet. We've been talking about the person more than with them.

## Scene 3: How the pathway is organized (0:51–1:29)
**Visual:** On-screen text, read aloud: "31 courses. 13 Foundations, 17 Intermediate, 1 Advanced."
**Narrator (C3):** The pathway has 31 courses: 13 at the Foundations level, 17 at Intermediate, and 1 at Advanced. They fall into three groups. Disability rights, integration and service values includes disability history, the Olmstead decision and Minnesota's Olmstead Plan, and Employment First. Disability, identity and respectful language includes person-first and identity-first language, non-apparent disabilities, and Deaf, DeafBlind and hard of hearing communities. Person-centered practice, choice and integration covers informed choice, positive supports, dignity of risk, supported decision-making and the rights of people receiving services.

## Scene 4: Choosing a starting point (1:29–1:59)
**Visual:** Course cards for "Disability history in Minnesota and the U.S." and "Dignity of risk."
**Narrator (C3):** Staff in any role can begin with "Disability history in Minnesota and the U.S." or "Person-centered practices in Minnesota." If the moment you just saw sounds familiar, try "Informed choice in plain language" or "Dignity of risk." Leaders have parallel courses, such as "The Olmstead decision and Minnesota's Olmstead Plan for leaders," and the Advanced course looks at dignity of risk in policy and program decisions.

## Closing: How the courses work (1:59–2:22)
**Visual:** A lesson page showing a practice case, key terms cards and a job aid.
**Narrator (C3):** Each course has six lessons with DSD practice cases, scenario choices with feedback, knowledge checks, private reflection and a job aid. Learning is voluntary and not scored, and your progress stays in your own browser. The courses support your judgment; eligibility and other formal decisions stay with the responsible office.
