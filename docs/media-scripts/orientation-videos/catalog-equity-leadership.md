## Scene 1: What this pathway is for (0:00–0:27)
**Visual:** A management team meeting around a table, with a calendar on the wall showing a deadline circled.
**Narrator (C3):** Leaders shape what a team treats as normal: which questions get asked, how decisions are made under pressure, and whether commitments are followed through. The Equity Leadership pathway is for division and section leaders, managers, supervisors and team leads who want to build equity into the ordinary work of leading, rather than treating it as a separate project.

## Scene 2: A moment at work (0:27–0:52)
**Visual:** A program manager and a supervisor talk at a whiteboard listing next week's deadlines.
**Program manager (C2):** We have until Friday to send the guidance. There isn't time to do an equity analysis properly.
**Supervisor (C6):** The deadline doesn't take the analysis off the table. What we can decide now is where to start: who is most affected, and who we haven't heard from.
**Program manager (C2):** And we say plainly what we still need to check.

## Scene 3: How the pathway is organized (0:52–1:32)
**Visual:** On-screen text, read aloud: "15 courses. 3 Foundations, 4 Intermediate, 8 Advanced."
**Narrator (C3):** The pathway has 15 courses: 3 at the Foundations level, 4 at Intermediate, and 8 at Advanced. Foundations courses cover what the DHS Equity Policy asks of leaders, Executive Order 23-12 in plain language, and modeling inclusive leadership behavior. Intermediate courses turn to building equity into team goals, deciding under time pressure, using your influence at the management table, and coaching staff. Advanced courses address systems change, accountability without blame, leading when guidance shifts, organizational change, building a learning culture, and sustaining the work when resources are tight.

## Scene 4: Choosing a starting point (1:32–2:01)
**Visual:** Course cards for "Leading equity at DSD: what the DHS Equity Policy asks of leaders" and "Equity-informed decision-making under time pressure."
**Narrator (C3):** If you are new to a leadership role, start with "Leading equity at DSD: what the DHS Equity Policy asks of leaders." If the moment you just saw sounds familiar, "Equity-informed decision-making under time pressure" is written for it. The Advanced course "Equity leadership self-assessment and growth plan" is for private reflection and your own planning, with no personal score or formal assessment.

## Closing: How the courses work (2:01–2:28)
**Visual:** A lesson page with a leadership practice case and a short practice planning panel.
**Narrator (C3):** Each course has six lessons with DSD practice cases, scenario choices with feedback, knowledge checks, private reflection and a job aid. Learning is voluntary and not scored, and your progress stays in your own browser. Completing a course is a step in your learning, not proof of change; what counts is what you try next with your team.
