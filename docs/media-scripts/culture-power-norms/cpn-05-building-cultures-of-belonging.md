## Scene 1: After the listening session (0:00–0:30)
**Visual:** A small meeting room in a state office building late in the afternoon. Chairs are pushed back from a table, and a stack of unused handouts sits by the door. Three colleagues are gathering their things. No logos or real people.
**{{NARRATOR}}:** A DSD team has just finished a community listening session on respite services. Fewer families came than expected, and several left early. A county partner who helped plan the session has stayed behind.

## Scene 2: The partner raises a concern (0:30–1:25)
**Visual:** {{COMMUNITY_PARTNER}} sits at the table; {{SUPERVISOR}} stands holding a laptop.
**{{COMMUNITY_PARTNER}}:** Can I share something before everyone leaves? A few parents told me on the way out that they felt talked at. The handouts came the night before, and two of them had to leave for work before anyone asked a question.
**{{SUPERVISOR}}:** I hear you. This room was the only one we could book, though, and the handouts were held up in review. We really did try to make it work.
**{{COMMUNITY_PARTNER}}:** I know you did. I'm not saying anyone meant to leave people out.
**{{SUPERVISOR}}:** And we did get some good input from the people who stayed.
**{{COMMUNITY_PARTNER}}:** You did, and I want to be fair about that. But the parents who left are the ones I hear from most, and they're the least likely to come back and tell you why.

## Scene 3: What went unsaid (1:25–2:15)
**Visual:** {{NEW_COLLEAGUE}} by the window, holding a notebook. A brief close-up shows a handwritten note on the page; the narrator reads it aloud.
**{{NARRATOR}}:** The note in the notebook reads: "Ask about phone option?"
**{{NEW_COLLEAGUE}}:** I almost said something during planning. A couple of the self-advocates I work with said they'd rather call in than travel. It felt like the plan was already set, so I let it go.
**{{SUPERVISOR}}:** You could have brought that up.
**{{NEW_COLLEAGUE}}:** I know. It just didn't feel like the right moment.
**{{COMMUNITY_PARTNER}}:** That's kind of what the parents said, too. It didn't feel like the right moment to tell you what they needed.
**Visual:** A pause. {{SUPERVISOR}} sets the laptop down.
**{{SUPERVISOR}}:** I keep explaining, and I'm not sure that's what either of you needs from me right now.

## Scene 4: The narrator names the course (2:15–3:20)
**Visual:** The room fades to a simple title card with four short phrases that appear one at a time as the narrator speaks them.
**{{NARRATOR}}:** No one in that room meant to shut anyone out. Still, a concern was nearly brushed aside with explanations, a useful idea went unspoken, and families decided the session was not really for them. This course is about the everyday conditions that let people say what they see, inside a team and with the communities it serves.
**{{NARRATOR}}:** The title card reads: "Fear and speaking up. Comfort and courageous conversations. Defensiveness and listening. Either/or thinking and nuance."
**{{NARRATOR}}:** You'll look at research on psychological safety, including Amy Edmondson's work on teams, and at four patterns from Tema Okun's framework, used here as prompts for reflection rather than labels for people. You'll practice responding to feedback without defending first, holding a hard conversation with care, and keeping two real needs in view when a team splits into camps. You'll also look at what a team's response to bad news teaches people, and why telling someone what happened with their input matters as much as asking for it.

## Closing (3:20–3:35)
**Visual:** The meeting room again. The three colleagues sit back down at the table.
**{{NARRATOR}}:** What should the supervisor say next, and what would have made it easier for the newer colleague to speak up during planning? Keep those questions in mind as you begin.
