# Video script: Moving From Awareness to Action

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /courses/cpn-06-awareness-to-action
- **Length:** about 3.5 minutes (about 470 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Program manager (C2, Host voice), Supervisor (C6, Co-host voice), Case manager (C5, Host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: A whiteboard full of ideas (0:00–0:30)
**Visual:** A team room in a state office. A whiteboard is covered with sticky notes in several colors, grouped loosely. Three colleagues stand in front of it with coffee cups. No logos or real people.
**Narrator (C3):** This DSD team has finished a learning pathway on culture, power and organizational norms. They have a whiteboard full of ideas, broad agreement, and a planning meeting with leadership on Friday.

## Scene 2: Too many good ideas (0:30–1:20)
**Visual:** The program manager (C2) points to clusters of notes while reading them aloud.
**Program manager (C2):** So we have "slow down," "rewrite our letters," "more community voice," "share decisions," and about ten more. I agree with all of them.
**Supervisor (C6):** So do I. But leadership is going to ask what we're doing next quarter, and I can't say "everything."
**Program manager (C2):** Someone already suggested we report how many of us finished the training. It's an easy number.
**Case manager (C5):** It is easy. It just doesn't tell anyone whether a single family has an easier time with us.
**Supervisor (C6):** Leadership will also want to know whether any of this is working. If we can't show that, the next budget conversation gets harder.
**Program manager (C2):** Then we need something better to show than attendance.

## Scene 3: What counts as progress (1:20–2:15)
**Visual:** The case manager (C5) pulls one sticky note off the board and holds it up. The narrator reads the note.
**Narrator (C3):** The note reads: "Denial letter for plan changes."
**Case manager (C5):** I get calls every week from people who don't know what this letter means or what they can do next. That's something we could actually change.
**Supervisor (C6):** Can we change it, though? Some of that language is required.
**Case manager (C5):** Some of it. Not all of it. I don't know which parts. If a new version worked, I'd expect fewer of those calls, and maybe more people asking for a review before the deadline. But I'd want to hear from a few of them, not just count calls.
**Program manager (C2):** And if we rewrite it ourselves and then show it to the advisory group, they'll tell us, again, that we asked them too late.
**Supervisor (C6):** So who decides what goes in the letter? And how would we know if a new version worked?
**Visual:** The three look at the board. No one answers yet.

## Scene 4: The narrator names the course (2:15–3:20)
**Visual:** The scene fades to a title card. Four short steps appear one at a time as the narrator speaks them.
**Narrator (C3):** Awareness got this team to the whiteboard. It won't, by itself, change the letter. This course is about the next step: moving from what a team has noticed to a change it can make, test and keep.
**Narrator (C3):** The title card reads: "Choose one process. Plan, try, study, adjust. Share decisions. Sustain the change."
**Narrator (C3):** You'll map who has authority over a process and what binds it, and you'll use the DHS Equity Analysis Toolkit the way the DHS Equity Policy asks. You'll look at power hoarding and paternalism, two patterns from Tema Okun's framework that often grow from good intentions, and at what honest participation looks like. You'll measure change in the work, such as clearer notices, barriers removed, and community input that led to a documented change, and never by tracking people. And you'll look at what research says training alone can and cannot do. None of this asks the team to prove that it is good. It asks the team to learn whether the work got better for the people it serves.

## Closing (3:20–3:35)
**Visual:** Back in the team room. The case manager (C5) places the single note in the center of an empty part of the whiteboard.
**Narrator (C3):** Which process would your team choose first, and who should help decide what changes? Bring those questions with you as you begin.

## Transcript notes
Summary for the media manifest: A DSD team with a whiteboard of ideas must decide what to do next and how to show progress. The video introduces choosing one process, plan-try-study-adjust, shared decisions and measuring change in the work.
