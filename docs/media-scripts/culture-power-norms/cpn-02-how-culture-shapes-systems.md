# Video script: How Culture Shapes Systems

- **Status:** Script only, written for the recurring cast (docs/media-scripts/CAST.md). Not recorded.
- **Publish on:** /courses/cpn-02-how-culture-shapes-systems
- **Length:** about 3.5 minutes (about 465 spoken words)
- **Cast:** Narrator (C3, Host voice), Program manager (C2, Host voice), Case manager (C5, Host voice), Community partner (C1, Co-host voice) (see docs/media-scripts/CAST.md)
- **Accessibility:** captions and a full transcript are required before publishing (docs/ACCESSIBILITY-STANDARD.md). On-screen text must also be spoken. Describe any visual that carries meaning.

## Scene 1: The third extension (0:00–0:35)
**Visual:** A small conference room in a public agency. The program manager (C2) and the case manager (C5) sit at a table; the community partner (C1) joins on a screen from a county office. A simple line chart on the wall screen shows late responses dipping slightly twice, then rising back to the same level. No logos and no real people.
**Narrator (C3):** A DSD team is reviewing late responses to a renewal notice. The chart shows two small dips, one after each deadline extension, and then the line climbs back to where it started.
**Program manager (C2):** We've extended the deadline twice. I'm ready to propose a third. I don't know what else we can do in the time we have.
**Case manager (C5):** Every extension buys us about a month. Then we're back on the phone with the same families.

## Scene 2: What the county sees (0:35–1:20)
**Visual:** Close on the community partner (C1) on the screen, speaking calmly.
**Community partner (C1):** Can I tell you what it looks like from our side? A lot of people don't open that notice until their support person visits, sometimes two weeks after it arrives. By then half the time is gone. An extra few days doesn't change that.
**Program manager (C2):** So the deadline isn't really the problem.
**Community partner (C1):** The deadline is where it shows up. The problem starts earlier.
**Case manager (C5):** And the notice assumes the person reads English easily, checks mail every day, and can call us during business hours. That describes some of the people I work with. Not most.
**Program manager (C2):** We didn't design it for anyone in particular. It's just how the notice has always gone out.

## Scene 3: Who decides (1:20–2:15)
**Visual:** The program manager (C2) pulls up a procedure document on the wall screen; one paragraph is highlighted. The text is not readable; the highlight shows the team searching for where the timeline comes from.
**Case manager (C5):** Is the timeline in law, or did somebody pick it?
**Program manager (C2):** I honestly don't know. I inherited it.
**Community partner (C1):** And if we changed how the notice reaches people, who would have to say yes?
**Case manager (C5):** It isn't only the timeline, either. Interpretation for renewals isn't in the budget, so we request it case by case, and it's almost always late.
**Community partner (C1):** And some people would want their support person to get a copy of the notice. Right now there's no way for them to ask for that.
**Program manager (C2):** So every piece made sense to someone once. Put together, they keep producing the same chart.
**Case manager (C5):** Which means a third extension probably gets us the same chart again.
**Program manager (C2):** Maybe. Or maybe it's still the right short-term step. That's a better question than the one I came in with.

## Scene 4: What the course covers (2:15–3:05)
**Visual:** The room fades to an illustration of a single notice moving along a path. Along the path, four signposts appear and are read aloud by the narrator: "Rule," "Form," "Timeline," "Resources."
**Narrator (C3):** Habits don't stay habits. Over time they become rules, forms, timelines and budgets that keep working long after anyone remembers why. In this course you'll practice explaining a recurring problem as events, as a pattern and as a structure, an approach drawn from Peter Senge's work on learning organizations. You'll trace defaults back to where they came from and who owns them. You'll use john a. powell's idea that people are situated differently within the same structures, along with targeted universalism, which keeps one goal for everyone and designs different paths to reach it. And you'll look at where in a system a change makes the most difference, and how to tell whether it helped.

## Closing (3:05–3:20)
**Visual:** Back in the room. The chart is still on the screen. No one has written the proposal yet.
**Narrator (C3):** This team hasn't decided whether to extend the deadline again, and the course won't decide for you. As you work through the lessons, keep one question in mind: what in the design keeps producing the result?

## Transcript notes
Summary for the media manifest: After two deadline extensions fail to fix late renewal responses, a DSD team and a county partner start asking whether the problem lies with people or with the design of the notice, its timeline and who decides.
