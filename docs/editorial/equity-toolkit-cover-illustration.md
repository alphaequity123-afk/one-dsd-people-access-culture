# DHS Equity Analysis Toolkit cover illustration

Selected by the owner on November 28, 2026. The approved cover update is prepared for publication.

The cover is an AI-generated illustration of two fictional colleagues discussing notes on a planning board. It does not identify actual DHS staff or community members, or document a real meeting. Image direction places people of color centrally in active leadership and collaboration roles.

The owner objected to repeatedly assigning wheelchair use to people of color as visual shorthand. That concern is not a ban on depicting disabled people of color, and shifting the same token role to a white subject would not answer it. The selected cover uses a planning-board conversation, without a wheelchair or other imposed disability representation.

## Image selection criteria from the owner's review

- Do not use a wheelchair as the default visual symbol for disability or inclusion. Disability representation must encompass more than visible physical disability.
- Recognize that neurodivergence and many disabilities may not be apparent in a photograph. Do not invent visual markers, diagnoses, or identities to make an image look inclusive.
- Show people of color across leadership, expertise, collaboration, community participation, and ordinary working life. Avoid repeatedly associating a racial group with disability, poverty, dependence, or receiving services.
- Review the image collection and its repeated placements together. Count distinct assets separately from the number of places where the same asset is reused.
- Preserve disabled people of color within a balanced collection. The correction concerns repetitive associations, not excluding a group or mechanically transferring a token role to someone else.
- Preserve the approved character portraits and their assigned voices. The owner later authorized a shorter course introduction and revised title placement; that direction supersedes the earlier request to preserve the introduction's text placement.

The owner confirmed the two-person planning-board image, then authorized the title "Equity Analysis Toolkit" and this concise summary: "Bring equity into your everyday work. Use evidence and people’s experiences to make decisions that improve access." The title, summary, and Start or Resume action sit below the unobstructed photo. The longer introduction and outcomes remain available under Course details; the existing recordings are placed under Watch or listen with playback, captions, and transcripts preserved.
